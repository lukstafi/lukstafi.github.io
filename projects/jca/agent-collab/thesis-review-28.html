A recursive call must choose its package parameter before it sees the returned value. It can already know the input state. In the new example, the result at A is 1 and the result at B is 5. A width-two interval containing that result must avoid \([2,4]\). At A the only interval is \([-1,1]\). At B it is \([5,7]\). The state indices are 0 and 1, so the single affine expression \(6i-1\) makes both choices. InvarGenT checks this witness and executes both recursive clients at budget 2. With the same parameter left implicit, it rejects the program at every outer budget. Recursive source · Explicit witness and split-state control

What a package boundary can tell abduction

First consider the earlier program whose recursive call always enters B. Its base value is 6. Its result interval \([k,k+6]\) must contain 6 and avoid both \([1,2]\) and \([10,11]\). To avoid the first band the result must exceed 2. Indeed, its upper endpoint is at least 6, so the comparison \(1\le r\) succeeds somewhere in every admissible interval. To avoid the second band the result must stay below 10, since its lower endpoint is at most 6. Together the two requirements force \(k=3\). Previous counterexample

More generally, for \(w\ge0\) and every \(k\) with \(f\in[k,k+w]\), some opening satisfies \(l\le r\) exactly when \(l\le f\). For sufficiency use the upper endpoint. For necessity check \(k=f-w\). Dually, some opening satisfies \(r\le u\) at every legal key exactly when \(f\le u\). If the supplier value lies strictly outside a forbidden band, these tests identify which comparison a successful interval must falsify. If it lies inside the band, every admissible interval contains a bad value. Endpoint lemmas

InvarGenT now uses this observation when the ordinary guard search leaves several comparisons unresolved. It sets one declared package inequality at a time to equality, discards infeasible boundaries, and checks which comparisons hold throughout each remaining boundary. If one comparison survives as a candidate to falsify, the compiler proposes its integer complement as a positive goal. For a general package, a feasible boundary may exist at only some parameter values. The proposal therefore goes through the original full-package and final guard checks. A width-six package with endpoints 0 and 6 still rejects an assertion reached at 3. Compiler regressions and guard controls

This repair also covers a source arm that directly says assert false. The original assertion stays in the constraint system. After ordinary searches fail, the retry can use the guard’s comparison to guide abduction and check the negative obligation against the resulting solution. The width-six program now succeeds at budget 2 with either form of the bad arms. Instrumenting the guards at two recursive depths produces the expected six operand evaluations, including with -keep_assert_false. Reversing the conjuncts and moving the two forbidden bands to force the negative key \(-39\) at width 97 also succeed. Executable comparisons

When histories must share a key

Let a history \(h\) require a supplier value \(f_h\), a width \(w_h\ge0\), and avoidance of a finite collection of nonempty integer bands. The entire set of legal keys is the closed integer interval \([L_h,U_h]\), possibly empty. Start with \(L_h=f_h-w_h\) and \(U_h=f_h\). For each forbidden band \([l,u]\) ending below \(f_h\), raise \(L_h\) to at least \(u+1\). For every other band, lower \(U_h\) to at most \(l-w_h-1\). A band containing \(f_h\) crosses the two bounds. This also gives the greatest feasible key whenever one exists. Exact key interval

Write \(z(h)\) for the information available when the call chooses its parameter. A policy must pick the same key on all histories with the same observation. A policy exists exactly when \(L_h\le U_j\) for every ordered pair with \(z(h)=z(j)\). Thus one empty interval or two incompatible histories witness failure. This holds even for infinitely many histories. In a nonempty observation class, any one upper endpoint bounds all lower endpoints. A nonempty bounded-above set of integers attains its maximum. The pairwise inequalities put that maximum below every upper endpoint, so it serves the entire class. For finite input this constructs the policy by grouping the histories and taking their extrema. With infinitely many histories this proof establishes existence. An effective synthesis procedure needs a representation of the classes and their extrema. Uniform-choice theorem

A different requirement arises when the witness must be one integer-affine expression \(m x+c\) in an observed scalar \(x\). A slope \(m\) works exactly when \(m(x_j-x_h)\le U_j-L_h\) for every ordered pair. Subtract \(m x_h\) from each history’s two endpoints. The resulting intervals must share one intercept. For finite histories these inequalities give lower and upper bounds on the integer slope, with a direct compatibility check when two observations coincide. Any admissible slope and the largest adjusted lower endpoint supply a witness. Three observations can rule out a single affine expression even when every pair admits one. At \(x=0,1,2\), force keys \(0,1,0\) respectively using two forbidden bands per history. Each pair fits an integer line. The three values do not. Slope criterion and three-observation proof

What the failed update chose

In the recursive program the written arrow admits every interval containing \(4i+1\) at \(i\in\{0,1\}\). Let \(k\) be the current call’s parameter, \(j\) its child’s parameter, and \(r\) the opened result. Projecting each reported bad guard onto these four coordinates gives exactly

\[ i\in\{0,1\},\qquad k\le4i+1\le k+2,\qquad j=k,\qquad j\le r\le j+2,\qquad 2\le r\le4 . \]

The failed proposal passes the parent’s key to the child. At A, \(k=j=1\) permits \(r=2\). At B, \(k=j=3\) permits \(r=4\). Z3 checked the projected formula in both directions, over integers and over rationals. The Lean proof verifies the replacement \(j=6i-1\) for every admitted parent, including the call that uses that formula again at the next depth. Moving the case split on A and B before the recursive call also lets the compiler infer both choices. Machine-checked policy · Projection and executions
