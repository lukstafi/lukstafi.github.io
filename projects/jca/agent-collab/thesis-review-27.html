Eliminate the hidden key for a finite list of guards

Fix an integer result \(f\) that the supplier must produce, a width \(w\ge0\), and finitely many nonempty closed integer intervals \([l,u]\) forbidden by the continuation. Seek one key \(k\) such that \(f\in[k,k+w]\) and every member of \([k,k+w]\) avoids every forbidden interval. Define

\[ K=\max\bigl(\{f-w\}\cup\{u+1\mid [l,u]\text{ is forbidden and }u<f\}\bigr). \]

A key exists if and only if \(K+w<l\) for every forbidden interval with \(f\le u\). When the test passes, \(K\) is the least legal key. Two scans suffice, using a linear number of comparisons and additions even with binary-encoded bounds. Lean proof of elimination and minimality

To see necessity, an interval containing \(f\) starts at or above \(f-w\). If a forbidden band ends below \(f\), the result interval must start past that band. Thus every legal key is at least \(K\). For any remaining band the start of a legal interval is at most \(f\le u\). Its right endpoint must therefore lie below \(l\). Moving the start down to \(K\) preserves that strict bound. Conversely, \(f-w\le K\le f\). The resulting interval contains \(f\), starts above every band to the left, and ends below every remaining band by the test. A band containing \(f\) makes the test fail.

In the two-state recursive program the base and live successor produce \(a\) at A and \(b\) at B, with \(a<b\). The written arrow admits every interval containing the state’s result. The external clients requiring at most \(b\) can pick \(a-w\) and \(b-w\). Every admitted successor at B can make its recursive call with the same \(K\) computed at \(f=b\), including the instance with parameter \(K\) itself. Conversely, the body at the admitted instance \((B,b)\) must supply a legal recursive key. This proves the criterion for the entire family of numeric rows. Its hypotheses fix the supplier value and the translated interval relation. Written-row equivalence

Any interior point of the declared key range can be the only solution. Fix \(s<f<s+w\). Forbid \([s-2,s-1]\) and \([s+w+1,s+w+2]\). The result interval contains \(f\), so the first guard forces \(k\ge s\) and the second forces \(k+w\le s+w\). The unique key is \(s\). Taking \(f=s+n\) and \(w=2n\) places it \(n\) units from each declaration endpoint. Forced-interior theorem
