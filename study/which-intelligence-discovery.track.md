Questions whose answers draw on both essays of the stage. Attempt them after *The Operations of Discovery*.

### One list, two questions {kind=connect}

Both essays list the same four operations. What does each essay ask about them, and what does each answer?

::: answer
*Poetry as a Mode of Thinking* asks which operations are representational, and so available to an LLM. Its answer: forced adjacency and compression are available, thinking in negative space is in question, and register collision can be produced while the disruption that makes it productive is uncertain ([the LLM question](/notes/poetry-as-thinking.html#the-llm-question-honestly)). *The Operations of Discovery* asks whether performing the operations is enough for discovery. Its answer: they are necessary but not sufficient, and what makes them productive is evidential weight ([the evidential weight](/notes/operations-of-discovery.html#the-evidential-weight)). The second essay finds its question inside poetry too. A virtuoso poem deploys the operations with no lived problem behind it and yields admiration, not knowledge.
:::

### What is absent, twice {kind=connect}

Each essay grants an LLM the operations and names something it lacks. Name the lack in each essay, and compare how each is argued.

::: answer
The first essay names what grounds thinking in reality: homeostatic feedback loops, perceptual contact with the world, embodied stakes. With these goes the poem as vehicle, its sound, rhythm and breath ([attunement and the age of AI](/notes/poetry-as-thinking.html#attunement-and-the-age-of-ai)). The argument comes from the series' framework, on which LLMs have understanding and lack acquaintance. The second essay names evidential weight: "a life in which the problems are real, the stakes are felt, and the history of engagement with the territory is genuinely one's own" ([what the transition feels like](/notes/operations-of-discovery.html#what-the-transition-feels-like)). The argument comes from the reports of Tao, Karpathy and the Rate Limited hosts. Both essays place the lack outside the operations, in stakes. The second adds persistence: each AI session starts fresh, and depth needs temporal coherence.
:::

### Negative space {kind=connect}

Compare the two essays' verdicts on whether an LLM can think in negative space.

::: answer
The first essay calls the operation harder than forced adjacency. Training optimizes for producing content, and the operation may need a restraint that is architectural: "knowing where to stop is different from knowing what to say." The second essay gives the operation a scientific instance, Tao's "not adding more theories, but deleting some assumptions." Its closing section then concedes that AI can "even think in something like negative space". The second verdict is the more permissive one, hedged by "something like". The essay does not say what moved it.
:::

### Fake thinking and slop {kind=connect}

Carlsmith's "fake thinking" and Tao's "slop" name related failures. State each. How does each essay apply the worry to its own writing, and what does each offer against it?

::: answer
Fake thinking has the form of cognition without the function of tracking reality. Slop, in the deep variety the second essay means, is output that is internally coherent, formally sophisticated, and shaped by optimization pressures that are not truth-tracking. Both essays name the same risk in writing with an LLM: it builds a plausible possible world around whatever the user seems to want. The first essay answers with its practice: ideas tested against each other across articles, the human's corrections of the LLM within the essay, and transparency about the process. The second answers with a criterion. The judgment that a result is real rests on having a problem that is one's own and on willingness to hold results lightly.
:::
