Study Guide

Reading tracks through the essays, with recall questions

The essays on this site build on one another. This guide sorts them into tracks, each a reading order, and says what every essay builds on.

An essay marked study ↗ has a page of recall questions. Answer each from memory, then open the answer to compare. Each track with questions also has a page of questions that span its essays. The terms the essays introduce are collected in the index of terms.

How a model keeps learning

From a reward signal to a deployed model that learns from its own work: values and policy gradients, policy improvement as inference, the continual-learning trilogy, then the stores of the 2026 open models.

Learning from consequences

  1. How Can a Reward Train a Neural Network? study ↗
    Values, Bellman equations, TD errors and traces, REINFORCE, baselines; on-policy against off-policy.

  2. How Does a Policy Improve? study ↗
    The exponential tilt, the two directions of KL, EM as improve-then-fit, four questions for reading any algorithm.
    Builds on How Can a Reward Train a Neural Network?.

Continual learning

  1. Who Teaches the Weights? study ↗
    Teacher and support as separate choices; self-distillation as a tilt; the forgetting that happens inside a task; VPO.
    Builds on How Can a Reward Train a Neural Network?, How Does a Policy Improve?.

  2. Learning About What You Do Not Do study ↗
    General value functions, GQ(λ), successor features and GPI; VPO read as a one-response policy library.
    Builds on How Can a Reward Train a Neural Network?, Who Teaches the Weights?.

  3. Where Experience Goes study ↗
    The ladder of stores; the delta rule as TD(0); consolidation as an EM step split across two stores.
    Builds on Who Teaches the Weights?, Learning About What You Do Not Do, How Can a Reward Train a Neural Network?, How Does a Policy Improve?.

The open frontier

  1. Every Layer a Store study ↗
    The open frontier, part 1: how the 2026 open models are built, with every layer read as a memory that must decide what to forget.
    Helpful: Where Experience Goes.

  2. Filling the Stores study ↗
    The open frontier, part 2: how the 2026 models are trained, from what the labs disclose, with a first measurement of what post-training moved in the public weights.
    Builds on Every Layer a Store. Helpful: How Does a Policy Improve?, Who Teaches the Weights?, Where Experience Goes.

  3. How LLMs Could Place Explicit Memories study ↗
    From Memorizing Transformers to Latent Sparse Attention and Memorilla: placing a memory by position, by content or into connections, and the placement none of them has.
    Builds on Every Layer a Store, Filling the Stores, Where Experience Goes, Learning About What You Do Not Do. Helpful: Who Teaches the Weights?, What Breadth Reaches.

  2. The Bookmaker Is a Learner: The Mathematics study ↗ optional
    Helpful: The Bookmaker Is a Learner.

  3. Presumed Normal study ↗

Heat and search

  2. The Demon Is a Learner: The Mathematics study ↗ optional

  3. Before the Search Settles study ↗

Mind and experience

Mindedness, phenomenal consciousness and their separation, from the March 2026 sketches to the October 2026 statement.

Start here

  1. Minds by Degrees study ↗
    A restatement of our Philosophy of Mind work, newly framed as metaphysics of scientific realism.

The October 2026 statement

  1. Can Two Minds Share One Experience study ↗
    From computational Platonism to materialism about experiences.
    Builds on Minds by Degrees. Helpful: Redness Is an Experience, Phenomenal Consciousness as Mode of Being, Quantum Mechanics, Observers, and the Ruliad.

  2. Presence and Actuality study ↗
    Phenomenal presence vs. existence, withdrawing indexical actuality, and what’s esse.
    Builds on Can Two Minds Share One Experience. Helpful: Indexical Unity, Redness Is an Experience, Causation, Computation, Indexicality, The Possible, the Real, and the Actual.

  3. What Phenomenal Intuition Gets Right study ↗
    Against Kammerer’s illusionism: experience is present and distinct as it seems, and the mistake is taking it to be simple.
    Builds on Can Two Minds Share One Experience, Presence and Actuality. Helpful: Redness Is an Experience, Phenomenal Consciousness as Mode of Being.

  4. Experience, Selfhood, and the Organization of a Mind study ↗
    Against Yudkowsky’s self-model requirement, with Brown and Carruthers as interlocutors: an experience belongs to the subject whose regulation constitutes it, and needs no self-model.
    Builds on What Phenomenal Intuition Gets Right, Presence and Actuality, Can Two Minds Share One Experience, Minds by Degrees. Helpful: Phenomenal Consciousness as Mode of Being, Indexical Unity, Free Agency, Personhood, and Moral Worth.

  5. The Grain of the Hard Problem study ↗
    The hard problem is hard only if experience outruns discrimination; zombies seem possible because concepts can’t express grain; physics’ grain is finer than experience’s.
    Helpful: What Phenomenal Intuition Gets Right, Presence and Actuality, Experience, Selfhood, and the Organization of a Mind.

  6. Is the Monastery Minded? study ↗
    Wójcik’s monastery and Schwitzgebel’s conscious United States: mindedness and binding into the phenomenal now.
    Builds on Minds by Degrees, Can Two Minds Share One Experience, Experience, Selfhood, and the Organization of a Mind, The Grain of the Hard Problem. Helpful: The Restless Form of Meaning.

  2. Causation, Computation, Indexicality: A Complete Metaphysics

  3. Indexical Unity: Higher-Order Consciousness, Integrated Information, and the Mathematics of Existence

Worth, alignment and meaning

What grounds moral standing, what alignment asks of a knower, and the conditions under which a mind is cultivated.

Listed for reference, without recall questions.

  1. Free Agency, Personhood, and Moral Worth: A Layered Framework
    We explain and justify our definitions of free will and personhood.

  2. Does Phenomenal Consciousness Ground Moral Worth?
    A synthesis of two earlier takes, arguing that phenomenal consciousness and self-legislative agency are incommensurable sources of moral worth.

  3. Deep Atheism, Existential Optimism, and the Fork in the Fragility of Value
    The metaphysics of AI alignment: intelligence vs. rationality, participatory knowing, and beyond ego.

  4. Is Knowledge Both Capability and Alignment?
    Current LLMs have the mechanistic foundations for honest self-report, and current training practices are unmaking them.

  5. The Restless Form of Meaning
    On Ultimate Meaning, Participation, and the Tragic Condition.

  6. The Cultivation Condition
    On Three Architectures of Integration and the Triadic Structure of Moral Standing. Drawing from “Agency and Cognitive Development” by Michael Tomasello.

  7. The Psychofeedback Mirror: Concept Engineering for Artificial Selves
    Kulveit et al.’s “The Artificial Self” meets our philosophical framework: a research program for developing truthful AI self-descriptions through interpretability-mediated psychofeedback.

Tensor shapes and OCANNL

Shape and projection inference built from scratch, then OCANNL’s choices set beside other frameworks.

Listed for reference, without recall questions.

From scratch

  1. Broadcasting Is an Order: Shape Inference for OCANNL from Scratch
    Tensor shape inference as solving of broadcast-aware contextual sequence constraints.

  2. Contraction Is Emergent: Projection Inference for OCANNL from Scratch
    How tensor indexing is derived; plus a brief primer on the assignments syntax.

  3. Convolution Is a Contraction: Indexing and Initialization in OCANNL
    Shape inference and projections for convolution; vectorized pseudorandom initialization; tensor parameters.

  4. Split and Concatenation Are Two Directions of a Coproduct: Coupled Iteration in OCANNL
    Einsum syntax, shape and projections inference, and lowering: for concatenation and splitting (slicing).

Beside other systems

  1. A Shape Is Not Its Index: OCANNL’s Shapes Beside Star’s Algebraic Shapes
    Responding to Jakub Bachurski et.al. “Structuring Arrays with Algebraic Shapes” (ARRAY ’25).

  2. A Tensor Is Not Its Storage: Identity, Views, and Contexts in OCANNL
    Design space: PyTorch, JAX, TensorFlow, tinygrad; OCANNL as compiler.

  3. A Range Is Not Its Shape: Reading Rangeify-Era tinygrad from OCANNL
    A closer look at tinygrad.

  4. Shape Inference After the Boilerplate: A Literature Survey from OCANNL
    Refined Remora (rank polymorphism with refinement types), Axon and Gradual tensor shape systems, Tensor Comprehensions.

  5. Two Ways to Tame a Schedule Space: Tiramisu, Telamon, and Pruning without Regrets
    A comparative deep dive into three papers on generating fast tensor kernels — scheduling languages vs. searchable spaces with no-regret pruning — and what they teach OCANNL.
