Questions whose answers draw on more than one essay. Attempt them after the last essay of the stage.

### Which system? {kind=connect}

"Which AGI?" separates a running model from the deployment built around it. Each later essay but one applies the distinction to a subject of its own. Give the four applications.

::: answer
- *What Must Be True?*: the research on joint abduction was done by an arrangement of a human choice of problem, existing research, several model instances, executable checks and review. Those capacities belong to the arrangement, not to an isolated model ([section](/notes/what-must-be-true.html#reasoning-about-the-reasoner)).
- *Which World Model?*: the efficacy of a whole deployment does not locate its intelligence in one component. A fixed-size network asked to answer immediately and a deployment allowed to run an algorithm are different computational systems, so a claim about computational limits must say which is meant.
- *What Persists When the Agents Change?*: the relevant system is a capable interpreter working with records, tools, tests, local memories and other participants. An inquiry can depend on that organization and survive the replacement of any one running process ([section](/notes/what-persists-when-agents-change.html#representations-in-use)).
- *Where Does Seeing Become Thinking?*: the loop runs through action and renewed sensory input. A deployment that can mark, crop, draw and calculate has operations unavailable to a model required to answer immediately, and their contribution should be measured ([section](/notes/where-does-seeing-become-thinking.html#thinking-with-the-environment)).
:::

### Having and using {kind=connect}

*Which World Model?* separates possessing a representation from using it dependably. How do the two essays that cite the distinction extend it?

::: answer
*Which World Model?* separates representing structure, tracking the situation, predicting, using and maintaining. Its Manhattan case shows a real map beside a real failure of navigation. *What Persists* applies the same discipline to a research trajectory: possessing a record, finding the relevant part, interpreting it, deciding whether to trust it, and using it are different jobs. It extends the distinction again to traditions: hosting two perspectives in shared machinery does not guarantee judging well between them ([section](/notes/what-persists-when-agents-change.html#representations-in-use)). *Where Does Seeing Become Thinking?* asks what using a representation requires. Recoverable information is an informational possibility, and a learned operation makes it available at a cost and reliability ([section](/notes/where-does-seeing-become-thinking.html#from-representations-to-reliable-operations)). Decoding a variable, intervening on it and explaining the transformation are three levels of evidence.
:::

### Compiled and interpreted {kind=connect}

The compiled/interpreted distinction is introduced in "Which AGI?". How do the next two essays that cite it use it, and what instance does a third supply?

::: answer
"Which AGI?" contrasts a skill followed as written instructions with one consolidated into fluent, composable competence, and predicts linear context cost, brittleness under composition and no tacit knowledge ([section](/notes/which-agi.html#what-a-deployment-has-and-what-it-lacks)). *Which World Model?* uses it to locate the remaining work in continual learning: an interpreted record preserves a lesson and leaves the agent to work out its application each time. *What Persists* treats it as a difference in the cost and manner of using knowledge. The division runs through a system and not between systems, as when a trained reranker selects among explicit records, and different parts of one lesson can persist in different forms ([section](/notes/what-persists-when-agents-change.html#representations-in-use)). *What Should a Program Inherit?* gives an instance: an executable artifact changes the next attempt's starting point without being absorbed into the parameters of the proposing model ([section](/notes/what-should-a-program-inherit.html#alphaevolve-variation-selection-and-what-survives)).
:::

### What the series takes back {kind=connect}

Two later essays return to claims made in "Which AGI?". Which claim does *Which World Model?* defend? Which three does *What Persists* correct or weaken, and what does it keep?

::: answer
*Which World Model?* defends the inventory's entry that puts a structured world model among the roles filled inside the network. It adds what filling the role leaves unresolved: maintaining the model and using it reliably. *What Persists* makes three revisions.

- The sentence that a consensus front "can carry only what is consistent across the population" is corrected: a shared model can support incompatible perspectives.
- The conjectured advantage of insulated training no longer carries the argument for individually compiled trajectories. The conjecture stays an untested empirical question.
- The inference from rereading costs to a fixed ceiling on interpreted memory "was too quick": retrieval, summaries, executable artifacts and a changed environment loosen it.

It keeps the distinction between role coverage and learning profile. The concern about correlated failure survives as an evaluation question ([section](/notes/what-persists-when-agents-change.html#shared-weights-divergent-trajectories)).
:::

### One pattern, three uses {kind=connect}

The pattern $\operatorname{pair}(p,p)$, the most specific generalization of $\operatorname{pair}(a,a)$ and $\operatorname{pair}(b,b)$, appears in two essays. What does the repeated hole do in each of its three uses?

::: answer
In *What Must Be True?* the repeated hole is the point. Generalizing one successful example per case as specifically as possible keeps the agreements a joint answer needs, and this reformulates the existence of an answer ([section](/notes/what-must-be-true.html#can-generalization-tell-us-when-to-stop)). In *What Should a Program Inherit?* the same hole is an obstacle to recombination. It allows $2$ offspring where the linear pattern $\operatorname{pair}(p,q)$ allows $4$, which is why the 2002 draft required linear schemata ([section](/notes/what-should-a-program-inherit.html#finding-the-parts-that-can-vary-together)). Installed as a function, `duplicate(p)`, the dependency becomes a reusable operation, and it hides a choice that later variation may need ([section](/notes/what-should-a-program-inherit.html#babble-when-a-common-pattern-becomes-a-useful-function)). One mathematical object answers three questions differently: what it says about its instances, what it saves, and what variations it makes available.
:::

### The order nature took {kind=connect}

Carrier and Dai each argue from the order in which capacities arose in nature. State each argument and the reply. Which companion point about objectives and traces do the same two essays make?

::: answer
Carrier holds that a pathway from modeling one's surroundings and body up to a narrative self is the only way to real AI. *Which World Model?* replies that the biological order shows one successful route, and that uniqueness needs a further argument: a machine trained on the products of human culture starts with information the first organisms had to acquire otherwise. Dai holds that reasoning is fundamentally visual and that visual reasoning should be the foundation for linguistic reasoning. *Where Does Seeing Become Thinking?* replies that the evolutionary priority of vision does not establish a necessary engineering order ([section](/notes/where-does-seeing-become-thinking.html#what-visual-reasoning-requires)). The companion point: "next-token predictor" names an objective and an output interface and not the computations learned, and a textual reasoning trace does not show that every computation behind it has a linguistic format. The later essay says these are the distinctions that made the world-model debate tractable.
:::
