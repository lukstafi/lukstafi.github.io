The essay is a survey with commentary. The answers say whose view each claim is, and "the essay" marks the authors' own commentary.

### Four axes, two senses {kind=recall at=what-are-we-talking-about}

The essay sorts theories of concepts along four axes. Name them with the question each asks. Which two senses of "cognition" does it separate first, and why?

::: answer
Content: what a concept represents, and how. Format: what kind of representational vehicle carries it. Origin: whether it is innate, learned or culturally universal. Dynamics: how it changes, combines and is deployed.

In the broad sense cognition is information processing, from retinal edge detection to mathematical reasoning. In the narrow sense it is conceptual processing: categorization, inference, and representations that combine into thoughts with truth conditions. Some disagreements, including Block's with Quilty-Dunn, turn on where perceptual processing ends and conceptual thought begins.
:::

### Five accounts of content {kind=recall at=the-content-landscape-what-concepts-are-about}

Name the five accounts of conceptual content in the order the essay takes them. For each, say what a concept is and the main difficulty the essay raises.

::: answer
- *Classical.* A set of necessary and sufficient conditions. Wittgenstein's GAME resists definition: games share family resemblances, not a common set of conditions.
- *Prototype* (Rosch). A statistical best example, with new cases categorized by similarity to it. This explains graded typicality.
- *Exemplar* (Medin and Schaffer, formalized by Nosofsky). The remembered instances themselves, with new cases compared to the whole set. Both similarity theories fail to compose: the prototype of PET FISH is not built from the prototypes of PET and FISH.
- *Theory-theory* (Gopnik and Wellman, elaborated by Murphy and Medin). A node in an intuitive causal-explanatory theory, which is why deep causal features outweigh surface ones. It leaves open where the first theories come from, and it risks circularity: a theory needs concepts to be formulated in.
- *Atomism* (Fodor). An unstructured atom whose content is a law-like relation between mind and world. An atom has no parts to learn it from (the "doorknob problem"), which drove Fodor toward the view that all lexical concepts are innate and experience only triggers them.
:::

### Machery's challenge {kind=object at=macherys-heterogeneity-challenge}

Machery concludes that cognitive science should do without the category "concept". State his argument and the essay's reply. What does the reply open up?

::: answer
Machery argues that "concept" is not a natural kind. The label covers at least three kinds of structure, prototypes, exemplars and theories, each deployed for a different task: quick categorization, causal reasoning, recall of an instance. The essay accepts this diagnosis of the impasse and rejects the elimination. Concepts can be characterized functionally, as whatever plays the concept role: the structures in long-term memory that are activated by perception or by other concepts, compose into structured thoughts, and guide inference and action. The [introduction](/notes/concept_theories.html#what-are-we-talking-about) sketched this role with ACT-R's memory store and spreading activation. A functional target is stable whether or not the category is a natural kind. It opens what the essay calls the format turn: asking how concepts represent, not only what.
:::

### The case for a language of thought {kind=recall at=the-language-of-thought}

Which three features of thought does Fodor's argument rest on, and what does each suggest? What kind of claim is the Language of Thought hypothesis, and how does it relate to his atomism?

::: answer
*Productivity*: there is no longest thought, which requires a finite stock of primitives and rules for combining them. *Systematicity*: whoever can think "John loves Mary" can think "Mary loves John", so thoughts share constituents that can be rearranged. *Compositionality*: the meaning of a complex thought is determined by the meanings of its parts and how they are combined.

The hypothesis is that mental representations are discrete symbols combined by syntactic rules. It is a claim about vehicles. It does not say that thought is conducted in a natural language. Atomism is a claim about content, and the two are [logically independent](/notes/concept_theories.html#fodors-atomism): the vehicles could have language-like syntax while the primitives have internal structure, and atoms could be stored in a non-linguistic format.
:::

### The iconic alternative {kind=distinguish at=mental-imagery-and-the-iconic-alternative}

What did Shepard and Metzler find, and how did Pylyshyn answer the imagery tradition? State Barsalou's and Prinz's positions, and the challenge both face.

::: answer
Shepard and Metzler found that the time to judge whether two shapes are identical grows linearly with the angle between them, as if subjects rotate one shape mentally. Pylyshyn held that apparent imagery is description in a language of thought, and that the image-like phenomenology is epiphenomenal. For Barsalou, concepts are grounded in simulations, partial reactivations of sensorimotor states, so conceptual processing is perceptual processing in the absence of the stimulus. For Prinz, concepts are proxytypes, perceptual representations that stand in for categories, and the vehicles of concepts are always perceptual in origin. The challenge is composition: two images cannot be overlaid to give a conjunction. The essay takes this to suggest that image-like and language-like formats must coexist.
:::

### Block against Quilty-Dunn {kind=distinguish at=the-format-question-crystallized-block-and-quilty-dunn}

Where does Block place language-like structure, and where does Quilty-Dunn? Give Quilty-Dunn's evidence from visual working memory, Block's reply, and the rejoinder.

::: answer
*Block.* Perception trades only in iconic representations. Cognition can use discursive ones, and the border between seeing and thinking is the border between the two formats. Perceptual content that enters cognition is wrapped in a discursive "cognitive envelope".

*Quilty-Dunn.* His "perceptual pluralism" finds language-of-thought structure within perception. With his co-authors he lists six properties that cluster as a natural kind: discrete constituents, role-filler independence, predicate-argument structure, logical operators, inferential promiscuity, abstract conceptual content. All but logical operators, which remain an open question, show up in the perception of attended objects.

*Evidence and replies.* In visual working memory, an object's color and orientation can be lost independently. A picture-like representation would lose them together. Block assigns the discursive properties to the envelope. Quilty-Dunn answers that the visual system itself computes over object representations, to keep coherence across eye movements and to track objects, so the structure serves perception.
:::

### Analogy {kind=state at=analogy-as-the-missing-dynamic}

What do Hofstadter and Sander claim about analogy, and what follows for what a concept is? How does the essay relate analogy to the two formats?

::: answer
They claim that analogy-making is the core of all conceptualization: every categorization, even calling something a chair, is an analogical leap from prior encounters. Concepts are then never static data structures. They are patterns of analogical activity that stabilize for a while and are modified by the next encounter. The essay adds that analogical mapping needs parts and relations to map between, so it presupposes something like the language-of-thought properties. It also needs the fluid, context-sensitive similarity judgments that image-like representations support. Analogy might therefore be the process that bridges iconic and discursive formats.
:::

### The polysemy experiments {kind=case at=vehicles-content-and-the-polysemy-provocation}

Subjects judged arguments that equivocate on a word. What differed between "breakfast" and "bank"? What does the result suggest, and what conclusion does Quilty-Dunn draw? What does the essay's synthesis take from it for interpretability?

::: answer
"Breakfast" is polysemous: an event or a kind of food, two related senses. "Bank" is a homonym with unrelated meanings. People judged equivocating arguments over polysemous words significantly more valid than those over homonyms. This suggests a single vehicle for the polysemous concept, one that takes part in composition and inference without a determinate referent. Quilty-Dunn concludes that content is epiphenomenal: the causal work is done by vehicle properties such as syntactic structure, format and computational role. The [synthesis](/notes/concept_theories.html#synthesis-questions-for-artificial-minds) infers that interpretability research asking what a representation means may be looking in the wrong place, and that the mechanism of cognition may be best understood at the vehicle level.
:::

### Wierzbicka and Carey {kind=distinguish at=the-grounding-question-where-concepts-come-from}

What does each place at the base of the conceptual system? Where do their inventories fail to match, and where do they agree? What is Carey's answer to the doorknob problem?

::: answer
Wierzbicka's semantic primes are meanings lexicalized in every known language that cannot be decomposed further, such as SEE, FEEL, WANT, KNOW, DO, HAPPEN, GOOD, BAD. Every other meaning can in principle be paraphrased from them, so concepts have structure all the way down to the primes, against Fodor. Carey's core knowledge systems are innate, domain-specific systems for objects, number, agency and geometry, with limited compositional power.

Wierzbicka asks what is linguistically universal and Carey what is developmentally foundational. Carey's object mechanics has no obvious counterpart among the primes, and the evaluative primes match none of her domains. Agency appears in both. Both hold that the base is a structured repertoire of experiential and interactive primitives. Carey answers the doorknob problem with Quinean bootstrapping: new concepts are learned by restructuring the representational system, not by accumulating instances.
:::

### What the essay reads in the primes {kind=argue at=wierzbickas-semantic-primes}

The essay calls the primes phenomenally and agentively saturated. What does it mean, and what does it take the primes as evidence for?

::: answer
The bedrock includes experiential notions (SEE, HEAR, FEEL), agentive-cognitive notions (WANT, THINK, KNOW) and causal-dynamic notions (DO, HAPPEN, MOVE). If Wierzbicka is right, the universal foundation is a repertoire of experiential, agentive and causal primitives and not abstract logical machinery. The essay reads this as cross-linguistic evidence for two claims of its authors' earlier articles: that phenomenal acquaintance is a distinctive epistemic anchor, and that recursive self-modeling needs agentive conceptual resources. Every known language has lexicalized both kinds of notion.
:::

### The Bayesian school {kind=object at=the-bayesian-turn}

What is a concept on the Bayesian approach, and which earlier theory does it formalize? State the essay's criticism.

::: answer
A concept is a generative model, a probabilistic model of how category instances are produced. Learning a concept is Bayesian inference over a structured hypothesis space. The essay reads this as a computational-level formalization of theory-theory. Its criticism is that these are toy models. Each addresses one task with a hand-designed hypothesis space and no perception, memory management, attention, real-time dynamics or action. The hypothesis space, where the theoretical work lies, is stipulated by the modeler. The models give a normative ideal at Marr's computational level and say nothing about format, about retrieval, or about how the hypothesis space is built.
:::

### Which theory do networks vindicate? {kind=explain at=deep-learning-and-the-representation-question}

Why do the distributed representations of neural networks fit none of the surveyed theories cleanly? Give the three possibilities the essay lists and its caution about the third.

::: answer
They are too high-dimensional and context-sensitive to be Rosch's prototypes. They do not store individual instances, so they are not exemplars in the traditional sense. They are distributed and lack discrete symbolic structure at the level of single neurons, so they are not Language of Thought atoms. They are not analogical engines in any obvious sense.

They may be a new kind of concept. They may implement something like proxytypes, grounded in statistical co-occurrence instead of perceptual experience. Or interpretability tools may be uncovering structure like the language-of-thought properties, which is the possibility the essay finds most interesting. The caution: a sparse autoencoder assumes that the right decomposition has few active features, which is already a commitment about what concepts look like. The structure found may partly reflect the tool.
:::

### Grounding for abstract thought {kind=argue at=synthesis-questions-for-artificial-minds}

The essay grants that a concept such as DEMOCRACY may have no essential perceptual content. Reconstruct its conjecture that grounding still matters for such concepts. What concern about language models follows?

::: answer
Thinking about democracy usually involves perceiving tokens: a word read, heard or vocalized internally. The tokens do not resemble the content, but they keep the processing in contact with something real. The contribution is meta-epistemic. It goes to the truth-preserving character of the processing, through coherence calibration and error signals, and not to the truth of any particular thought. The ambient perceptual embedding of a biological reasoner may add a further layer of calibration. So grounding matters "architecturally rather than semantically". For a language model the question becomes whether its processing has this coherence. The concern is that without perceptual calibration, the truth-preserving character of its abstract reasoning "may be more fragile than it appears".
:::

### What the survey leaves open {kind=limits}

Name four questions the essay marks as open, and say where each arises.

::: answer
- Whether grounding extends to abstract thought. A grounding story for DEMOCRACY may give the concept's causal history and not its content ([synthesis](/notes/concept_theories.html#synthesis-questions-for-artificial-minds)).
- Whether the original grounding of proxytypes requires phenomenal acquaintance at some point. Prinz's framework leaves this open, and the essay's suspicion that it does is "a further commitment" ([Prinz](/notes/concept_theories.html#prinzs-grounding-story)).
- Whether the trade-off between compositional rigor and analogical fluidity is necessary. Language models seem to have the fluidity and cognitive architectures the rigor, and this may be an artifact of current designs (synthesis).
- What lies at the base, where Wierzbicka, Carey, Fodor and Hofstadter give four answers. Language models start from tokens and cognitive architectures from designed primitives. Whether it matters that neither starts from experientially and agentively saturated primes is "perhaps the deepest question connecting concept theory to AI" (synthesis).
:::
