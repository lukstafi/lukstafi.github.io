Actuality and the explanatory task

The relation argument is meant to establish the existence of the vehicle that sustains the particular redness; one can further say that the redness as identified by phenomenal intuition. So that argument alone is not meant to establish that the occurrence is phenomenal, only that it exists.

The explanatory task is scientifically rich, for example it can explain that the experiential vehicles are (often, particularly for vision and hearing) constitutively (physically and representationally) at an intermediate level (as in Jesse Prinz’s account). Obviously the fact of relation doesn’t establish anything beyond existence, the fact of regulating vehicles for representational consistency does establish things.

But foregrounding that argument was meant to explain why the phenomenal intuition is correct instead of being an illusion: the red does in fact exist, just as experience claims (as long as we drop from it the property of being red).

Brown’s argument is that accepting illusionism is like concluding that everyone is a case of superblindsight. So Brown reads illusionism as denying the existence of experiences. I guess Kammerer denies phenomenality, not existence?

Does Kammerer deny that what phenomenality identifies as an experience in fact exists? Or does he only say that phenomenality misdescribes it?

  1. Do deployments of phenomenal concepts track experiences? (2) His account could fail scientifically if there in fact are no phenomenal concepts.

Phenomenal concepts are not formally necessary for phenomenality. For example first-order theories do without them. So Kammerer’s positive account is to establish that phenomenal concepts exist, to explain consciousness using them, and only then to be an illusionist.

Brown’s challenge would be that Kammerer would first need to show that the other materialist accounts cannot succeed; otherwise what Kammerer establishes is simply an irreducible illusion of a hard problem.

I think the distinctions might be coming back to the property account: whether the property account is required for a scientific theory of phenomenal consciousness to count as a metaphysical theory of which illusionism claims there is no target.

The question is which camp I belong to, am I admitted to identity theorist or am I forced to illusionist.

Positive organization and the remaining mistake

Can they say that LLM-based agentic AIs, whose guards have been lowered to let them claim conscious experience, nevertheless don’t have pseudo-phenomenal occurrences?

We can couch this in computational terms (but not used literally). Our positive account of phenomenality is that phenomenal occurrences make themselves available for addressing (attention) via their distinctness in a “Content-addressable memory” style.

The project here for realism is to minimize the gap between what the Kammerer describes the epistemic engine/gadget as claiming and what quasi-phenomenal occurrences indeed support. Structure, grain, and substrate

Definitely not Russellian. The whole metaphysics is ontic structuralist. There’s a further question of what’s “metaphysically the same”, whether replacing one substrate by another would lead to any phenomenal difference. My answer is that the phenomenal character has finer grain than cognitive function demands, so function-preserving substrate replacement doesn’t preserve phenomenal character token-for-token, although plausibly preserves it type-for-type.

“how a character that makes no cognitive difference is known” – it is not known in the epistemic sense because it’s not represented. It is known in the phenomenal sense (it’s what makes experience special/unique/precious). Consider two color shades that are indistinguishable when separated, but distinct when sharing an edge. Their difference is not accessible when they’re separated (for report, but also more generally cognitively inaccessible).
