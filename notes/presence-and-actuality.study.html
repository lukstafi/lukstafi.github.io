Study notes: Presence and Actuality

Recall questions for Presence and Actuality. Answer each from memory, then open the answer to compare. The link under a question leads to the section it draws on.

Before reading

The essay and these questions assume:

  • Can Two Minds Share One Experience, “What the identity claim commits us to”: the identity of an experience with a regulated representational vehicle, a concrete occurrence.
  • Can Two Minds Share One Experience, “From the multiverse to worldliness”: the author’s move from abstract algorithmic organization to concrete occurrences.

Helpful, not required:

  • Indexical Unity: Higher-Order Consciousness, Integrated Information, and the Mathematics of Existence: the proposal that perspective constitutes actuality, which this essay withdraws.
  • Redness Is an Experience: On Consciousness, Properties, and the Moral Status of AI: phenomenal redness is not an ingredient detachable from experiencing.
  • Causation, Computation, Indexicality: A Complete Metaphysics: pattern-level causation: real patterns cause through a structure-preserving grounding.
  • The Possible, the Real, and the Actual: Against the Plenitude of Suffering: the earlier response on suffering, which this essay qualifies.

Questions

The answers state what the essay argues. The essay is written in the first person by Łukasz Stafiniak, with Codex and Claude. It withdraws one of his earlier proposals and reports Michał Wójcik’s position from his published dialogues. The last question asks what it postpones.

Presence renamed existence

argue Experience without determinate content ↗

A meditator’s report in Metzinger’s collection reads “I was very present, but not there. […] Pure existence.” What does the essay grant about such a report, and where does it locate an equivocation? Of which earlier mistake is this the mirror image?

Answer

Calling the episode pure existence can express the absence of an experienced distinction between something appearing and someone to whom it appears. An identity between experience and existence needs a further argument. The earlier mistake treated phenomenal redness as an ingredient that could be detached from experiencing and remain the same redness. The mirror image starts from phenomenal presence, removes determinate content, self-representation and the subject–object distinction, and announces that what remains is existence itself. The removals may describe possible experience. The equivocation is that phenomenal presence supplies the intuitive warrant and bare existence supplies the conclusion.

What can fail

distinguish Experience without determinate content ↗

On the working proposal, an experiential mode can persist when no determinate content is presented. What exactly fails in such a case, and what does not? Which three things must be kept apart for the proposal to explain anything?

Answer

The occurrence does not stop being a representational vehicle. It keeps its place in an economy organized around representing, and the regulation still treats it as a bearer of content to be maintained, compared and corrected. What can fail is the reconstruction of its content: the system’s embodiment and semantic history no longer warrant attributing any determinate content. Realism about the vehicle’s role survives where realism about a particular content does not. The three things: successful content presentation, continuing organized activity, and merely retained capacity. If the regulatory organization can be removed while experience persists, its claim to be constitutive fails. If a minimal state only retains capacities the theory required to be exercised, the theory needs revision.

IIT and intrinsic existence

object IIT and intrinsic existence ↗

Tononi and Boly write: “Consciousness can thus be considered as intrinsic, absolute, or genuine existence.” What does the essay welcome in IIT, what does it reject, and what obligation does it say IIT shares with the authors’ own account? What does the EEG study of pure presence challenge?

Answer

It welcomes the attempt to explain individuation through causal organization. It rejects the exclusion of overlapping organizations in favor of a maximally qualifying whole. Establishing an organization with IIT’s causal conditions and establishing its phenomenal character are distinguishable achievements, so the identification needs warrant. The authors’ identity between experience and a regulatory mode faces the same obligation: the identity must preserve the phenomenon it was introduced to explain. Boly and colleagues report that pure presence in experienced meditators came with reduced broadband EEG power. The essay notes that this is a preprint and that reduced power does not show an absence of organization. It still challenges any theory on which vividness tracks the amount of processing in a simple way.

Ninety-nine moments

case Mindedness and integration ↗

Michał grants that the Chinese Room may understand, then imagines ninety-nine successive moments verifying a proof through shared memory. What is his question, and what does the essay reply? State the two commitments of the position.

Answer

He asks why any one moment should amount to more than local processing under an inherited criterion. The essay denies that understanding is unproblematic while only phenomenality requires real unity. On the authors’ account functional organization is insufficient even for mindedness: the vehicles must participate in an integrated cognitive economy through their actual causal relations. Shared memory might be an active part of an integrated understanding, in which earlier commitments constrain later judgments and later discoveries revise earlier ones. Or it might preserve only a succession of locally adequate operations. The description does not decide. The commitments: some organizations are minded, and some activity occurs in an experiential mode. The hypothesis is that phenomenal activity is a subset of mental activity.

Michał’s esse

explain What does Michał mean by actuality? ↗

What does Michał borrow from Aquinas, and how far does the borrowing commit him? Give the three steps of Thomas Epistemes’s reconstruction and the step it leaves unsettled.

Answer

He borrows esse, the act of being by which a thing exists, as distinct from its essence. The borrowing does not commit him to Aquinas’s whole account. His own description is methodological: “I have accumulated formidable constraints on a theory of esse.” Physics names a missing science of actuality. Thomas, his chatbot interlocutor, distinguishes descriptive coarse-graining, operational individuation, and individuation as a being possessing esse. The middle step already identifies an objectively operative organization. What stays unsettled is whether that organization is a substantial individual, and Michał answers: “Now you get the picture.” On this reading organizational unity does not yet answer his question of actuality.

Three questions in one demand

distinguish Organization and individuality ↗

The essay says Michał’s demand bundles three questions that the metaphysics of material objects keeps apart. Name them, with the philosophers attached. Which one does the essay’s own argument address, and what is that argument?

Answer
  • Whether an organization composes one thing at all: van Inwagen’s Special Composition Question. He holds that simples compose something only when their activity constitutes a life.
  • Whether a composite is a substantial unity or an aggregate. Hylomorphists answer by form, and Koslicki develops that view. Michał invokes this distinction.
  • Whether a fully constituted individual additionally exists, possessing esse.

The essay’s argument addresses only the third. A being without esse does not exist. Correctly identifying a pattern in reality already establishes its existence, so we cannot grant that an organization exists and then ask whether it also possesses existence.

Merricks and redundant powers

object Organization and individuality ↗

Merricks holds that composites whose causal powers are redundant with those of their parts do not exist, except conscious persons. Why does the essay call this the closest analogue to Michał’s pressure, and what are its two replies? How strong does it say they are?

Answer

Merricks makes being an individual depend on a distinctive causal contribution and ties that contribution to consciousness. First reply: his argument describes the parts as arranged in the relevant way, and that arrangement, actually obtaining, is the organization. The redundancy is between two descriptions of one causal pattern, not between two causes. Real patterns cause through a structure-preserving grounding without adding productive power. Second: the exception is relocated. A person’s distinctive contribution is owed to the organization of mental activity and self-governance, which the authors treat as independent of phenomenality. The replies need defending against exclusion arguments and do not refute Michał. They show the disagreement is a recognized one, in which the authors take the permissive side.

The Chinese nation

case Organization and individuality ↗

How does Block’s Chinese nation make individuality look like an achievement beyond organization? What does holding the example fixed show, and what does it not decide?

Answer

Let “organization” evoke the distributed nation and “individual” evoke the familiar person. The nation’s apparent disunity makes organization seem insufficient, and the person’s apparent unity makes individuality seem additional. The example has changed along with the vocabulary. Hold it fixed: compare the nation’s organization with the nation as an individual, and the person’s organization with the person as an individual. In neither case does the second description add anything once the constitutive organization is established. A missing causal relation would be a missing part of the organization, not a further passage to esse. The correction does not decide which organizations are minded or experiential. Stipulated functional equivalence does not settle whether Block’s system preserves the relevant causal integration.

Other esses

argue Existence and construction ↗

Suppose an organization’s existence, mental activity and experiential mode are all accounted for, and someone asks whether there are further metaphysical obtainings. What standard does the essay apply? To which of the authors’ own claims does it apply the same standard?

Answer

The essay calls this its own shorthand for a possible objection, not Michał’s formulation. It grants that measurements and coarse-grainings can miss what matters. A proposed distinction still needs a route to determination: an account of what would make it obtain, distinguish its instances, or establish its possibility. The route may be empirical evidence, an explanatory theory or a constructive proof. The problem arises when every route is disallowed in advance and the bare postulate is still treated as a fact to accommodate. The same holds for the authors: calling a preferred process “experience” does not establish the proposed identity. And “genuine understanding” cannot be reserved for a remainder left after every organizational requirement is met.

Indexical actuality withdrawn

state Existence and construction ↗

Which claim of Indexical Unity does the essay withdraw, and what was wrong with it? What replaces it? How does this fit the larger change from the author’s 2025 position?

Answer

The withdrawn claim is that actuality is indexical: that perspective constitutes actuality. The author had pictured our quantum branch as undoubtedly actual with the actuality of other branches in question. That mixes our warrant for believing something exists with a difference in its status once it exists. An indexical account can locate us within a plurality. It does not establish the existence of the plurality’s members. Nothing replaces it as a separate ontological status. The questions are what exists, how it is organized and what measure it has. In 2025 the author located experiential identity in an abstract algorithmic organization across its realizations, and the 2026 writing locates it in concrete organized occurrences. The indexical account already reflected that change and carried the additional claim now given up.

Physics as a coarse-graining

explain Existence and construction ↗

Why does the author find “computational” a misleading word for the Ruliad framework? What does the framework say physics is, and how does that bear on Russellian monism?

Answer

Computation, as discussed with Michał, is a relation between a computer and a content: an organization whose states and transitions realize what is computed. In the Ruliad framework physics is already a coarse-graining. Bounded observers embedded in the Ruliad aggregate distinctions and encounter the regularities that physical laws describe. The coarse-graining is constitutive of the physical articulation: what counts as a physical object, state or relation emerges through it. A physical computer has to be located within that account. Russellian monism seeks the basis of experience in intrinsic natures beneath fundamental physical properties. On a structuralist reading there are no such natures beneath structure. And experience is organized at a grain far coarser than that of fundamental physics.

The constructive criterion

state Existence and construction ↗

State the criterion for an organization’s being real. Why does the author call the view anti-Platonist? What does the criterion not establish about a particular system? In what sense does he accept the Ruliad as a necessary abstract object?

Answer

A proof that a constructible model of the organization is possible establishes that it is real, provided the construction preserves the relations claimed to constitute it, including the embedding. A list of outputs or an interpretation imposed on an unrelated sequence does not. The view is anti-Platonist because arbitrary abstractions do not become real by being specified or mapped onto something. Once constructive possibility is established, no further act or substance bestows existence. A constructive proof for a mind would not show that an arbitrarily interpreted rock is that mind, since instantiation must be established in the particular embedding. The Ruliad is necessary in that any admissible grounding must accord with it, with infinite groundings excluded. It is abstract as an object within a theory of grounding. Neither claim commits him to an independently existing abstract entity.

Levin’s sorting arrays

case Levin’s Platonic space ↗

In Zhang, Goldstein and Levin’s arrays, elements following the same sorting policy cluster though no one specified clustering. What does Levin infer, and which inference does the essay reject? What would the constructive criterion require?

Answer

Levin interprets the tendency as an intrinsic goal alongside the designers’ sorting goal. His Platonic-space proposal extends this to an ontology in which abstract patterns can be minds, with physical bodies as interfaces. The essay rejects the inference from unanticipated capacities to minds acting from such a space. What an architecture explicitly specifies and what its operation entails are different questions, and surprise at the second does not establish a new source of agency. The experiment constructs a process that exhibits a regularity. To establish a mind one would need the organization constituting its mindedness. To establish Levin’s further claim one would need an account of how the pattern acts through its interface.

What is postponed

limits Suffering and reality measure ↗

Michał presents a plenitude of suffering as a consequence of Wolframian physics. How does the essay qualify the authors’ earlier response in The Possible, the Real, and the Actual? What does the demand for construction still provide, and why does the objection stay open?

Answer

Finitude of each occurrence does not imply finitely many occurrences, and it sets no upper bound on the intensity of finite suffering. Constructibility does not establish that severe suffering is scarce. Indexical location cannot make a suffering unreal because it occurs elsewhere. What remains is the demand for construction and measure: describing an arbitrarily intense suffering does not prove the possibility of its full embedding, including how the creature came about. A suffering Boltzmann brain shows why this leaves the objection open. If it is constructible, its lack of an evolutionary history does not disqualify it. Weighing it requires an account of reality measure, and until there is one the answer is postponed. The proposal about finitude and constructibility is itself a direction still to be worked out from the Ruliad framework.

Terms

Introduced in this essay:

  • content-sensitive regulation: organized activity that maintains, revises and coordinates representing states, responsive to differences relevant to their representational roles.
  • vehicle role without determinate content: an occurrence keeps its place in an economy organized around representing when no particular content can be attributed to it.
  • esse: Aquinas’s act of being, borrowed by Wójcik for the actuality a theory of consciousness must account for.
  • operational individuation: the middle step of Thomas Epistemes’s reconstruction: an objectively operative organization whose relations matter to physical interactions.
  • route to determination: what a proposed distinction needs: an account of what would make it obtain, distinguish its instances, or establish its possibility.
  • physics as a coarse-graining: in the Ruliad framework, bounded observers aggregate distinctions, and physical objects, states and relations emerge through that aggregation.
  • constructive criterion: a proof that a constructible model of an organization is possible, preserving its constitutive relations and embedding, establishes that the organization is real.
  • reality measure: how occurrences should be counted or weighted; left unresolved.
