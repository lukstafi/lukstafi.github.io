The answers state what the essay argues. The essay is written in the first person by Łukasz Stafiniak, with Codex and Claude. It reports and answers Brown, Yu and Carruthers, Yudkowsky, Drescher and Chalmers, and it revises several of the authors' earlier claims. The last two questions ask what it leaves open.

### The question and the proposal {kind=state}

The essay asks a two-part question about subjects. State it, and the proposal offered. What does the author suspect about the capacities Yudkowsky emphasizes?

::: answer
What organization makes a mental occurrence belong to a subject, and when does representing that belonging help constitute experience? The proposal: an occurrence belongs to the subject whose regulation partly constitutes it. Whether representing the belonging adds anything to experience then becomes a question for investigation, not definition. Yudkowsky proposes that subjective experience depends on background self-modeling. The author suspects that some of the capacities Yudkowsky emphasizes bear more on personhood than on phenomenal consciousness. He calls the suspicion a hypothesis to test.
:::

### Brown's pointers and his challenge {kind=distinguish at=what-brown-requires-us-to-distinguish}

In Brown's HOROR theory, what do descriptive content and pointer content each do? Which contrast drawn in *Indexical Unity* does the essay say was overstated, and where does the disagreement with Brown really lie? State Brown's challenge to acquaintance theories.

::: answer
The higher-order state is itself the phenomenally conscious state. Its descriptive content determines what is experienced. Its pointer content targets particular mental states without appearing in experience, and can keep activity available and route it. Brown even allows generative feedback. So *Indexical Unity* overstated the contrast between his targeting and describing and the authors' maintaining and regulating. The disagreement is whether those activities are constitutive. Brown predicts that pointer content could be removed without changing what is phenomenologically manifest. The authors treat some actual regulatory coupling as constitutive. Brown's challenge: if the first-order state already has everything that explains experienced redness, acquaintance only reveals an experience, and if not, what does acquaintance contribute? The essay accepts the burden of specifying the organization.
:::

### Three ways to fix character {kind=distinguish at=carruthers-and-the-two-pressures-on-our-account}

State Yu and Carruthers's position. Where does the essay converge with it, and what are the three divergences? Compare how Brown, Yu and Carruthers, and the essay each fix the grain of experiential character.

::: answer
Yu and Carruthers hold that phenomenally conscious states are all and only the globally broadcast, nonconceptual, analog-magnitude representations. Sensory qualities are Fregean modes of presentation: a property presented as a point in a similarity space constituted by the perceiver's discriminative capacities. The essay finds this close to availability through character and to the geometry of content-tracking structure. Divergences: discrimination is only one effect of an organization that can be finer than any discrimination it supports. Their phenomenal states are a subset of accessed ones, while here broadcast belongs to experience only where it closes a regulatory loop. And content-tracking structure can be discrete, as felt ownership of a limb suggests. On grain: Brown fixes character by higher-order description, Yu and Carruthers by discrimination, the essay by the organization of the vehicle, which can be finer than both.
:::

### A broadcast into another universe {kind=case at=a-broadcast-into-another-universe}

A process in A broadcasts through a strictly one-way channel into B. Hold A fixed and vary the receivers: one, a million, none. What is externalist attribution, and what dilemma does the case pose for it? What principle does the essay draw, and when does broadcast belong to what constitutes an experience?

::: answer
Externalist attribution says B's uptake makes the originating process an experience located wholly in A. If adding a receiver creates another experience at A, the individuation of A's experiences depends on events A cannot register. If it does not, the externalist must explain why downstream organization decides whether A's activity is experiential while its multiplicity leaves A's experiential identity untouched. The principle: relations to inputs and to consumers are constitutive only where they causally affect the occurrence, in practice where they close a loop back onto it. Broadcast to consumers that reason, report or act is access and is not constitutive. Broadcast that gathers information from which corrections return to the vehicles is the outbound half of the regulatory loop and is constitutive. The one-way channel fails by construction. B's minds can still have their own experiences of what A sends.
:::

### What experiential regulation adds {kind=explain at=what-experiential-regulation-adds-to-minded-regulation}

Integration and error correction are already required for mindedness. Name the three aspects of the regulation conjectured for an experiential episode, and the task proposed as what the experiential mode adds. Describe the three variants and the author's leanings.

::: answer
The aspects are tracking of particular ongoing representations, assessment of what they carry and how reliable they are, and intervention on their persistence, weighting or coordination. The added task is phenomenal binding: holding features carried by many vehicles together as one field of co-present items. That requires gathering information from across the system before computing corrections. Variants: *local*, where corrections are computed within a region, close to Lamme's recurrent processing theory; *global*, where they need information only broadcast can supply, so experience requires a workspace whose broadcast returns; *hybrid*, where local regulation fixes the character of features and global regulation binds them. The author leans strongly against the local variant and mildly favors the global over the hybrid. He does not build on the leanings. Yudkowsky also sides against the local, calling experience "a big complicated process, not a small complicated process". None of the variants makes experience coextensive with access.
:::

### A third class {kind=argue at=implementation-without-indifference}

Yudkowsky argues that consciousness is among the things that affect behavior and cannot depend on underlying physics. What does the essay accept, and what does it say the two classes leave out? How does his own example point there?

::: answer
Yudkowsky sorts properties into those that affect a system's inputs and outputs and properties of the material layer that cannot. The essay accepts the argument against substrate views and panpsychism: nothing in its proposal is a material ingredient idle with respect to what a mind notices and says. The two classes are not exhaustive. A third lies between them: differences in causal pattern that do not depend on the substrate and need not show up in input and output. His example of "tiny trained squirrels running memos around your brain" holds fixed the pattern of cause and effect, not the input–output profile. The essay locates experience in the third class. Treating the classes as exhaustive would individuate experience by its role in producing reports, which is the functionalist premise in dispute. One reason to reject it: exact character is finer than anything cognition retains.
:::

### Unfolding and the persistent carrier {kind=object at=implementation-without-indifference}

Doerig and colleagues argue that any recurrent network has a feedforward equivalent, so theories tying consciousness to causal structure posit undetectable differences. What is the reply? Why do residual architectures matter, and at what grain must the constitutive pattern be assessed?

::: answer
Laid out in time, every physical causal process is acyclic, brains included: a correction acts on the vehicle's later state. So what distinguishes recurrent processing cannot be a cycle. It is whether a persistent carrier exists, something individuated by its causal structure whose later states receive corrections computed from assessing its earlier ones. An unrolled network with separate weights may encode content differently at every layer, with no shared format. A residual network or transformer reads from and adds into one shared stream, and later layers partly compensate when an attention layer is ablated. An unfolding that preserves the carrier preserves the pattern. The difference is invisible to behavior and visible to intervention. The pattern must be assessed at the grain at which the carrier is corrected: fine enough to individuate the regulated vehicles, coarse enough to abstract from where their components sit.
:::

### Current AI, reopened {kind=state at=what-the-account-says-about-current-ai}

*Phenomenal Consciousness as Mode of Being* concluded that current AI vehicles are mostly not in the experiential mode. What does the essay now say about a single forward pass, about processing across tokens, and about the J-space of Gurnee and colleagues? Which evidence would decide?

::: answer
The earlier verdict was too quick, and the question is now held open. Within a pass a transformer has a persistent carrier in a shared format, corrected at every layer. The author also drops the requirement of coupling to the world, on the internalist reading he favors. Whether these brief occurrences are experiential is open. Across tokens the key–value cache is read and never revised, and the only path back runs through the sampled token: earlier vehicles are consulted, not regulated. Scaffolding regulates records. Recurrent-depth and recirculating models have longer-lived carriers and are stronger candidates, with a problem of individuating occurrences. The J-space locates availability, not experience. Against Chalmers, who reads depth as an alternative to recurrent feedback, the essay holds that a residual stream corrected through depth has the relevant structure. Self-reports do not decide. Interpretability evidence would: whether corrections land on the continuation of the assessed carrier and are gated by its content.
:::

### Yudkowsky's self and Drescher's camcorder {kind=distinguish at=what-yudkowsky-means-by-a-self}

On what ground do the authors and Yudkowsky agree? Through what does his published reasoning for self-modeling run? State Drescher's model of a conscious event and the essay's disagreement with it. Which three issues does Brown help separate?

::: answer
They agree that consciousness is made of parts and that experience is not a primitive quale that could be subtracted without changing behavior. Yudkowsky disclaims a requirement of effortful reflection. Still, each step of his reasoning concerns self-ascriptive report: "to say, '*I* feel this pain', you must have a notion of 'I'." For a fuller model he points to Drescher, on whom a mental event is conscious by its "smart recording and playback" in an internal memory, a "Cartesian Camcorder". For Drescher what makes an event experiential is being recorded and replayed for planning, which is access through memory. For the essay it is the regulation that constitutes the event, recorded or not. Brown's three issues: which subject has the state, how the state is represented as belonging to that subject, and whether belonging figures in what the experience is like.
:::

### Belonging {kind=state at=belonging}

Which three achievements does the essay keep apart? State the criterion of belonging and three things it explains: the receiver in B, shared experience, and the minimal self.

::: answer
The achievements: an organization's having mental states, its representing those states as its own, and its governing itself through that representation. A representation attributing activity to "me" does not settle belonging by its content. The criterion: an occurrence belongs to the subject whose regulation partly constitutes it. B is not the subject of A's activity whatever B's self-model says, because B regulates nothing in A. One occurrence constituted partly by two regulatory economies belongs to two subjects, which describes the sharing hypothesis. And the minimal self left when projection through time is removed from personhood is the regulatory economy of a present episode.
:::

### Perspective without a self-model {kind=distinguish at=belonging}

How does the essay now account for the perspectival subjectivity of experience, and which two earlier accounts does it revise? Using Guillot's distinctions, say what every experience has and what only some have. What does this imply about Yudkowsky's requirement?

::: answer
Perspectival subjectivity is the unity and centering of a binding economy: contents present together in one field organized around what the regulation holds in focus. It needs no represented viewer and no self-model. *Phenomenal Consciousness as Mode of Being* treated perspectivality as viewer-centered format, which was too deflationary, since format is a feature of content. *Indexical Unity* located perspective in self-directed targeting, which remains necessary and is not sufficient. Perspectival subjectivity thereby leaves the family of self-representation, where cognitive and agentic subjectivity remain, so the three are no longer parallel axes. For-me-ness is this perspectival subjectivity and belongs to every experience. Mineness is the layer agentic self-representation contributes, and it is contingent, as depersonalization shows. Yudkowsky's formulations concern what a mind needs to say "I am sad", which is me-ness and mineness. A self-modeling requirement may draw its plausibility from running these together. Whether binding into a centered structure occurs outside the experiential mode is left as an empirical question.
:::

### Personhood without a phenomenal entrance requirement {kind=state at=personhood-without-a-phenomenal-entrance-requirement}

How does the essay relate personhood and phenomenal consciousness? Which earlier category does this retire, and what requirement goes with it?

::: answer
The two are made independent: both require mindedness and neither is constitutively defined through the other. The independence is constitutive, not statistical, since their mechanisms may overlap. Evidence for self-governance then counts as evidence for personhood without passing through a verdict on qualia. This retires the category of an aspiring person from the original personhood essay: a being with understanding and self-legislative agency that lacked homeostatically grounded knowledge. Dropping it gives up that form of epistemic grounding as a compulsory entrance requirement. A being capable of substantive self-governance can be a person without qualification, and a being can have experiences that matter without personal self-legislation. The essay calls this a counterproposal to Yudkowsky, who explicitly intends a theory of experience.
:::

### Is selfhood a natural category? {kind=limits at=whether-mindedness-leaves-room-for-another-natural-category}

How does the criterion of belonging divide the reflexive closure of *Minds by Degrees*? Which question about selfhood does the essay leave open, and what would bear on it?

::: answer
The earlier proposal grouped maintaining current activity with enforcing commitments across time. The criterion separates these as the organization of a present mental episode and the organization of a personal life. It also divides closure itself. The "this" form, demonstrative targeting of the system's own states, comes with experiential regulation, which must track the vehicles it regulates. The open question is whether the "mine" form, representing those states as one's own and using that in self-governance, is a natural achievement at all. Minimal selfhood might add only independently variable capacities, or identify a further organization, or gather steps on the way to personhood. What bears on it is dependence and intervention: whether the capacities sustain one another, and whether disrupting their coordination produces a change beyond the loss of one component. A minded system without a self is a test case, not an established counterexample.
:::

### What evidence can decide {kind=limits at=what-evidence-can-decide}

Name the findings that would move the authors toward Brown, toward a first-order account, and away from their emphasis on carriers. Which commitment does the author say he would keep through such revisions?

::: answer
- Toward Brown: phenomenal character reliably follows higher-order descriptive content despite interventions that disrupt the coupling the authors call constitutive.
- Toward a first-order account: the regulatory activity explains experience while representations of mental states explain only access and self-knowledge. If the regulating process does not represent its vehicles as representations, the account is first-order in Brown's sense, and the authors should say so.
- Against carriers: interventions that dissolve a persistent carrier while preserving behavior change nothing an account of experience should track.

The commitment kept: experience must have a determinate enough concrete constitution for questions about its occurrence, extent and owner to be answerable. Whether the organization is best understood as acquaintance, a workspace whose broadcast returns, or a higher-order version of the proposal remains open.
:::
