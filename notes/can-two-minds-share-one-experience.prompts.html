Phenomenal unity and acquaintance

There is an intuition behind the notion of unified phenomenal field. That intuition is that the unification comes from having a connected scene graph (thus a clique under transitive closure, e.g. via “experienced beside of”). Is such a relation there (represented), and is it phenomenally manifest?

I have the intuition that I experience thoughts as located in my head – but only when I reflect on it. I think it’s wrong (e.g. need not be there when I don’t reflect on it). The regulatory relation (as it’s teleologically epistemic) tends to ride along representational edges, and in a broad sense it’s representational (we even called it acquaintance), but maybe doesn’t have to and maybe acquaintance was already a bit metaphorical?

The regulation is epistemic in the sense of addressing content (bearing to vehicles), so not arbitrary physiological regulation. We used “acquaintance” to get a handle on the hard problem. It’s articulated in one of (some of?) the essays.

Identity theory and the earlier metaphysics

Is there more that’s worthwhile bringing up in the essay, like some things about identity theory we could pull from primary sources, like Place and Smart?

Identity theory and Platonism (in the sense of “anti-identity” / functionalism) have a funny disagreement. Identity says one experience can be shared between subjects (as in our account in a metastable / unstable way, but despite the constitutive nature of the acquaintance relation). Platonism says an experience cannot be shared between subjects, but subjects can be shared between worlds in a metastable / unstable way.

Under Platonism, vehicle-identical experiences will be numerically distinct in two subjects; are they also phenomenally distinct, or is that moot on Platonism (no fact of the matter)?

What makes identity theory work is that some conceivable possibilities are not metaphysically possible (I claim). Two arbitrary regulatory processes cannot overlap a vehicle because of incompatible causal claims on the vehicle. There could be a conceivable assignment of vehicle to content where the vehicle participates with a different content in a different process; but that assignment is gerrymandered by the conceiver.

I was a Platonist (because algorithmic functionalist) not that long ago, switched over the course of writing the blog this year. One MRW reaction essay stated that forcefully (a section “Against Platonism”).

Physics and grounding

I tried to undermine Michal’s position by pointing out that physics is merely another explanatory science of patterns at some level of description, rather than the science of grounding itself. He conceded that it’s a worry for his position, but he hasn’t changed his position. I think that Chalmers’ psychophysical laws is a misnomer for a similar reason. On one hand, it reframes first by stating the form of the law. On the other hand, it picks the particular term “physical”.

To clarify: the metaphysics of Wolfram’s Physics shows that the physics of gravity and quantum mechanics is descriptive of emergent patterns rather than a science of grounding itself.

I would distinguish the success of Wolfram’s project as physics (explaining a yet unexplained phenomenon) from its success as metaphysics (where it’s on a slightly firmer ground). But we already have a long essay on the Wolfram project, which we shouldn’t retread in this essay.

Revisions to the first draft

“The move also produces an interesting reversal.” – I think it’s not clear enough why this is a reversal. The reason is that with the Platonic account an experience cannot be shared by two phenomenal spaces because of how it’s constitutively relational to the subject.

Do you think that “metastable” rather than “unstable” is the right word? I used “metastable” because regulation is temporally thick, a “pass through” intersection would not establish a shared phenomenal now.

Agreed, go for it. Except maybe “Regulation is temporally thick: compatible involvement must persist long enough to constitute a shared phenomenal now.” can be confusing as it connects two things that aren’t intrinsically explanatory in either direction, only assuming our framework gives the implication: “and that [on our account] explains the thickness of the phenomenal now”.

I would also change: “B’s experience may continue while A represents what it no longer shares.” to “B’s experience may continue while A’s experience changes without necessarily upsetting its functionality.”

My point was not so much to introduce the no-upsetting condition, which was just for illustration. My point was that your original phrasing sounded like a piece of experience would disappear while a representation is sustained. My point was that, among different options, my intuition says it’s more likely that the qualitative character of the representation would change for one of the subjects.

We could mention in the second half of “Metastability and larger subjects” That our account is less metaphysically burdensome than either Platonism or IIT because, with shared experiences, there is not a problem of experiences flickering in and out of existence, or a drastically sudden shift of qualitative experience (not driven by the relevant dynamics)

At the end of “Conjoined twins and divided brains”, would it be worthwhile to repeat that the framework makes the alternatives less mysterious, because even if ordinary were in fact, surprisingly, circling between two subjects and one unified subject, experiences would “stay as they are” because of sharing?

In the last section: “Yet asking physics to supply actuality leaves the crucial work ahead: specifying the event and the conditions under which it is one experience.” is unclear: what does “supply actuality” mean, and why wouldn’t actuality entail individuation?

What work does this sentence do? “This does not require an actual occurrence to await some further act of individuation.”
