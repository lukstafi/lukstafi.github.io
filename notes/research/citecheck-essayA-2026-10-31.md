## 1. Rovelli, "Aristotle's Physics: A Physicist's Look"

**(a)** Carlo Rovelli, "Aristotle's Physics: A Physicist's Look", *Journal of the American Philosophical Association* 1 (2015) 23–40. DOI [10.1017/apa.2014.11](https://doi.org/10.1017/apa.2014.11). arXiv [1312.4057](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.4057).

**(b) Partly accurate.** Abstract: "I show that Aristotelian physics is a correct and non-intuitive approximation of Newtonian physics in the suitable domain (motion in fluids), in the same technical sense in which Newton theory is an approximation of Einstein's theory." The domain is "objects in a spherically symmetric gravitational field (that of the Earth) immersed in a fluid (air or water)." The mechanism is the steady (terminal-velocity) regime of a falling body: Rovelli writes Newton's equation with buoyancy and a **quadratic** drag term −Cρ|v|v, notes that after a short transient "a steady fall where the velocity stabilizes" is reached, drops buoyancy, and gets v = c (W/ρ)^½. He reads Aristotle's claims as weaker than a precise power law ("What one can deduce from Aristotle's discussion is indeed a bit weaker"), writes v ~ (W/ρ)^n with n > 0, and says "What Aristotle does not have is only the square root, namely n = 1/2." He concludes "His factual statements are all correct."

**(c) Corrections for the essay.**
- Say "approximation" (Rovelli's word), not "limit".
- "Strong friction" and "terminal velocity" are glosses, not his terms. The regime is the post-transient steady fall. His drag is quadratic (high-Reynolds), not linear Stokes drag. Linear drag would give Aristotle's literal v ∝ F/R, but that is not Rovelli's argument. If the essay says "speed proportional to force over resistance", attribute the proportionality to Aristotle and the square-root correction to Rovelli.
- The paper is about natural motion (falling and rising in a medium) and also the heavens. It does not cover violent (projectile) motion as an approximation of Newton.

## 2. İnönü & Wigner (1953)

**(a)** E. İnönü and E. P. Wigner, "On the Contraction of Groups and Their Representations", *Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA* 39(6) (1953) 510–524. DOI [10.1073/pnas.39.6.510](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.39.6.510). Free at [PMC335486](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC335486/).

**(b) Accurate.** The paper introduced group contraction. Its model case is the inhomogeneous Lorentz (Poincaré) group going over to the Galilei group as c → ∞ (secondary: Gilmore-style reviews, e.g. [hep-th/0210304](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0210304)).

**(c) Caveat.** Contraction of the Poincaré *group* gives the Galilei group without central extension. Mass as a central charge comes from Bargmann (item 5), via contracting Poincaré × U(1) or its representations. Keep the two steps separate if the essay links contraction to mass.

## 3. Bacry & Lévy-Leblond, "Possible kinematics" (1968)

**(a)** H. Bacry and J.-M. Lévy-Leblond, "Possible Kinematics", *J. Math. Phys.* 9 (1968) 1605–1614. DOI [10.1063/1.1664490](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1664490). (Wikipedia's Bacry page gives 1969; CrossRef gives October 1968.)

**(b) Accurate.** Using Inönü–Wigner contractions, they found 11 kinematical groups: de Sitter, anti-de Sitter, Poincaré, para-Poincaré, the two Newton–Hooke groups, Galilei, para-Galilei, Carroll, static, and one more (labelled E′₁ in a later review). They connected these by a cube of contractions. Three assumptions underlie the list: (i) SO(3) isotropy; (ii) parity and time reversal are automorphisms; (iii) each boost generates a non-compact one-parameter subgroup. (Secondary: Huang et al., [arXiv:1007.3618](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3618), §I.)

**(c) Caveats.**
- The Carroll group was introduced earlier by Lévy-Leblond, "Une nouvelle limite non-relativiste du groupe de Poincaré", *Ann. Inst. Henri Poincaré A* 3 (1965) 1–12 (not checked against the original). Bacry & Lévy-Leblond place it in the classification; they did not discover it.
- The full classification without the parity and time-reversal assumptions is Bacry & Nuyts, *J. Math. Phys.* 27 (1986) 2455–2457 (as cited by Figueroa-O'Farrill).
- "Static" is the fully contracted algebra: boosts commute with H and P.

## 4. Figueroa-O'Farrill and coauthors: kinematical Lie algebras and "Aristotelian" spacetimes

**(a) The relevant papers:**
- J. Figueroa-O'Farrill, "Kinematical Lie algebras via deformation theory", *J. Math. Phys.* 59 (2018) 061701. DOI [10.1063/1.5016288](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5016288), arXiv [1711.06111](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06111). The D = 3 classification as deformations of the static algebra.
- J. Figueroa-O'Farrill, "Higher-dimensional kinematical Lie algebras via deformation theory", arXiv [1711.07363](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07363) (J. Math. Phys. 59 (2018); companion, D ≥ 4).
- T. Andrzejewski and J. Figueroa-O'Farrill, "Kinematical Lie algebras in 2+1 dimensions", arXiv [1802.04048](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04048) (J. Math. Phys. 59 (2018); companion).
- J. Figueroa-O'Farrill, "Classification of kinematical Lie algebras", arXiv [1711.05676](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05676). A 4-page summary with no journal reference.
- **Main citation for "Aristotelian":** J. Figueroa-O'Farrill and S. Prohazka, "Spatially isotropic homogeneous spacetimes", *JHEP* 01 (2019) 229. DOI [10.1007/JHEP01(2019)229](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP01(2019)229), arXiv [1809.01224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01224).
- Follow-up: J. Figueroa-O'Farrill, R. Grassie and S. Prohazka, "Geometry and BMS Lie algebras of spatially isotropic homogeneous spacetimes", *JHEP* 08 (2019) 119. DOI [10.1007/JHEP08(2019)119](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP08(2019)119), arXiv [1905.00034](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00034).

(The 1711.07363 and 1802.04048 journal details are from the papers' own comments; I did not resolve their DOIs.)

**(b) What "Aristotelian" means there.** From 1809.01224, Appendix A, Definition 4: a real Lie algebra **a** is aristotelian (with D-dimensional space isotropy) if it contains **r** ≅ so(D) and decomposes as **a** = **r** ⊕ V ⊕ S, with "only one copy of the vector representation". The basis is (J, P, H): rotations, spatial translations and time translation, and no boosts. Section 1.2 calls the resulting homogeneous spaces "spacetimes without boosts." Many arise by quotienting a kinematical Lie algebra by the ideal spanned by its boosts. The paper also notes that "aristotelian spacetimes … admit simultaneously a galilean and carrollian structure": absolute time (an invariant clock form) and absolute space (an invariant time vector field) together, i.e. a preferred state of rest. The simply connected aristotelian spacetimes (Table 2) are:
- A21: static (all spacetimes have limits to it);
- A22: torsional static;
- A23: ℝ × S^D (Einstein static universe) and ℝ × H^D;
- A24: a torsional example in D = 2 only.

The para-Galilean and static *kinematical* algebras both give the static aristotelian spacetime A21.

**(c)** In this literature "Aristotelian" is a technical label: no boost symmetry, so rest is absolute. It makes no claim about Aristotle's dynamics. The term "Aristotelian spacetime" for absolute time plus absolute space is older in the philosophy-of-spacetime literature (e.g. Penrose, *The Road to Reality*, ch. 17; I did not check this against the book).

## 5. Bargmann (1954)

**(a)** V. Bargmann, "On Unitary Ray Representations of Continuous Groups", *Annals of Mathematics* (2nd ser.) 59(1) (1954) 1–46. DOI [10.2307/1969831](https://doi.org/10.2307/1969831).

**(b) Accurate (standard statement).**
- Physical states carry ray (projective) representations. Bargmann showed when these reduce to ordinary representations: they do whenever the Lie algebra's second cohomology H² vanishes, as for semisimple groups and the Poincaré group.
- For the Galilei group they do not reduce. Its unitary ray representations carry a nontrivial exponent (cocycle) labelled by a real parameter, identified with mass. At the Lie-algebra level this is the central extension [K_i, P_j] = i m δ_ij, the "Bargmann algebra".
- In Poincaré-invariant theory, by contrast, mass is a Casimir (p²), not a central charge.

**(c) Caveat.** The mass superselection rule is usually attributed to Bargmann but is debated (e.g. Giulini, and [arXiv:1906.03725](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03725)). Say "mass appears as a central charge"; do not say "Bargmann proved mass superselection" without a hedge.

## 6. Stability of physical theories

### 6a. Mendes

**(a)** R. Vilela Mendes, **"Deformations, stable theories and fundamental constants"**, *J. Phys. A: Math. Gen.* 27 (1994) 8091–8104. DOI [10.1088/0305-4470/27/24/019](https://doi.org/10.1088/0305-4470/27/24/019). Author's PDF: [label2.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/vilela/Papers/DeforJPA94.pdf](https://label2.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/vilela/Papers/DeforJPA94.pdf) (scanned). The title "Stability of physical theories" is **wrong**.

**(b) Accurate in substance.** The abstract, from IOP via INSPIRE:
> "Models or theories that are stable, in the sense that they do not change in a qualitative manner under a small change of parameters, have a higher probability of having a wider range of validity. This also seems to be true for the fundamental theories of nature. Using the deformation theory of algebras, we review the stabilizing deformations leading from non-relativistic to relativistic and from classical to quantum mechanics. … One then finds that the resulting relativistic quantum algebra is itself unstable and admits a two-parameter stabilizing deformation. … a new algebra is then proposed as the stable algebra of relativistic quantum mechanics in tangent space. This is isomorphic to the algebra of the pseudo-Euclidian group in five dimensions."

His later summary ([arXiv:1111.5576](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.5576)) calls the two new parameters ℓ (a fundamental length or time) and 1/R (curvature).

### 6b. Faddeev

**(a)** L. D. Faddeev, **"A mathematician's view of the development of physics"**, talk at *Frontiers in Physics, High Technology and Mathematics* (ICTP 25th anniversary conference, Trieste, 1989). Published in H. A. Cerdeira and S. O. Lundqvist (eds.), World Scientific, 1990, pp. 238–246 (secondary). Reprinted in *Miscellanea Mathematica* (Springer, 1991) and in *Publ. Math. IHÉS* S88 (1998) 73–79, DOI [10.5802/pmihes.19](https://doi.org/10.5802/pmihes.19), free PDF at [pmihes.centre-mersenne.org](https://pmihes.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/pmihes.19.pdf). For an accessible citation, use the 1998 IHÉS reprint.

**(b) Accurate.** Quotes from the IHÉS text:
- "quantum mechanics is a deformation of the classical one with ħ playing the role of deformation parameter."
- "We say that the given algebraic structure is stable if all deformations of it lying near to it are equivalent to it … classical mechanics is not stable."
- "the group of relativistic dynamics is a deformation of the group of nonrelativistic motion. The role of the deformation parameter is played by 1/c². … the passage to relativism is into a stable structure. In other words, the Lorentz group is stable."
- "the two main revolutions in physics … are deformations from unstable structures into stable ones."
- He adds GR as a third deformation, with parameter the gravitational constant, since Minkowski space is "a kind of degeneracy" among Riemannian spaces. He predicts that the unification of ħ, c and G "must give us the ultimate physical theory, which is stable."

**(c) Caveat.** Faddeev says the *Lorentz group* is stable. The homogeneous Lorentz algebra so(3,1) is semisimple, hence rigid. The *Poincaré* algebra is not rigid: it deforms to the (anti-)de Sitter algebras. That gap is what Mendes and Chryssomalakos–Okon exploit. The essay should not write "Poincaré is stable."

### 6c. Chryssomalakos & Okon

**(a)** C. Chryssomalakos and E. Okon, "Generalized quantum relativistic kinematics: a stability point of view", *Int. J. Mod. Phys. D* 13 (2004) 2003–2034. DOI [10.1142/S0218271804006632](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218271804006632), arXiv [hep-th/0410212](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0410212). Companion: "Linear form of 3-scale special relativity algebra and the relevance of stability", arXiv [hep-th/0407080](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0407080).

**(b) Accurate.** Their claims:
- They apply Lie-algebra deformation theory to find the stable form of the quantum relativistic kinematical algebra.
- Starting from Galilean kinematics, their computer code reaches the Poincaré-plus-Heisenberg algebra.
- Continuing leads to "a three-dimensional deformation space" of stable algebras "with an instability double cone at its origin."
- Taking "moment" operators rather than positions as generators, the deformation parameters become invariant length and mass scales.
- Their toy example (§2) shows that the homogeneous Galilei algebra is unstable, with a single nontrivial 2-cocycle adding [K_a, K_b] = i t ε_abc J_c. The Lorentz algebra sits at t = −1/c², "being semisimple, it is stable."
- They credit Vilela Mendes 1994 as their motivation ("for the first time, the stability criterion for the non-commutative spacetime algebra is invoked"). They note he proposed the so(n) algebras ab initio, which left uniqueness open.

**(c)** Point for the essay: the same deformation with t > 0 gives so(4). Stability alone selects "semisimple", not "Lorentzian". The sign of t is an empirical input.

## 7. Bombelli, Henson & Sorkin

**(a)** L. Bombelli, J. Henson and R. D. Sorkin, "Discreteness without symmetry breaking: a theorem", *Mod. Phys. Lett. A* 24 (2009) 2579–2587. DOI [10.1142/S0217732309031958](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217732309031958), arXiv [gr-qc/0605006](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0605006) (posted 2006).

**(b) Accurate, but say it precisely.** Theorem 1: in dimensions n > 1 "there exists no measurable equivariant map" D from the space Ω of sprinklings (Poisson processes in Minkowski space) to ℋ, the hyperboloid of future unit timelike vectors. Equivariance means D(Λω) = ΛD(ω) for all Λ in the connected Lorentz group. The abstract adds "(even locally)." The proof: such a map would push the Poisson measure forward to a Lorentz-invariant *probability* measure on ℋ, which cannot exist because ℋ has infinite invariant volume.

The neighbour statement is a corollary: "no finite valency graph can be associated to a sprinkling of Minkowski space consistently with Lorentz invariance." The intuition is that "there is no nearest neighbour to a given point in a sprinkling". Every Lorentzian neighbourhood of a point has infinite volume, so infinitely many points lie arbitrarily close to the light cone.

**(c)** Write "no Lorentz-equivariant rule can pick out a timelike direction (a frame) from a sprinkling; hence none can assign each point finitely many neighbours." The theorem concerns measurable equivariant maps on the random sprinkling, not any single configuration. Later work (Dowker–Sorkin; [arXiv:1803.11484](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11484)) notes it does not by itself establish full Lorentz invariance of causal-set dynamics.

## 8. Fermi-LAT, GRB 090510, and later limits

**(a)** A. A. Abdo et al. (Fermi LAT and GBM Collaborations), "A limit on the variation of the speed of light arising from quantum gravity effects", *Nature* 462 (2009) 331–334. DOI [10.1038/nature08574](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08574).

**(b) Accurate.** Abstract: "place a lower limit of 1.2 E_Planck on the scale of a linear energy dependence … subject to reasonable assumptions about the emission." E_Planck ≈ 1.22 × 10¹⁹ GeV. The burst was at z = 0.903 and showed emission up to 31 GeV. Two independent conservative limits:
- (a) a dispersion test with |Δt/ΔE| < 30 ms/GeV at 99% CL, giving ξ₁ > 1.22 for either sign;
- (b) the 31-GeV photon not emitted before the burst's onset, giving ξ₁ > 1.19 for the subluminal case.

Less conservative assumptions give ξ₁ > 3.42, 5.12, 10.0 and finally 102. The paper says models with n > 1 "are not significantly constrained."

**Later, tighter limits (verified):**
- V. Vasileiou et al., "Constraints on Lorentz invariance violation from Fermi-Large Area Telescope observations of gamma-ray bursts", *Phys. Rev. D* 87 (2013) 122001. DOI [10.1103/PhysRevD.87.122001](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.87.122001), arXiv [1305.3463](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.3463). E_QG,1 > 7.6 E_Pl (95% CL, subluminal, from GRB 090510, no allowance for source-intrinsic dispersion).
- LHAASO Collaboration (Z. Cao et al.), "Stringent Tests of Lorentz Invariance Violation from LHAASO Observations of GRB 221009A", *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 133 (2024) 071501. DOI [10.1103/PhysRevLett.133.071501](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.133.071501), arXiv [2402.06009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06009). At 95% CL: E_QG,1 > 10 E_Pl and E_QG,2 > 6 × 10⁻⁸ E_Pl. (arXiv v3 was posted Feb 2026; I did not check whether its numbers changed.)

**(c)** These bound *linear-in-energy vacuum dispersion*. DSR-type models (item 9) can evade or soften such bounds through relative-locality effects, as Bisio et al. themselves note.

## 9. Bisio, D'Ariano & Perinotti

**(a)** A. Bisio, G. M. D'Ariano and P. Perinotti, "Special relativity in a discrete quantum universe", *Phys. Rev. A* 94 (2016) 042120. DOI [10.1103/PhysRevA.94.042120](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.94.042120), arXiv [1503.01017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01017).

**(b) Accurate.**
- A change of inertial frame is defined as a change of representation of the quantum-walk (QCA) dynamics that leaves the dynamics invariant.
- "The group of symmetries of the dynamics of the quantum walks … contains a nonlinear representation of the Poincaré group, which exactly recovers the usual linear one in the small wavevector regime."
- The resulting "nonlinear Lorentz transformations … happen to be of the same kind as those of doubly special relativity."
- Abstract: the usual Lorentz group is recovered at low energies, "while at the Planck scale the covariance is nonlinearly distorted."
- For the massive (Dirac) walk the symmetry group is "a nonlinear representation of the de Sitter group SO(1,4) with infinite cosmological constant, with the rest mass of the particle playing the role of the additional coordinate."

**(c)** "Deformed" is fair, but "nonlinear representation" or "nonlinearly distorted" is their wording. The construction depends on a choice of function f, and varying f gives "infinitely many copies of the nonlinear Lorentz" group. On GRB tests, they cite a Fermi analysis bounding the discreteness scale and suggest relative locality may compensate. Do not present the QCA as already ruled out or already safe.

## 10. Peierls–Nabarro barrier and the Frenkel–Kontorova model

**(a) Standard references:**
- R. Peierls, "The size of a dislocation", *Proc. Phys. Soc.* 52 (1940) 34–37. DOI [10.1088/0959-5309/52/1/305](https://doi.org/10.1088/0959-5309/52/1/305).
- F. R. N. Nabarro, "Dislocations in a simple cubic lattice", *Proc. Phys. Soc.* 59 (1947) 256–272. DOI [10.1088/0959-5309/59/2/309](https://doi.org/10.1088/0959-5309/59/2/309).
- O. M. Braun and Yu. S. Kivshar, "Nonlinear dynamics of the Frenkel–Kontorova model", *Phys. Rep.* 306 (1998) 1–108. DOI [10.1016/S0370-1573(98)00029-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0370-1573(98)00029-5). Book version: Braun and Kivshar, *The Frenkel–Kontorova Model: Concepts, Methods, and Applications* (Springer, 2004).
- Textbook: J. P. Hirth and J. Lothe, *Theory of Dislocations*, 2nd ed. (Wiley, 1982).
- Retrospective: F. R. N. Nabarro, "Fifty-year study of the Peierls–Nabarro stress", *Mater. Sci. Eng. A* 234–236 (1997) 67–76 (secondary).

**(b) Accurate.** The lattice imposes a periodic energy landscape on a dislocation (or a kink, in Frenkel–Kontorova). The Peierls stress needed to move it falls off exponentially with core width. A common textbook form is τ_P ≈ 3G exp(−2πw/b) ([DoITPoMS / LibreTexts](https://eng.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Materials_Science/TLP_Library_I/21%3A_Dislocation_Energetics_and_Mobility/21.9%3A_Determining_the_Peierls_Stress)). The Frenkel–Kontorova chain (Frenkel and Kontorova, 1938) is the standard discrete-soliton model. Its continuum limit is sine-Gordon, whose kinks move freely. On the lattice they feel the Peierls–Nabarro potential, which is exponentially small for wide kinks.

**(c) Caveats.** The exponent's coefficient differs between Peierls, Nabarro and Huntington (factor-of-2 corrections). Real crystals can deviate. I did not verify a closed-form PN-barrier formula for the FK model, so do not quote a coefficient. A moving kink in a discrete lattice also radiates phonons and decelerates. That point supports the essay's theme, but cite Braun–Kivshar for it.

## 11. Conway's Game of Life: glider and speed limits

**(a) Sources:**
- Glider: [Wikipedia, "Glider (Conway's Game of Life)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glider_(Conway%27s_Game_of_Life)).
- Spaceships: [Wikipedia, "Spaceship (cellular automaton)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceship_(cellular_automaton)).
- Speed limits: N. Johnston, ["Spaceship speed limits in 'B3' Life-like cellular automata"](https://njohnston.ca/2009/10/spaceship-speed-limits-in-life-like-cellular-automata/) (2009); also Johnston and Greene, *Conway's Game of Life: Mathematics and Construction* (2022).
- Canonical print source: Berlekamp, Conway and Guy, *Winning Ways*, vol. 2, ch. 25 (1982).
- LifeWiki (conwaylife.com) blocked automated access, so its claims below come via search snippets.

**(b) What is right.**
- The glider is period 4. Every 4 generations it moves one cell diagonally: speed c/4, where Life speed is max(|Δx|, |Δy|)/period. Its four phases come in two pairs related by glide reflection.
- It is the smallest spaceship (5 cells). Richard Guy found it in 1969 (Wikipedia; it was published in Gardner's October 1970 column).
- Speed limits: "The maximum speed that a spaceship can travel in Conway's Game of Life is c/4 diagonally and c/2 orthogonally" (Johnston, Theorem 1). He attributes the proof to Conway in 1970. So the glider sits *at* the diagonal limit, and the lightweight, middleweight and heavyweight spaceships (c/2) sit at the orthogonal limit.
- "Light speed" c (one cell per generation) bounds all information propagation. Only patterns in a non-empty medium (agars, "lightspeed wires") reach c.
- Most random initial patterns settle into still lifes and oscillators, plus escaping gliders. This is qualitatively right (Catagolue soup statistics); I did not check a figure.

**(c) What is wrong.** "Spaceships exist only at specific speeds" is incorrect.
- Each *elementary* ship has one fixed speed, and small natural ships cluster at a few speeds (c/2, c/3, c/4, c/5, c/6, c/7 orthogonal; c/4, c/5, c/6, c/7, c/12 diagonal; 2c/5, 2c/7, 17c/45, c/10 copperhead, …).
- Engineered ships go much further. Berlekamp proved in 1982 that ships exist at every rational slope. Gemini (2010) was the first oblique ship, and Sir Robin (2018) the first elementary knightship, at (2,1)c/6. Caterloopillars (2016–) give orthogonal speeds below c/4. LifeWiki: Life "has spaceships that travel in all rational directions at arbitrarily slow speeds."
- Corrected sentence: "Speeds are capped at c/2 orthogonally and c/4 diagonally; below the caps, engineered ships can reach every rational direction and arbitrarily slow speeds, but any given ship has one fixed speed."

## 12. Trautman and Newton–Cartan theory

**(a) Cited paper:** A. Trautman, "Fibre bundles associated with space-time", *Rep. Math. Phys.* 1 (1970) 29–62. DOI [10.1016/0034-4877(70)90003-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/0034-4877(70)90003-0). Trautman's own memoir ([arXiv:1909.12165](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12165)) describes it as his fibre-bundle treatment of gauge fields, based on 1967 Syracuse lectures. It is not his Newton–Cartan paper. (I could not access its full text to rule out a Newtonian example inside it.)

**Correct sources for the claim:**
- É. Cartan, *Ann. Sci. École Norm. Sup.* 40 (1923) 325–412 and 41 (1924) 1–25. The original geometrization.
- K. Friedrichs, *Math. Ann.* 98 (1928) 566–575.

**(b) Claim accurate.** In Newton–Cartan theory, absolute time is a closed clock one-form (τ = dt). The spatial metric is a degenerate cometric h^{ab}. There is no non-degenerate spacetime metric. Gravity is the curvature of a compatible, symmetric, non-flat affine connection, with R_ab = 4πGρ τ_a τ_b. Free fall follows its geodesics.

**(c)** Cite Trautman 1963/1965/1966 (or Cartan 1923–24 plus Malament 2012), not Trautman 1970. Say "curvature of a connection, since there is no spacetime metric to curve," not "rather than of the metric." Recovering a Newtonian potential needs extra curvature conditions (the Trautman/Künzle conditions).

## 13. Jacobson (1995)

**(a)** T. Jacobson, "Thermodynamics of Spacetime: The Einstein Equation of State", *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 75 (1995) 1260–1263. DOI [10.1103/PhysRevLett.75.1260](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.75.1260), arXiv [gr-qc/9504004](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9504004) (v1 withdrawn; v2 is the published version).

**(b) Accurate.** The Einstein equation follows from entropy ∝ horizon area together with δQ = T dS, required for all local Rindler causal horizons through each point. Here δQ is the boost-energy flux across the horizon and T is the Unruh temperature. "Viewed in this way, the Einstein equation is an equation of state." He also writes "it may be no more appropriate to canonically quantize the Einstein equation than it would be to quantize the wave equation for sound in air."

**(c)** The cosmological constant is left undetermined, and the derivation assumes local equilibrium.

## 14. Connes & Rovelli (1994); Heller, Pysiak & Sasin (2005)

**(a)**
- A. Connes and C. Rovelli, "Von Neumann algebra automorphisms and time-thermodynamics relation in generally covariant quantum theories", *Class. Quantum Grav.* 11 (1994) 2899–2917 (CrossRef; arXiv lists 2918). DOI [10.1088/0264-9381/11/12/007](https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/11/12/007), arXiv [gr-qc/9406019](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9406019).
- M. Heller, L. Pysiak and W. Sasin, "Noncommutative Dynamics of Random Operators", *Int. J. Theor. Phys.* 44 (2005) 619–628. DOI [10.1007/s10773-005-3992-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10773-005-3992-7), arXiv [gr-qc/0409063](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0409063). (MaRDI lists the authors in a different order; the paper and CrossRef have Heller first.)

**(b) Connes–Rovelli: accurate.** The thermal time hypothesis says that in a generally covariant quantum theory the physical time flow "is not a universal property of the mechanical theory, but rather it is determined by the thermodynamical state of the system." It is implemented as the Tomita–Takesaki modular flow of the state on the von Neumann algebra of observables.

**Heller et al.: the link is justified.** Their abstract: "on the strength of the Tomita-Takesaki theorem, there exists a one-parameter group of automorphisms of the algebra A which can be used to define a state dependent dynamics; i.e., the pair (A, φ) … is a 'dynamic object'. Only if certain additional conditions are satisfied, the Connes-Nikodym-Radon theorem can be applied and the dependence on φ disappears. In these cases, the usual unitary quantum mechanical evolution is recovered." In the introduction they cite Connes and Rovelli (1994) by name for "modular dynamics" and add: "strangely enough, this dynamics depends of the state φ."

**(c) Caveat.** Heller et al. use modular flow as the dynamics of a *noncommutative pre-spacetime regime* (an algebra on a transformation groupoid). They do not adopt the thermal-time reading that the state fixes physical time. They treat state-dependence as something that disappears, via the Connes cocycle / outer automorphism class, in "more realistic models". In their finite-group toy model the state-independent flow is trivial: "the state independent time does exist, but nothing changes in it." The modular parameter s "is not directly visible from the spacetime perspective." So "Heller's state-dependent dynamics is the same mathematical device as Connes–Rovelli thermal time" is accurate. "Heller endorses thermal time" is not.
