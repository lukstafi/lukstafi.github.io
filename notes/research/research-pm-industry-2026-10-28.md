# Prediction markets in practice, late October 2026: freshness research for the AGT-notes essay

Compiled 2026-10-28. Sources are web pages fetched or surfaced by search on that date. Search summaries were cross-checked against fetched pages where possible. Anything not confirmed from a primary or dated news source is marked **UNCONFIRMED**. Several outlets (Bloomberg, CNBC, CNN) blocked direct fetching; for those I rely on search-surfaced excerpts and on secondary outlets that quote them. That is noted where it matters.

**A measurement caveat that runs through everything.** Kalshi and most US venues report "volume" as contracts counted at $1 face value (notional). Cash actually paid is much smaller: for the 30 days Sep 28–Oct 27, 2026, Kalshi's notional was $89.6B against $20.4B in dollars paid, roughly 23%. The gap is largest for parlays/longshots: a 2¢ contract counts as $1 of notional. Polymarket's international exchange historically reported cash. Do not add venues' headline numbers together. (DeFi Rate methodology: https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/volume/)

## 2. Market mechanisms actually in use

**Summary: every major real-money venue in 2026 is a central limit order book plus paid professional market makers, with RFQ for combos. No notable real-money venue runs LMSR or another cost-function market maker. AMMs survive in play-money venues (Manifold) and small on-chain venues.**

- **Polymarket.** It began (2020) with an on-chain AMM over Gnosis's Conditional Tokens Framework. The usual account is the Gnosis-style fixed-product market maker (FPMM/CPMM). Wikipedia and one vendor blog instead say "LMSR-based"; I could not confirm which from a primary source, so the FPMM attribution is **UNCONFIRMED**, though it is consistent with the CTF stack. Polymarket moved to a hybrid CLOB in **late 2022** (off-chain matching, on-chain settlement; secondary sources, e.g. https://phemex.com/news/article/polymarket-shifts-from-amm-to-clob-to-enhance-liquidity-in-prediction-markets-28317 ; no primary announcement found).
  - **CLOB v2 cutover on 2026-04-28:** new exchange contracts (CTF Exchange V2, Neg Risk CTF Exchange V2), a rewritten backend, a new fee model, the pUSD collateral token, and a **$1M liquidity-rewards program** for market makers (https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog/predictions.md ; https://crypto.news/polymarket-rolls-out-clob-v2-with-1m-liquidity-rewards-to-harden-prediction-markets/).
  - **negRisk adapter:** in a mutually exclusive multi-outcome event, 1 NO share in any outcome can be converted into 1 YES share of every other outcome. This makes "against A" equal to "for the rest" at the token level and enforces the partition constraint without a joint pricing rule. The outcome set must be complete; Polymarket adds an "Other" outcome for that. (https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/negative-risk ; ChainSecurity audit https://reports.chainsecurity.com/Polymarket/ChainSecurity_Polymarket_NegRiskAdapter_Audit.pdf)
- **Kalshi.** CFTC-regulated DCM with a central limit order book. **2024-04-03:** Susquehanna (SIG) became its first dedicated institutional market maker (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240403664852/en/Kalshi-Onboards-Its-First-Dedicated-Institutional-Market-Maker).
  - A market-maker program has been in effect since 2024-03-11, with two-sided quote, maximum-spread and minimum-size obligations (CFTC filing https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules02262412176.pdf). It was later renamed the "Designated Liquidity Provider" program, with DLP slots auctioned (https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15410219-liquidity-provider-program).
  - Volume and Liquidity Incentive Programs ran from Aug 2025. **Kalshi filed to end its Volume Incentive Program (no earlier than Oct 13 / mid-Nov 2026) amid wash-trading scrutiny** (https://www.gambling.com/us/news/kalshi-end-volume-incentive-program-october-13).
  - Bürgi–Deng–Whelan (GWU/UCD WP 2026-001) describe Kalshi as quote-driven: every trade has a recorded maker and taker.
- **Manifold** (play money).
  - Dynamic pari-mutuel (DPM) for binary markets until **2022-03-15**, then a Uniswap-inspired CPMM ("Maniswap"; the Maniswap name is from memory and not found in current docs, **UNCONFIRMED**). DPM stayed for legacy free-response markets. A Multi-CPMM design was tried and removed in late 2022 because liquidity drifted (https://git.nunosempere.com/open.source/manifold/src/branch/main/docs/docs/market-details.md).
  - Current multiple-choice markets are a set of binary CPMM pools kept summing to 100% by **built-in arbitrage trades** (`calculate-cpmm-arbitrage.ts`; https://github.com/manifoldmarkets/manifold/blob/main/common/src/calculate-cpmm-arbitrage.ts). Orders fill "first against limit orders, then against the AMM". Creators and anyone else subsidize liquidity, and the FAQ warns "you should always expect to lose mana from subsidising a market" (https://docs.manifold.markets/faq). This is the closest live analogue to a subsidized automated market maker, though its loss is not bounded the way LMSR's is.
  - **Sweepcash** (real-money sweepstakes) launched Sept 2024 and was **sunset 2025-03-28**. The platform is now mana-only; mana can't be cashed out, only donated to charity (https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Manifold_(prediction_market)).
- **Gnosis / Omen.** Gnosis's codebase shipped both an LMSR market maker and the FPMM. Omen (DXdao, July 2020) used FPMM on CTF. DXdao deprioritised Omen in 2022. Gnosis now runs "AIOmen", an Omen environment for AI trading agents with Kleros arbitration (https://pm.wiki/uk/projects/aiomen ; https://mintlify.com/gnosis/prediction-market-agent/concepts/markets).
- **Augur.** v1 (2018) and v2 used on-chain order books; Augur Turbo used AMMs. Some secondary sources say "Augur's original design used LMSR"; that refers to the pre-launch design (**UNCONFIRMED**). A **revival was announced Aug 2025** (Lituus Foundation: new market plus a generalized oracle), with a REP migration ("Moon Fork") test reported July 2026 (secondary: https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2025/09/251885-augurs-planned-reboot-may-capitalize-on-rise-of-decentralized-prediction-markets/).
- **Is LMSR or any cost-function market maker in notable production use in 2026?** I found **no evidence** that any notable real-money venue uses one. Smaller crypto venues (Myriad and others) run "AMM" or "vAMM" products alongside order books, with unspecified invariants.
  - Research continues: Paradigm's **pm-AMM** (Moallemi & Robinson, Nov 2024; https://www.paradigm.xyz/2024/11/pm-amm) designs constant loss-versus-rebalancing for outcome tokens. "**Uniform-Loss Automated Market Making for Prediction Markets**" (Moallemi, Robinson, Zhu; arXiv 2607.17428, AFT 2026) studies how LP loss is distributed across states and time, which, the authors say, earlier bounded-loss work did not address.
- **Liquidity subsidy in practice.** The 2010 idea was a market maker with bounded worst-case loss b·ln n. Today's subsidy is an **explicit cash budget paid to professional market makers**: Polymarket's $1M liquidity rewards plus maker rebates, Kalshi's market-maker agreements, DLP auctions and volume incentives, and Crypto.com's market-maker incentives to chase Kalshi's parlay business (https://gamingamerica.com/news/1105858/crypto-com-market-maker-combo-incentive-kalshi-parlays). The volume-incentive variant is entangled with wash trading (§7).

## 3. Combinatorial and correlated bets

- **Kalshi "Combos"** (multivariate event contracts) were the first on a DCM, launched as an "Exotics" category around **Sept 2025**. A combo pays $1 per contract only if every leg hits. **Combos are priced by RFQ, not on the order book:** a request goes out and market makers return a price for the whole bundle; if no one quotes, there is no trade.
  - Share of volume: combos were **58% of Kalshi volume in Sept 2026 but under 13% of transactions** (CNBC, 2026-10-06, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/prediction-market-combo-contract-volume.html, via search excerpt). They were 71.6% and 69.4% of notional on the record days Oct 10–11, 2026 ($2.80B and $2.75B; https://cryptobriefing.com/kalshi-daily-volume-records-parlays/).
  - Cash at risk is far smaller than notional. On Oct 4, combos were more than 68% of $3.7B notional, but takers risked $90.7M (secondary).
  - Kalshi began charging maker fees on combos in late Aug 2026 (https://www.ingame.com/polymarket-us-launches-parlays/).
- **Who prices them? Oddpool census (2026-05-28, https://www.oddpool.com/research/kalshi-rfq-market-makers).**
  - The authors fired 270 RFQs and sampled 12,178 RFQs (~135/s) from the public firehose.
  - **13 distinct quoters.** Two "backbone" makers answered about half of all requests. Singles-only and combos-only makers were disjoint populations (8 and 5).
  - Distinct quoters fell from 3.7 at 2 legs to 2.7 at 5 legs, with the inside spread flat at about 40.5¢. Legs ran up to 35. Non-sports RFQs got zero quotes.
  - From latency, the authors infer that **only one maker appears to do correlation-aware combo pricing**, and they predict most price 2-leg combos under independence. This is an inference, not measured mispricing.
- **Polymarket US "Combos"** (Combinatorial Athletic Outcome Contracts, CAOCs): self-certified with the CFTC on 2026-05-20, private beta 2026-08-05, public around **Aug 21–24, 2026**. They take 2–10 legs (moneyline, spread, total) and are **RFQ-priced**. The offshore exchange offered combos from June 2026 (https://www.ingame.com/polymarket-us-test-launches-parlays/ ; https://cryptobriefing.com/polymarket-combo-trading-feature/). By Oct 5–11, 2026, CAOCs were **51.5% of Polymarket US weekly trade volume** (60% on Sunday) (DeFi Rate).
- **Multi-outcome consistency:** Polymarket uses the negRisk conversion (§2) and Manifold uses automatic arbitrage between linked pools (§2). Kalshi lists mutually exclusive outcomes as separate binary markets on the order book; consistency is left to traders (**UNCONFIRMED** whether Kalshi has any linking mechanism).
- **Measured arbitrage and mispricing:**
  - **Saguillo, Ghafouri, Kiffer, Suarez-Tangil, "Unravelling the Probabilistic Forest: Arbitrage in Prediction Markets"** (arXiv 2508.03474; AFT 2025, https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.27). Using on-chain Polymarket data, Apr 2024–Apr 2025, they separate "market rebalancing" arbitrage (within one market or condition, e.g. outcomes not summing to 1) from "combinatorial" arbitrage (across logically dependent markets, which they found partly with LLMs). They estimate **about $40M of realized arbitrage profit**.
  - **Gebele & Matthes, "Semantic Non-Fungibility and Violations of the Law of One Price in Prediction Markets"** (arXiv 2601.01706, Jan 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01706). Over 100k events across 10 venues, 2018–2025, about 6% are cross-listed. Semantically equivalent markets show **persistent execution-aware price gaps averaging 2–4%**, attributed to the inability to identify "the same event" across venues ("semantic non-fungibility") rather than to disagreement about information.
  - Bots: secondary sources say bots dominate small markets. CNBC is cited as finding bots were more than 80% of volume in Polymarket markets under $10k (CNBC 2026-07-02 "Prediction markets mostly have thinly traded contracts": https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/02/prediction-markets-mostly-have-thinly-traded-contracts-.html ; the 80% figure is **UNCONFIRMED** from primary).

## 5. AI and forecasting

- **ForecastBench (Forecasting Research Institute).**
  - Earlier extrapolation: LLM–superforecaster parity around Nov 2026 (95% CI Jan 2026 – Nov 2027) (https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human).

On *market* questions, Cassi AI's system was the first to rank above the superforecaster median, followed by an anonymous ensemble. On *dataset* questions, 17 submissions rank above superforecasters on the preliminary board. ## 7. Accuracy and integrity

**2024 US election**
- On the eve of the vote, Polymarket gave Trump about 58% and Kalshi about 55%, against near-50/50 polling averages. Trump won.
- **"Théo"** (French trader, 11 linked accounts) bet about $30M+ on Trump. Chainalysis estimated his profit at $78.7M, later revised to **$83.5M**. He said he distrusted polls and commissioned "neighbor" polls (https://www.theblock.co/post/324996/french-polymarket-whale-us-election-profit-france-ban ; https://foxbusiness.com/media/election-bettor-whale-made-much-more-trump-win-than-originally-thought-analysis-shows). Polymarket found no manipulation.
- One correct call is not a calibration test.

**Systematic accuracy**
- **Bürgi, Deng, Whelan, "Makers or Takers: The Economics of the Kalshi Prediction Market"** (GWU CER WP 2026-001, Jan/Feb 2026, https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2026-001.pdf). On 300k+ prices, Kalshi prices are informative and grow more accurate approaching close, but show a **clear favorite–longshot bias**. Contracts under 10¢ lose over 60% of money staked; a 95¢ contract has a +3.1% pre-fee return. **Average return: makers −9.64%, takers −31.46%.** The authors reproduce the pattern from modest disagreement plus a small tendency to overweight small probabilities.
- **Bloomberg (2026-10-07/08)** analyzed over a billion trades (https://news.bloomberglaw.com/securities-law/longshots-losing-98-of-time-dominate-kalshi-polymarket-trading). Contracts priced for ≥1,000% payoffs (≤~10%) **lose about 98% of the time**. They were about half of Kalshi volume since early 2025 and 52% of Polymarket volume over 21 months. Polymarket disputes this, saying about 1%. Yes-longshots lost 15% (Kalshi) and 27% (Polymarket) of stake, paying out 1.9% of the time against 2.2% implied.

**Wash trading**
- **Columbia study** (Sirolly et al., SSRN, Nov 2025, not peer-reviewed; https://decrypt.co/347842/columbia-study-25-polymarket-volume-wash-trading ; Bloomberg https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-07/polymarket-volume-inflated-by-artificial-activity-study-finds): about **25% of Polymarket volume over roughly three years was artificial**, with a peak near **60% in July 2024** and about 20% in early Oct 2025. Suspicious patterns showed in 14% of 1.26M wallets. The authors suspect airdrop farming, enabled by no KYC and no fees. Sirolly: "no evidence that the exchange is involved."
- **CNBC (2026-09-30)** (https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/kalshi-polymarket-trading-volume-scrutiny.html, via excerpts): on Sept 20, nearly half of dollar volume in Kalshi's ether perpetuals came from trades sized $5,495–5,505, about $5B in near-identical trades now under CFTC examination. Kalshi says hundreds of distinct users took part. On Polymarket international, low-odds legs see anomalously high activity. Both deny wash trading. Selig: "zero tolerance". **Kalshi then filed to end its Volume Incentive Program.**

**Insider trading**
- **Nobel Peace Prize (2025-10-10):** Machado's odds jumped from about 3.6% to over 70% hours before the announcement on a few accounts (about $90k profit). Norway's Nobel Institute opened a probe and said "we have been prey to a criminal actor" (https://www.theblock.co/post/374199/officials-probe-polymarket-over-suspicious-trades-predicting-nobel-peace-prize-winner-reports).

He pleaded not guilty (https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/april/27/doj-and-cftc-bring-first-of-its-kind-prediction-market-insider-trading-case ; https://www.forbes.com/sites/walterpavlo/2026/04/27/prediction-markets-first-insider-trading-case/).
- **2026 midterms:** more than $1.4B traded on midterm markets (CNN 2026-10-23, https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/23/politics/prediction-markets-midterm-elections-kalshi-polymarket). ## 8. Corporate prediction markets

- **Google:** "Prophit" (from about 2005; about 20% of employees bet over three years; studied by Cowgill et al.) shut down in 2011 after a blocked external launch. **"Gleangen"**, a play-money market (launched April 2020 by Dan Schwartz), reached about 8% of employees (~15,000) by late 2022 and over 1,000 monthly traders, more than Metaculus or Manifold had at the time. **Executives paid little attention to it.** (Asterisk, "The Death and Life of Prediction Markets at Google", 2024: https://asteriskmag.com/issues/08/the-death-and-life-of-prediction-markets-at-google ; ACX https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/mantic-monday-let-me-google-that)
- **Inkling Markets → Cultivate Labs:** Cultivate Labs (Adam Siegel, an Inkling co-founder; **UNCONFIRMED** lineage detail) **stopped supporting prediction markets on 2022-08-04** and kept only opinion pools scored by proper scoring rules (https://www.cultivatelabs.com/posts/why-we-stopped-supporting-prediction-markets). The reasons it gave:
  1. A market price depends on the whole trade sequence, so you "can't calculate a crowd forecast using only a subset" of forecasters, for example the best ones.
  2. The currency intimidated users and became "an impediment to participation".
  3. "Markets are ripe for manipulation": extra accounts, and markets created by people who know the outcome.
  4. "We don't know what game people are playing": traders may bet on what others think, and they have no incentive to share information.

  The company conceded that market prices are about as accurate as the alternatives. Cultivate now sells crowd-forecasting with an integrated "AI Forecaster" and runs government programs; INFER / the RAND Forecasting Initiative is **UNCONFIRMED** in this pass.
- **Microsoft, NewsFutures, CrowdIQ:** nothing current found. NewsFutures became Lumenogic around 2009–2010 (**UNCONFIRMED**). I found **no evidence of a 2025–2026 revival of internal corporate prediction markets**. The corporate niche has moved to surveys/opinion pools plus AI forecasters. Firms consume public market prices instead: Google Finance integration, CNN/CNBC data deals, and ICE (NYSE's parent) distributing Polymarket data to institutions as part of its 2025 investment (**UNCONFIRMED** detail).
