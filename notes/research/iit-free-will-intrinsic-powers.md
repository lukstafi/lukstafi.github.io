## The argument

1. **Experience exists first.** IIT starts from phenomenal existence. Its axioms (intrinsicality, information, integration, exclusion, composition) become postulates about cause–effect power. An experience *is* the Φ-structure unfolded from a complex, which is a maximum of integrated cause–effect power.
2. **The principle of maximal existence.** "Over a given substrate, what exists is only the entity that exists the most" (§6.1). So once the main complex exists, its neurons do not also exist, neither as a substrate as such nor as small entities of their own. "So it is I who exist—not my neurons."
3. **"Existence is not constitution."** A substrate is an operational basis, a transition probability matrix, from which cause–effect power is unfolded. It is not something that exists "as such".
   - Only intrinsic entities exist absolutely.
   - Bodies, organs, rocks and computers are extrinsic entities. They exist only relative to conscious observers.
   - A digital computer taken as a whole is reducible (φ_s = 0). It is "an aggregate of 'dust'", whatever it simulates (§6.3).
4. **"Only what exists can cause."** Actual causation runs between Φ-structures. The decision is a Φ-fold, a sub-structure of a Φ-structure, whose actual cause is a reason-Φ-fold and whose effect is the action. "So I exist and cause—not my neurons."
5. **Four requirements for free will (§5):**
   - *imagination*: envisioning alternatives;
   - *evaluation*: weighing reasons;
   - *decision*: deciding and intending;
   - *execution*: causing and controlling the action.

   Each must be conscious. "There cannot be free will without consciousness, but there can certainly be consciousness without free will."
6. **A fork in the mind, not a fork in the road.** "Not a fork in the road ahead (-<) but a fork in the mind here and now (Y)." Alternatives are co-present sub-structures of one experience, not branching trajectories. Indeterminism is required, but only so that intrinsic existence has alternatives "all the way down", and it buys freedom from predictability, not freedom of choice. Free will "is not only compatible with a high degree of determinism but requires it" (§8). "Causation is not prediction": a perfect simulation predicts the decision without existing or causing.
7. **Responsibility grows through self-changing actions.** These are freely willed actions that durably change one's own substrate, ultimately its transition probability matrix, and through it one's character, values and resolve. World-changing actions do the same for the surroundings. The paper likens this to Kane's self-forming actions, without his indeterminism. "With full rationality comes full responsibility."
8. **Positions against which the view is defined (§9):**
   - *Denial* (d'Holbach, Sapolsky, Harris, Pereboom, Strawson): substrate-only ontologies.
   - *Redefinition* (Hume, Frankfurt, Fischer and Ravizza, Wallace, Dennett, List, Lloyd): "substrate+" or multilevel ontologies, on which macro constructs are granted existence for explanation while "genuine causation only happens at the micro level".
   - *Libertarianism* (Kane, Chisholm, O'Connor, Ginet, McCann): the wrong medicine.

   IIT claims a fourth path: free will grounded in what exists and causes.
9. **Tests (§10).** The same hand movement is produced four ways: by tendon reflex, by TMS of motor cortex, by a Libet-style urge, and after deliberating about a donation. The predictions:
   - the causes of voluntary actions, but not of reflexes, originate within the main complex;
   - deliberate decisions originate in larger sub-structures, with higher structure-integrated causation (𝒜) than urges.
10. **AI.** "Digital computers as currently built support no significant Φ-structure, no matter how intelligently they act." They "cannot genuinely cause nor are they responsible for anything". The paper says this has legal implications.

## Where it meets our framework

### 1. A direct opponent of our dissociation

*Free Agency, Personhood, and Moral Worth* defines free will, in extension, as "*recursive self-modeling decision architecture*" (free-agency-personhood-moral-worth.md:35). The concept's intension is "saturated with consciousness, since we built the concept from the first-person case". Its extension includes systems with the structure "whether or not it is conscious" (free-agency-personhood-moral-worth.md:45). The bee-and-beehive case dissociates the two.

IIT asserts the opposite: there is no free will without consciousness. It is the strongest available statement of the view our essay rejects, and the personhood essay should use it as the main foil.

### 2. The crux is exclusion, and IIT's dichotomy misses our position

The whole argument runs through the principle of maximal existence, which is IIT's exclusion postulate applied to ontology: one maximum per substrate, and nothing else exists absolutely. *Is the Monastery Minded?* already adopts residual integration *without* the exclusion postulate. So we diverge from IIT at exactly the premise its free-will argument rests on.

IIT files views like ours under "redefinition", on a "substrate+" ontology (§9.2). On such a view, the micro substrate "exists as such", "endowed with intrinsic properties such as mass and charge", and causally closed. So higher levels are "carried along for the ride".

That description does not fit our metaphysics:
- We hold structuralism with no categorical natures (GRAIN:85; P&A:123). The micro level has no intrinsic properties that would privilege it.
- A coarse variable "earns reality when interventions and interactions repeatedly respect it" (GRAIN:131).
- "Correctly identifying a pattern in reality already establishes its existence" (P&A:85).

On this view no level exists "as such" more than another, so the micro level has no claim to causal closure that excludes the rest. Which grain does the causing is settled the interventionist way: by proportionality to the question, not by fiat about the micro level. [inferred]

The paper offers a dichotomy: intrinsic powers, or a substrate+ ontology with micro primacy. A structuralist ontology of real patterns at many grains, with no privileged level, is a third option. The paper does not consider it.

We share IIT's two slogans. "Existence is not constitution" matches our view that patterns exist at their own grain. "Causation is not prediction" matches the carrier criterion, whose differences are "invisible to behavior and visible to intervention" (ESOM:93). We locate both differently: IIT confines existence to one maximum, and we spread it across grains.

### 3. IIT makes the field the self, and pays the cost the user predicted

For IIT, "I" am the Φ-structure, which is a phenomenal field. During the monastery discussion the user argued that a phenomenal field is not a self, and that if it were, the self would become a distinct metaphysical category. IIT is the worked-out case. The field-self becomes the *only* absolute existent, while neurons, bodies and the substrate exist only relative to observers.

The personhood essay can present IIT as the clearest instance of the field-as-self view and read off its costs. [inferred]

The sharpest cost is **diachronic**. Responsibility in IIT accrues through self-changing actions that change the substrate's transition probability matrix over months and years. But what exists absolutely is the Φ-structure of the present moment. What persists across moments, carrying character and resolve, is the substrate and its matrix, and by IIT's ontology those do not exist "as such". So IIT's account of responsibility needs a person who persists over time, and its ontology supplies only intrinsic entities at a moment. [inferred]

Our framework has no such gap. Persons, characters and references are real patterns at slow grains, and in the v2 proposal the slowest defended references are revised in place over a lifetime.

### 4. The fork in the mind, given a mechanism

The distinction between Y and -< is IIT's best contribution to the personhood essay. It converges with the v2 overhaul proposal ([theory-overhaul-proposal-v2.md](theory-overhaul-proposal-v2.md)). The four freedoms map onto defended-reference dynamics:

| IIT's freedom | In defended-reference terms (v2) |
|---|---|
| Imagination: envisioning alternatives | Entertaining several references under quarantine, by simulation that mirrors each course of action (v2 §4, "Entertaining without assent") |
| Evaluation: weighing reasons | Assessing the entertained references against one's standing references: values, commitments, the model of one's situation |
| Decision: deciding and intending | Assent: one alternative swaps from target to term of one's own reference. The intention is a new defended reference, and its hysteresis is resolve. |
| Execution: control | Defending the intention against competing urges. Weakness of will is the loud failure by strain (v2 §2), and IIT's "push" from outside the main complex is input the reference fails to resist. |
| Self-changing actions | In-place revision of the slowest references, character, by the actions they govern (v2 feature 5) |

On this mapping, all four freedoms work at the grain of truth-bearers. They are features of mindedness, not of experience. That keeps our dissociation: a fork in the mind is a structure among references in one economy of error (v2 §5). It need not be a structure in a field. When the alternatives are also *felt* as pulls, that is the entertained references projecting into the field, which is IIT's Y as experienced. [inferred]

### 5. Responsibility through self-change converges with *The Cultivation Condition*

IIT's claim that "my capacity for genuine free will grows the more I can exercise self-changing actions", and its link between full rationality and full responsibility, converge with *The Cultivation Condition*'s account of self-legislative agency. The personhood essay can note the convergence and the difference in ground. IIT grounds it in intrinsic existence. We ground it in architecture: in-place revision of slow references, which a non-conscious system could in principle meet. [inferred]

### 6. AI, and the ground of the disagreement

IIT denies computers existence, causation, responsibility and free will, whatever their function. On our side:
- *Minds by Degrees* grades current AI as minded.
- *Free Agency* allows free agency to non-biological systems.
- v2 makes the question architectural: an AI qualifies through in-place revision and defense of references.

The disagreement goes back to two premises: exclusion, and IIT's assessment of Φ at the level of the physical substrate (the PQRS′ argument), not of the computation. Our carrier criterion and real-patterns ontology locate the relevant organization at the grain of the computation. The essay should state this disagreement at its source rather than at its conclusion. It should also note the authors' declared commercial interest, neutrally, since the AI conclusion has legal and commercial stakes.

### 7. Tests in parallel

IIT's four-scenario design can be reused with v2's predictions.
- *Resolve.* After a deliberate decision, the intention should show defense signatures: resistance to perturbation, such as distracting cues or competing urges, in proportion to the deliberation's depth.
- *Urges.* Libet-style urges should show little such resistance.
- *Reflexes and TMS.* These should involve no reference at all.

These are tests at the grain of mindedness, and they do not depend on Φ. If IIT's 𝒜 and v2's defense signatures dissociate across the four scenarios, the dissociation would be informative. [inferred]

## Recommendations for the personhood essay

1. Use IIT as the main foil for three claims: that free will requires consciousness, that the self is the field, and that only one level exists and causes.
2. Answer IIT's dichotomy with a third option: structuralist real patterns at many grains, and causation settled by intervention and proportionality.
3. Press the diachronic objection: IIT's responsibility through self-change needs a persistent person that its ontology cannot supply.
4. Adopt the fork in the mind with credit, and give it the v2 mechanism of entertained references, assent, and defended intention.
5. Keep the intension/extension dissociation of *Free Agency*. The four freedoms are features of mindedness, and consciousness is how we notice them.
