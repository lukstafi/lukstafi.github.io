## 1. Inventory of the notes (English, by section)

**§1 What prediction markets are.** A market mechanism for aggregating information about a future event. Examples, as of 2010: Microsoft, Google, InklingMarkets, thewsx.com, yoonew.com, CrowdIQ, NewsFutures. Eight InklingMarkets corporate case studies (consumer products, auto, energy, natural resources, telecom, consulting, financial services, a US bank's HR culture-change programme), and the telecom company's sample questions ("How many critical bugs will be in the bug tracker for product X by date Y?"). Securities pay off on properties of a future event; simple (which of *n* outcomes) or combinatorial (partial orders over *n* objects, Boolean formulas over *n* binary variables). Markets are fully or partly pari-mutuel; in a subsidized market the operator's payout is not balanced by other clients' losses. Order matching: divisible vs indivisible orders; for combinatorial markets the matching is a computational problem, and orders can match jointly when no pair matches (player 1 bets A > B, player 2 bets B > C, player 3 bets C > A). Liquidity is solved by a central bookmaker (market maker). **Three market types:** (1) markets that match orders among clients as soon as a match exists; (2) markets that pool wagers and split the pool "fairly" among winners; (3) markets where a client trades immediately with the market maker at a payout fixed at trade time.

**§2 Desirable properties; markets of type (2).** Budget balance, anonymity, truthfulness (called properness for MSRs), normality, sybilproofness, individual rationality, monotonicity. Theorem 1: a wagering mechanism is budget-balanced, anonymous, truthful, normal and sybilproof iff payoffs are $\Pi_i = m_i + m_i\big(s(r_i,\omega) - \tfrac1M\sum_j m_j s(r_j,\omega)\big)$, $M = \sum_j m_j$, with $s$ a strictly proper scoring rule (smooth, values in $[0,1]$). Remark: dynamic mechanisms are usually built on properness, so they are only *myopically* incentive compatible; under some information structures and initial estimates a trader gains by bluffing. The remark names, without covering, truthfulness for type (3) and for type (2) with multi-stage ordering.

**§3 Dynamic markets, type (3).** Three automated market makers, "differing in user-friendliness":
- *Market scoring rules.* A trader moving the market from $\bar p$ to $\bar p'$ pays $s_k(\bar p)$ and receives $s_k(\bar p')$ when outcome $k$ occurs. Proper / strictly proper defined. Variants: logarithmic $s_i = b\log p_i$; quadratic $s_i = b(2p_i - \sum_j p_j^2)$.
- *Cost-function markets.* The operator sells Arrow–Debreu shares; buying $\bar a$ at state $\bar q$ costs $C(\bar q+\bar a) - C(\bar q)$; instantaneous price $p_i = \partial C/\partial q_i$; valid cost function: prices nonnegative and summing to one. LMSR: $C(\bar q) = b\log\sum_i e^{q_i/b}$.
- *Sequential convex pari-mutuel mechanism (SCPM).* An order is a limit price $\pi$, a quantity limit $l$, and a 0–1 outcome vector $\bar a$. The operator solves $\max_{x,z,\bar s}\ \pi x - z + u(\bar s)$ s.t. $\bar a x + \bar s + \bar q = z\bar 1$, $0 \le x \le l$; $\pi x - z$ is the operator's worst-case profit, $\bar s$ slack, $u$ a regularizer that damps price swings. The price vector is the dual solution (shadow prices of the equality constraints); the client pays $\bar p^\top\bar a$. Log variant $u(\bar s) = \sum_i\theta_i\log s_i$.

**§4 Optimization background.** Lagrangian duality, weak and strong duality, Slater's condition; zero-sum games as a dual pair (minimax from strong duality); proof sketch of strong duality by a separating hyperplane; the price interpretation of dual variables. KKT conditions, necessary and (for convex problems with strong duality) sufficient. Eisenberg–Gale program for the linear Fisher market (maximize $\sum_i e_i\log u_i$; why the geometric mean: invariance under rescaling a buyer's utility and under splitting a buyer's budget), its four optimality conditions, an exercise to derive them from KKT. KKT for SCPM, giving Lemma 2: prices sum to one; are nonnegative when $u$ is nondecreasing; the total instantaneous price $\bar p(\varepsilon)^\top\bar a$ is nondecreasing in $\varepsilon$ (proof by concavity of $u$); the price function is integrable. Perturbation and sensitivity: $\lambda^*_i = -\partial p^*/\partial u_i$ (shadow prices). Ellipsoid method, three subroutines (feasibility check, separation oracle, minimal ellipsoid of an ellipsoid ∩ half-space).

**§5 Equivalences with regret minimization.**
- *5.1.1 From learner to market.* Split orders into chunks of at most $\varepsilon$; define losses $l_{i,t} = (\varepsilon - q_{i,t})/(\eta b)$ (valid for $\eta \ge 2\varepsilon/b$); exponential weights equal LMSR prices, $p_{i,t} = e^{Q_{i,t}/b}/\sum_j e^{Q_{j,t}/b} = w_{i,t}$. The Weighted Majority regret bound turns into a bound on the market maker's loss; as $\varepsilon\to0$ (with $T = O(1/\varepsilon)$) the worst-case loss is $b\ln n$.
- *5.1.2 From market to learner.* Weights $w_{i,t} = p_i(-\varepsilon\bar L_{t-1})$. $\varphi$-stable prices; Lemma 3 (second-order bound $\varepsilon^2\varphi/2$ on the gap between cost and linear price); Theorem 4: a valid cost function with $\varphi$-stable prices and market-maker loss bounded by $B$ yields a learner with regret $\le\sqrt{2B\varphi T}$.
- *5.2 MSR ⇔ cost-function markets.* Lemma 5: valid iff differentiable, monotone, translation-invariant ($C(\bar q + k\bar1) = C(\bar q)+k$). Lemma 6: valid iff $C(\bar q) = \sup_{\bar p\in\Delta}\big(\bar p\cdot\bar q - \alpha(\bar p)\big)$ for convex lsc $\alpha$ (from the representation theorem for convex risk measures). Lemma 7: prices are the maximizing distributions. Lemma 8: market-maker loss $\le\sup_{\bar p,\bar p'}(\alpha(\bar p)-\alpha(\bar p'))$. Definition 9 (equivalence on a price set). Theorem 10: a one-to-one correspondence between cost-function markets with strictly convex differentiable $\alpha_C$ and MSRs with strictly proper regular scoring rules, via $\alpha_C(\bar p) = \sum_i p_i s_i(\bar p)$ and $s_i(\bar p) = \alpha_C(\bar p) - \sum_j \partial_j\alpha_C\,p_j + \partial_i\alpha_C$ (the Savage/Gneiting–Raftery representation).
- *5.2.1 FTRL.* Theorem 11: when $\alpha_C$ equals the FTRL regularizer, regret $\le\sqrt{2T\varphi\sup(\alpha_C(\bar p)-\alpha_C(\bar p'))}$.
- *5.3 Cost-function markets ⇔ SCPM.* The original SCPM is not truthful (exercise). Repair: charge $\big(\int_0^{x^*}\bar p(\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon\big)^\top\bar a$ instead of $\bar p(l)^\top\bar a$; splitting an order then does not change the payment. Lemma 12 (duals of the capped problem are duals of the fixed-quantity problem). Theorem 13: the payment equals $C(\bar q+\bar a x^*) - C(\bar q)$ for $C(\bar q) = \min_t\, t - u(t\bar1 - \bar q)$. Theorem 14: any cost-function market is an SCPM with $u(\bar s) = -C(-\bar s)$. Theorem 15: if $\nabla u$ ranges over the whole simplex, the SCPM's cost function defines a market whose equivalent MSR is proper.

**§6 Combinatorial markets: complexity and mechanisms.** Permutation outcome spaces; two bidding languages: *subset betting* (candidate α finishes in a set of positions Φ, or a set of candidates Ψ fills position *j*) and *pair betting* (α beats β).
- *6.1 Matching (market type 1).* Decision and optimal matching problems as (integer) linear programs over all states. Theorem 16: optimal divisible matching for subset betting is polynomial (ellipsoid with a separation oracle by maximum-weight bipartite matching between candidates and positions). Pair betting, though it has only $n(n-1)$ securities, is NP-hard in both the indivisible and divisible cases (Lemmas 17–26, via minimum feedback arc set). Lemma 27: a sufficient condition for a match is a cycle $C$ with $\sum_{e\in C}b_e \ge |C|-1$ (a cycle of pair bets is contradictory, so at least one bet loses). Lemma 28: the best such cycle is found in polynomial time (shortest cycle under weights $1-b_e$). Greedy removal of shortest cycles misses interleaved cycles; Lemma 29: it is at best an $O(\sqrt n)$ approximation of the maximum number of disjoint cycles.
- *6.2 Pricing (market type 3).* LMSR over compound securities: the price of a compound security is the sum of its outcomes' prices. Theorem 30: computing LMSR prices and the cost function for subset betting is #P-hard (reduction from the permanent of a 0–1 matrix). Theorem 31: the same for pair betting (reduction from counting linear extensions of a partial order).
- *6.3 Regret minimization against the best permutation; 6.3.1 Polynomial-time approximation of subset-betting prices; 6.4 Polynomial pricing for tournaments.* Headings only, no text.

**Exercise sheet (V 2010).** (1) Eisenberg–Gale optimality conditions from KKT. (2) The implicit scoring rule $s_i(\bar p) = q_i - C(\bar q) + K$ and the explicit formula of §5.2 agree. (3) Build an example where the original SCPM is not truthful. (4) Show FTRL ⇔ SCPM directly, without going through cost-function markets. (5) Justify Theorem 1 of the *Operations Research* version of "A Unified Framework for Dynamic Prediction Market Design" (`http://www.stanford.edu/~yyye/scpm-or-final.pdf`): SCPM with VCG pricing is truthful for any $u$, and the client pays $\chi(0)-\chi(x^*)$ with $x^* = \arg\max_{0\le x\le l}\pi x+\chi(x)$, $\chi(x) = \max_{z,\bar s}\,-z+u(\bar s)$ s.t. $\bar ax+\bar s+\bar q = z\bar1$.

**Bandit sheet (IV 2010).** Eight exercises: proofs from Blum–Mansour ch. 4; second-order bounds (Cesa-Bianchi, Mansour, Stoltz, arXiv math.ST/0602629); high-probability bounds for Polynomial Weights; Exp3, Exp4, Exp3.S from Auer, Cesa-Bianchi, Freund, Schapire, "The non-stochastic multi-armed bandit problem"; follow the perturbed leader, uniform vs Laplace perturbations (Cesa-Bianchi & Lugosi, *Prediction, Learning, and Games*, §4.3).

## 2. Slips found by hand (28 Oct)

Checked by rederivation; the literature agent checks the statements against the papers in §3.

1. **§5.1.1, Weighted Majority bound.** The notes write $L_{A,T} - \min_i L_{i,T} \le \eta T + \ln(\eta)/\eta$; it should be $\ln(n)/\eta$. The derivation below it then uses $\ln(n)$ correctly in the last line.
2. **§5.1.1, the market-maker bound.** Multiplying the regret bound by $\eta b$ with $\eta = 2\varepsilon/b$ gives $\max_i\sum_t q_{i,t} - \sum_{t,i}p_{i,t}q_{i,t} \le \eta^2 bT + b\ln n = 4\varepsilon^2T/b + b\ln n$. The notes have $4\varepsilon^2Tb$. The conclusion (loss → $b\ln n$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, $T = O(1/\varepsilon)$) is unaffected.
3. **§5.1.1, the weights display.** $Q_{i,t}/b = \varepsilon t/b - \eta L_{i,t}$, not $2\varepsilon t/b - \eta L_{i,t}$. The term cancels, so the identity $p_{i,t} = w_{i,t}$ stands.
4. **§5.1.2, Theorem 4's proof.** The statement takes $\varepsilon = \sqrt{2B/(\varphi T)}$, the last line says $\sqrt{B/(\varphi T)}$. The statement is right: $B/\varepsilon + \varepsilon\varphi T/2$ is minimized at $\sqrt{2B/(\varphi T)}$, giving $\sqrt{2B\varphi T}$.
5. **§2, lead-in to Theorem 1.** The sentence says the mechanism has *all* seven properties iff it pays a weighted score; the theorem characterizes by five (individual rationality and monotonicity are then extra properties weighted-score mechanisms happen to have). The theorem also carries a "[TODO: add definition]" for strictly proper scoring rules.

Typos in the Polish (*sprzedarzy*, *zabużonym*, *tranzakcja*) do not matter for an English essay.

**Confirmed and extended against the papers** (literature agent, 28 Oct; details in `notes/research/research-pm-literature-2026-10-28.md`, Part A). Items 1–5 above are confirmed; the $4\varepsilon^2Tb$ slip is already in the 2008 source paper. Further corrections for the companion:

6. **§2 properties.** Individual rationality should say non*negative* expected *profit* (the notes say "nonzero payout"). Lambert et al.'s monotonicity is two-sided and about profit. Normality should say "expected". The family characterized in their Thm 3 allows the scoring rule to depend on the total wager, and assumes smooth nonnegative payouts. "Weighted average of a scoring rule" misdescribes the payout: each wager is paid its own score relative to the wager-weighted mean score.
7. **§4.1 Eisenberg–Gale standard form.** Indices should run $k = n+1,\dots,n+nn'$ with $m = n+nn'$. Lemma 2 omits the source's "consistency" item.
8. **§5.2 Lemma 6** needs "valid **convex** cost function" (Föllmer–Schied requires convexity, and Lemma 5's "valid" does not include it). Theorem 10 omits "differentiable" for the scoring rules.
9. **§5.3.** The original SCPM charges $\bar p(x^*)^\top\bar a\,x^*$ (final marginal price times quantity), not "$\bar p(l)$". The two repairs (integral pricing, EC 2009; VCG pricing, *OR* 2011) coincide.
10. **§6.1 Lemma 17**: "weighted" should be "unweighted" feedback arc set.
11. **§6.1 Lemma 29** is a lower bound (greedy can be off by a factor $\sqrt n/2$), not an approximation guarantee; the $O(\sqrt n)$ guarantee is Krivelevich, Nutov, Salavatipour, Verstraëte, Yuster 2007.
12. **§6.2** never defines $q_\omega$, and omits the Boolean-betting hardness results of the same paper.
13. The Hedge bound $\eta T + \ln n/\eta$ is valid but looser than the standard $\ln n/\eta + \eta T/8$.

### 3.2 After 2010, by thesis

**Market maker = learner.**
- Abernethy, Chen, Wortman Vaughan, "Efficient market making via convex optimization, and a connection to online learning", *ACM TEAC* 2013. Axioms (path independence, no arbitrage, information incorporation) force a duality-based cost function over the convex hull of the payoff vectors; trading is FTRL. Subsumes the notes' §5; gives an efficient pair-betting market maker by relaxing the no-arbitrage set.
- Frongillo, Della Penna, Reid, NIPS 2012: price dynamics as stochastic mirror descent. Abernethy, Kutty, Lahaie, Sami, EC 2014: the cost function is a log-partition function (exponential-family markets), with an equilibrium account of how traders' beliefs aggregate (check the exact result before using it).
- Freeman et al., ICML 2020: wagering mechanisms give incentive-compatible no-regret learning. Nueve & Waggoner, NeurIPS 2025: market designs from learning algorithms beyond FTRL.

**One mechanism, many faces.**
- Frongillo, Papireddygari, Waggoner, "An axiomatic characterization of CFMMs and equivalence to prediction markets", ITCS 2024 (arXiv 2302.00196; checked): "every such CFMM on $n$ assets is equivalent to a cost-function prediction market", and "every CFMM implicitly defines a proper scoring rule". **Uniswap is a fourth face.** With §4 item 1 this gives the essay a twist: the cost-function market maker lost the prediction-market venues and won DeFi.
- Chen, Devanur, Pennock, Wortman Vaughan, EC 2014: the §2 weighted-score mechanisms admit arbitrage; no-arbitrage variants. Freeman & Pennock, IJCAI 2018: axioms for the pari-mutuel consensus mechanism (the Eisenberg–Gale line).

**The computational wall.**
- Dudík, Lahaie, Pennock, EC 2012: constraint generation over a relaxed price space. Kroer, Dudík, Lahaie, Balakrishnan, EC 2016: Frank–Wolfe Bregman projection with an integer-programming oracle, run on $2^{63}$ outcomes. Hossain, Wang, Yu, SODA 2025 (arXiv 2411.08972): combinatorial LMSR price queries and updates as range queries/updates in computational geometry, with a VC-dimension dichotomy for sublinear pricing. Reframing: the price space is a marginal polytope, and tractability is optimization over it, which is the variational-inference view of graphical models.
- Ostrovsky, *Econometrica* 2012: information is aggregated in every equilibrium iff the security is "separable", which bounds the §2 bluffing concern.

**LLM era (2025–26).**
- Galanis, arXiv 2604.20050: LLM traders aggregate information only up to about two levels of interactive reasoning.
- Begin et al., arXiv 2606.26583: errors of preference-tuned LLM traders correlate at about 0.7, so a 10-agent market underperforms a single agent. Diversity, not number, is what a market aggregates.
- Scoring rules as RL rewards (RLCR, 2025); Turtel et al., TMLR 2025: no model beats Polymarket.
- Not found: any paper that runs a cost-function market maker as a learner over model outputs. (Possible remark for the essay, not a claim.)

Citations the agent gave from memory and flagged unverified: Shao et al. ICML 2024; Oesterheld & Conitzer's content; Kong & Schoenebeck; the Manifold/Gnosis industry note.

## 4. Practice in 2026

From the industry agent's report, `notes/research/research-pm-industry-2026-10-28.md` (all URLs there; 26 items flagged UNCONFIRMED). Two claims spot-checked against their sources on 28 Oct: the Oddpool RFQ census and the Saguillo et al. arbitrage estimate. The numbers the essay uses were then fact-checked in `notes/research/research-pm-factcheck-2026-10-28.md`, whose corrections supersede this section (e.g. the Kalshi sports share counts combos as sports, the wash-trading peak was Dec 2024, and the Prophet Arena result is not a loss verdict). Volume caveat: US venues count contracts at $1 face value, so "notional" overstates money at risk severalfold (Kalshi, 28 Sep–27 Oct 2026: $89.6B notional, $20.4B paid).

What the 2010 theory did and did not anticipate:

1. **No cost-function market maker runs a notable real-money venue.** Every large venue is a limit order book with paid professional market makers (Susquehanna became Kalshi's first institutional maker on 3 Apr 2024). Polymarket began on an on-chain AMM and moved to an order book in late 2022 (whether the AMM was FPMM or LMSR, and the exact date, UNCONFIRMED); order book v2 launched 28 Apr 2026 with a $1M liquidity-rewards programme. The subsidy survives as a cash budget paid to makers, not as a loss bound like $b\ln n$. AMMs survive in play-money venues (Manifold) and in research: Paradigm's [pm-AMM](https://www.paradigm.xyz/2024/11/pm-amm) (Moallemi & Robinson, Nov 2024), and "Uniform-Loss Automated Market Making for Prediction Markets" (AFT 2026, arXiv 2607.17428).
2. **Combinatorial betting took over, in its simplest bidding language: parlays (conjunctions of independent-looking legs), priced by dealers on request.** Kalshi Combos launched around Sep 2025: 58% of Kalshi volume in Sep 2026 but under 13% of transactions ([CNBC, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/prediction-market-combo-contract-volume.html)). Polymarket US Combos (CFTC self-certification 20 May 2026; public late Aug 2026) reached about half of its weekly volume by mid-Oct. Both price combos by **request for quote**, not on the book and not by a joint market maker. The [Oddpool census](https://www.oddpool.com/research/kalshi-rfq-market-makers) (28 May 2026; checked) found 13 quoters, two of which answered about half of all requests; non-sports RFQs got no quotes; and "our reading is that it's the only maker in the visible universe actually running correlation-aware combo pricing" (an inference from latency, not measured mispricing). **The #P-hard pricing problem is sidestepped, not solved: the operator does not maintain a joint distribution, and dealers mostly price legs as independent.** This is the essay's best 2026 hook.
3. **Consistency across related markets is left to arbitrageurs.** [Saguillo, Ghafouri, Kiffer, Suarez-Tangil](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474) (AFT 2025; checked): about $40M of realized arbitrage profit on Polymarket, of two kinds, "market rebalancing" within one market and "combinatorial" across logically linked markets. [Gebele & Matthes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01706): persistent 2–4% gaps between equivalent markets on different venues. Polymarket's [negRisk](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/negative-risk) adapter enforces mutual exclusivity at the token level (a NO on one outcome converts to YES on all the others), which is the notes' "simple" market structure built into the contracts. Manifold keeps multi-choice answers summing to 100% by automatic arbitrage between linked pools.
4. **Prices aggregate information, with biases.** Bürgi, Deng & Whelan ([GWU WP 2026-001](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2026-001.pdf), 300k+ Kalshi prices): accuracy improves toward close; a clear favourite–longshot bias; average returns −9.64% for makers, −31.46% for takers. Bloomberg (Oct 2026): longshots lose about 98% of the time and are about half of volume.
5. **Sports, not information, drive scale.** Sports are about 85% of Kalshi notional and 89% of its 2025 fees. All venues together: $79.6B notional in Sep 2026, against about $44B for Kalshi plus Polymarket in all of 2025. Valuations (Kalshi $22B; reported ~$40B and Polymarket ~$21B rounds UNCONFIRMED as closed).
6. **The binding constraint is law, which the 2010 theory did not model.** A circuit split on state power over sports event contracts: Third Circuit for Kalshi (Apr 2026), Ninth (28 Aug) and Sixth (25 Sep) for the states; New Jersey's certiorari petition, No. 26-299, pending (response due 9 Nov). Integrity problems outside the model: a Columbia study estimates ~25% of Polymarket volume was wash trading (peak near 60%, Jul 2024); the first DOJ/CFTC insider-trading case (Apr 2026); UMA resolution votes dominated by the top 10 wallets (WSJ).
7. **Corporate markets died, for reasons the notes' §1 examples could not show.** Cultivate Labs (Inkling's successor) [stopped supporting prediction markets](https://www.cultivatelabs.com/posts/why-we-stopped-supporting-prediction-markets) on 4 Aug 2022 in favour of opinion pools scored by proper scoring rules. Its reasons: markets cannot weight a subset of good forecasters; the currency puts people off; manipulation is easy; traders' reasons are unreadable. It conceded market prices are about as accurate as the alternatives. Google's play-money Gleangen reached about 15,000 employees; executives ignored it. No 2025–26 revival found.
