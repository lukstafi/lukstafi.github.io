## 1. "Pure motion" in Wolfram's writings

**Claim:** Wolfram's 2020 technical introduction discusses "pure motion" and how it relates to causal invariance/relativity.

**Verdict: CORRECTED.** The exact phrase "pure motion" does not appear in *A Class of Models with the Potential to Represent Fundamental Physics* (Complex Systems 29(2), 2020, pp. 107–536; https://doi.org/10.25088/ComplexSystems.29.2.107). I searched the full 448-page PDF (https://www.wolframphysics.org/technical-introduction/inc/Wolfram-ModelsForPhysics.pdf). The phrase is also absent from the April 2020 announcement post and the 2021 one-year update.

**What the 2020 Introduction actually says about motion** is the *passive* reading, which is the essay's foil:

- §8.3 "Potential Basic Translations" (https://www.wolframphysics.org/technical-introduction/potential-relation-to-physics/potential-basic-translations/): "motion: possible because of causal invariance; associated with change of causal foliations". Also: "Lorentz invariance: consequence of causal invariance in the causal graph"; "time dilation: eﬀect of diﬀerent foliations of the causal graph"; "inertial frame: parallel foliation of causal graph"; "point particle: a persistent structure in the hypergraph involving comparatively few nodes".
- §8.6 "Motion and Special Relativity" (https://www.wolframphysics.org/technical-introduction/potential-relation-to-physics/motion-and-special-relativity/): "To see how this works, consider the phenomenon of motion. In standard physics, one thinks of diﬀerent states of uniform motion as corresponding to diﬀerent inertial reference frames … These diﬀerent reference frames in turn correspond to diﬀerent choices of sequences of spacelike hypersurfaces, or, in our setup, diﬀerent foliations of the causal graph." The example is the string rule BA→AB with tilted foliations. The section closes: "if the underlying rule has causal invariance, its limiting behavior will show relativistic invariance, and (so long as it has limiting geometry corresponding to ﬂat d‐dimensional space) all the usual phenomena of special relativity."

So in 2020, "motion" means re-slicing a fixed causal graph. No object is moved.

**Where "pure motion" is actually discussed (and is said to be nontrivial):**

- *The Concept of the Ruliad* (10 Nov 2021), section "Observers Like Us" (https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2021/11/the-concept-of-the-ruliad/): "At the outset, it’s not obvious that there can be “pure motion”, in which something can move in space without “fundamentally changing”. But it turns out again to be consistent to view this as how things work for us: that even though we’re “made of different atoms of space” when we’re in different places, we can still imagine that in some sense we maintain the “same identity”." This is the earliest occurrence I found.
- *The Physicalization of Metamathematics* (Mar 2022) (https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2022/03/the-physicalization-of-metamathematics-and-its-implications-for-the-foundations-of-mathematics/): "the concept that pure motion is possible in physical space—or, in other words, that it’s possible for things to move around in physical space without fundamentally changing their character." Also: "even in physical space a concept like “pure motion” in which objects can move while maintaining their identity doesn’t always work. For example, close to a spacetime singularity … any “object” [will] inevitably be “shredded”."
- **Main source: *On the Concept of Motion* (18 Mar 2022)** (https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2022/03/on-the-concept-of-motion/). Sections: "How Is It That Things Can Move?", "Motion Can Be a Complicated Story", "The Observer Is Actually inside the System", "Motion beyond Physical Space: The Branchial Case", "Motion in Rulial Space".
  - "How Is It That Things Can Move?": "the concept of “pure motion” is that there can be a “thing” that “just moves” without “changing its character”. But if the thing is “made of atoms of space” that are continually getting updated, what does this mean? Somehow the identity of the “thing” has to be associated with some collective characteristic that doesn’t depend on the particular atoms of space from which it’s made."
  - Same section, the key "nontrivial" sentence: "So in the end what we imagine is that there are certain “carriers of pure motion”: certain collective features of space that are persistent enough that we can consider them to “just move”, without changing. At the outset it’s not obvious that any such features should exist at all, and that pure motion should ever be possible. Unlike in the traditional “pure geometrical” view of space, in our Physics Project it’s something one has to explicitly derive from the underlying structure of the model—though it seems quite likely that it’s ultimately an inevitable and ubiquitous consequence of rather general “topological” features of hypergraph rewriting."
  - "The Observer Is Actually inside the System", on causal invariance: "this particular underlying system has the property of causal invariance, which makes the causal graph have the same structure independent of these choices. And in general whenever there’s causal invariance (which there inevitably will be at least at the ultimate level of the ruliad) this has the important implication that there’s relativistic invariance in the system."
  - Same section, linking pure motion to the observer and the continuum rather than to causal invariance: "If our perception of “where we are” could “jump around” the causal graph, we’d never be able to define a coherent concept of pure motion." And: "pure motion is in a sense an operational consequence not necessarily of an actual continuum world, but of a continuum perception of the world by an embedded observer like us."

**Relevance to the essay:** Wolfram does treat pure motion as something to be *derived*, separately from causal invariance. He attributes its likely origin to "topological" features plus observer coarse-graining, not to a velocity-dense family of boosted structures. He never states the active/passive distinction in those terms. So "Wolfram himself flags that pure motion is nontrivial" is safe if cited to the 2022 essay. "The 2020 Introduction discusses pure motion" is not safe. The 2020 Introduction's line "motion: possible because of causal invariance" is a usable quotation for the passive reading the essay criticizes.

## 2. Energy, momentum, mass as causal-edge fluxes

**Verdict: VERIFIED for energy and momentum; mass CORRECTED.**

- 2020 announcement, *Finally We May Have a Path to the Fundamental Theory of Physics… and It’s Beautiful* (14 Apr 2020), section "What Is Energy? What Is Mass?" (https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2020/04/finally-we-may-have-a-path-to-the-fundamental-theory-of-physics-and-its-beautiful/): "The technical statement is: energy corresponds to the flux of causal edges through spacelike hypersurfaces. And, by the way, momentum corresponds to the flux of causal edges through timelike hypersurfaces."
- On the background: "a lot of the causal edges are really just going into “maintaining the structure of space”. … whatever we consider to be “energy” corresponds to the fluctuations of that flux around its background value."
- On mass (same section): edges on the light-cone boundary "“reach out” in the hypergraph"; "more vertical" edges "are associated with events that in a sense reuse elements in the hypergraph, without involving new ones. And it looks like these causal edges have an important interpretation: they are associated with mass (or, more specifically, rest mass). OK, so the total flux of causal edges through spacelike hypersurfaces corresponds to energy. And now we’re saying that the flux of causal edges specifically in the timelike direction corresponds to rest mass." He then derives E = mc² for v ≪ c, "with a backdrop of causal invariance, and a whole host of detailed mathematical limit questions that we’re not discussing here".
- Technical Introduction §8.3: "energy: ﬂux of edges in the multiway causal graph through spacelike (or branchlike) hypersurfaces"; "momentum: ﬂux of edges in the multiway causal graph through timelike hypersurfaces"; "(rest) mass: numbers of nodes in the hypergraph being reused in updating events".
- Technical Introduction §8.8 "Matter, Energy and Gravitation" hedges more: "a potential hypothesis about energy and momentum is that they may simply correspond to excess “ﬂuxes” of causal edges in time and space."

**Suggested wording:** "rest mass as the timelike-directed part of that flux: activity that reuses the same hypergraph elements rather than reaching out to new ones." "Rest-frame energy" is an acceptable gloss, since E = mc² is derived from it, but it is not Wolfram's definition.

## 3. Time dilation as a computational budget

**Verdict: VERIFIED. Cite the 2024 essay.**

*On the Nature of Time* (8 Oct 2024), section "So What in the End Is Time?" (https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2024/10/on-the-nature-of-time/): "One starts from the fact that in our Physics Project space and everything in it is represented by a hypergraph which is continually getting rewritten. And the evolution of any object through time is then defined by these rewritings. But if the object moves, then in effect it has to be “re-created at a different place in space”—and this process takes up a certain number of rewritings, leaving fewer for the intrinsic evolution of the object itself, and thus causing time to effectively “run slower” for it. (And, yes, while this is a qualitative description, one can make it quite formal and precise, and recover the usual formulas for relativistic time dilation.)"

The 2020 versions are the foliation account:
- Announcement: "in the effort to sample space faster, our observer experiences slower updating of the system in time."
- Technical Introduction §8.6: "time dilation is then, for example, associated with the fact that to reach what corresponds to an event at slice t in the original foliation, one now has to go through a sequence of events that is longer by a factor of γ".

Note for the essay: the 2024 budget picture is the one place where Wolfram describes motion *actively*, as a body being re-created at new positions. It presupposes the very carrier the essay says must be earned.

## 4. Pure motion in rulial space

**Verdict: VERIFIED.**

- *The Concept of the Ruliad* (Nov 2021), section "Living in Rulial Space": "So how do we “move” from one point to another? Effectively we have to be emulating the behavior of one rule by another. … One can think of the program as an interpreter or translator that goes from one rule to another. The Principle of Computational Equivalence tells one that such a translator must essentially always exist. But how fast will the translator run? Effectively that’s what distance in rulial space measures." The section also introduces "“emulation cones”—and a “maximum emulation speed”".
- Same essay, section "Communicating across Rulial Space": "when an observer “moves” in rulial space, they’re effectively doing a translation from one description language to another. (And, yes, there’s a maximum rate of motion ρ in rulial space—which is the rulial analog of the speed of light—and which is effectively determined by the fundamental processing speed of the universe.)"
- 2020 announcement (section on rulial space): "we can think of different reference frames in rulial space as corresponding to different description languages … in rulial space it just takes choosing a different reference frame to have our representation of the universe use a different description language." This is again the passive version.
- *On the Concept of Motion* (2022), section "Motion in Rulial Space": "Inevitably, though, there’s a translation that can be made. … there’s always an “interpreter” that can be created in one system that can translate to the other." Also: "at this level there are no “things that are moving from one place to another in rulial space”; everything is just being “atomized”." And: "I suspect that the analog in rulial space of particles in physical space is basically concepts … they’re a reflection of the possibility of pure motion in rulial space: they’re a way to have some kind of persistent “thing” that can be traced across rulial space."
- *Observer Theory* (11 Dec 2023), section "In the End It’s All Just the Ruliad" (https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2023/12/observer-theory/): "So is there a way to translate from one observer to another? Essentially that’s a story of motion. … If the ruliad is “too wild” (think: spacetime near a singularity) then the observer will inevitably be “shredded” as it “moves”. … to achieve “pure motion” the observer still has to be “made of” something that is somehow robust—essentially some “lump of computational reducibility” that can “predictably survive” the underlying background of computational irreducibility." And: "concepts (as represented for example by words) are the analog of particles in rulial space: they are the robust structures that can move across rulial space and “maintain their identity”".
- *On the Nature of Time* (Oct 2024), section "Time in the Ruliad": "we can effectively “move” from one place to another in the ruliad by translating the computations done by one set of rules to computations done by another. (And, yes, it’s nontrivial to even have the possibility of “pure motion”.)"
- **Strongest "costly" wording:** *What Ultimately Is There? Metaphysics and the Ruliad* (4 Feb 2026), section "The Question of Objective Reality" (https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2026/02/what-ultimately-is-there-metaphysics-and-the-ruliad/): "The difficulty of translation depends, though, on how far one is trying to go in the ruliad. Human minds exposed to similar knowledge, culture, etc. are nearby and fairly easy to translate between. Animal minds are further away, and more difficult to translate to. … Translation between places in the ruliad is in a sense just a generalization of translation in physical space. And the process of moving in physical space is what we describe as motion." Also: "potentially one can think of particles—like electrons or photons—as being in a sense “elementary carriers of pure motion”: minimal objects that can move without changing."

## 5. Second Law essay: GR, QM and the Second Law from irreducibility + bounded, persistent observers

**Verdict: VERIFIED, with a nuance.**

*Computational Foundations for the Second Law of Thermodynamics* (3 Feb 2023) (https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2023/02/computational-foundations-for-the-second-law-of-thermodynamics/):

- Section "The Mystery of the Second Law": "And the remarkable thing is that it now seems as if all three of general relativity, quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics are actually derivable, and from the same ultimate foundation: the interplay between computational irreducibility and the computational boundedness of observers like us."
- Section "Why the Second Law Works" adds persistence for spacetime: "when we’re thinking about spacetime, the observer is necessarily embedded within it. And it turns out that there’s then one additional feature of observers like us that’s important. Beyond the statement that we’re computationally bounded, it’s also important that we assume that we’re persistent in time. Yes, we’re made of different atoms of space at different moments. But somehow we assume that we have a coherent thread of experience. And this is crucial in deriving our familiar laws of physics."
- Section "The Second Law and Order in the Universe", relevant to the essay: "There’s an extreme version of this in our Physics Project, where the underlying structure of space … is full of computational irreducibility, but where there are certain overall features that observers like us notice, and that show computational reducibility. One example involves the large-scale structure of spacetime, as described by general relativity. Another involves the identification of particles that can be considered to “move without change” through the system."

The clean two-feature statement is in *On the Concept of Motion* (2022), "Motion Can Be a Complicated Story": "A key finding of our Physics Project is that certain basic laws of physics—in particular general relativity and quantum mechanics—inevitably seem to emerge as soon as we assume that observers have two basic characteristics: first, that they are computationally bounded, and second, that they are persistent in time."

**Precise phrasing for the essay:** all three theories derive from irreducibility plus bounded observers. Persistence is the extra assumption Wolfram needs for the two where the observer is embedded (GR, QM).

## 6. Particles: persistent structures, topological obstructions, open status

**Verdict: VERIFIED.**

**Non-planarity as the model particle:**
- Technical Introduction §8.9 "Elementary Particles" (https://www.wolframphysics.org/technical-introduction/potential-relation-to-physics/elementary-particles/): "Elementary particles are entities that—at least for some period—preserve their identity through space and time. In the context of our models, one can imagine that particles would correspond to structures in the hypergraph that are locally stable under the application of rules." Then: "consider rules that operate on an ordinary graph, and have the property of preserving planarity. Such rules can never remove non‐planarity … If one inserts such subgraphs into an otherwise planar graph, they behave very much as “particle‐like” structures. They can move around, but unless they meet and annihilate, they are preserved". And: "There are presumably analogs of this in hypergraph‐rewriting rules of the kind that appear in our models. … Existing results in graph theory do not go very far in elucidating the details."
- 2020 announcement, "Elementary Particles—Old and New": "The “core feature” of each particle will be some kind of locally stable structure in the hypergraph (a simple analogy might be that it’s a lump of nonplanarity in an otherwise planar graph)."
- Gorard 2020 (§2.4, via Kuratowski's theorem): "if the update rules for spatial graphs have the property that they always preserve planarity, then this immediately implies that each such nonplanar tangle will effectively act like an independent persistent structure that can only disappear through some kind of “annihilation” process with a different nonplanar tangle." Test particles are glossed as "a persistent nonplanar tangle embedded within a spatial hypergraph", and are *assumed* to follow geodesics in the continuum limit.
- *One-Year Update* (14 Apr 2021), section "Can We Finally Understand Quantum Mechanics?" (https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2021/04/the-wolfram-physics-project-a-one-year-update/): "we imagine that there can be localized pieces that have a certain stability that allows them to “move largely unchanged through space” (even as “space itself” is continually getting remade). And these correspond to particles. … we can view particles in our models as some kind of “topological obstructions” that prevent features of the hypergraph from “readily unraveling”."

**Concrete exhibits are toys only:**
- Technical Introduction §8.9 shows the rule {{x, y}, {y, z, u, v}} → {{x, y, z, u}, {u, v}}, giving "a circular “background”, with a localized “particle‐like” deformation", with a causal graph showing "a particle‐like structure on a simple grid‐like background". It adds: "in most of our models the “background” tends to be much more complicated, and so such direct methods for identifying particles cannot be used."
- *On the Concept of Motion* shows a rule that "maintains a very simple (effectively 1D and cyclic) form of space" in which a "particle" "can “move around”, but can’t be destroyed".
- I found no rule with a 3D (or any d > 1) limiting space exhibiting a persistent, freely propagating structure. I also found none with a family of such structures at different velocities, i.e. boosted twins.

**Wolfram's statements that the question is open:**
- 2022, *On the Concept of Motion*: "We know less about the specifics of how particles work." Also: "It’s fairly clear roughly how such particles must work, being based for example on essentially topological features of the system. But we don’t yet know the details, and there’s probably quite a depth of mathematical formalism that needs to be built to clarify them." And: "to determine this is a formal question that’s important to explore as our Physics Project progresses."
- 2024, *Five Most Productive Years: What Happened and What’s Next* (Aug 2024) (https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2024/08/five-most-productive-years-what-happened-and-whats-next/): "Another direction has to do with particles—like electrons. … there’s in a sense a much lower level version of the Feynman diagrams … where we can discuss the “effect of particles” without ever having to say exactly what a particle “is”. … My guess is that in our model particles are structures a bit like black holes—but we may be able to go a very long way without having to know the details."
- 2021, One-Year Update, section "Towards Experimental Implications": "In the current universe there could be a signature of momentum discreteness in “maximum boosts” for sufficiently light particles." This bears directly on boosted twins: Wolfram himself expects boosts to be bounded.

- *What Can We Learn about Engineering and Innovation from Half a Century of the Game of Life Cellular Automaton?* (Mar 2025) discusses spaceship speeds (including 31/240), not physics.

**Caution on the essay's Life example.** "Gliders move only at a few discrete speeds" is literally true of gliders: one speed, c/4 diagonal. But Life *spaceships* are richer. LifeWiki and the conwaylife.com forum thread "Caterloopillar WIP (all speeds < c/4)" say Michael Simkin's caterloopillar (first completed 9 Apr 2016, at c/8) "can be trivially adapted to produce spaceships with … in theory, any rational speed strictly less than c/4". So orthogonal speeds are dense below c/4, via enormous engineered machines. The ceiling comes from glider-based signalling. I took this from search snippets; the LifeWiki page returned 403, so re-check before quoting.

What survives, and arguably sharpens the point: dense speeds exist only by construction, at sizes growing with the speed's denominator. There is no natural family, and no transformation carries one spaceship into a "boosted" twin. An elementary fact (mine, not a citation): a periodic localized structure in a CA with period p and displacement d has velocity d/p. So any CA particle species has a rational velocity, and density requires unbounded period, hence unbounded size.

**Graph-theoretic theorem for dense-velocity localized structures:** none found. Wolfram's own assessment (Technical Introduction §8.9): "Existing results in graph theory do not go very far in elucidating the details."

**Causal-set and QCA precedents (all verified on arXiv):**
- Bombelli, Henson, Sorkin, "Discreteness without symmetry breaking: a theorem", gr-qc/0605006, Mod. Phys. Lett. A24 (2009) 2579. Abstract: "there exists no equivariant measurable map from sprinklings to spacetime directions … Another consequence is that there is no way to associate a finite-valency graph to a sprinkling consistently with Lorentz invariance."
  - This is a pointed precedent. Wolfram-model hypergraphs and causal graphs are finite-valency: each event has boundedly many inputs and outputs, which is my inference from the rule format, not a quoted statement.
  - Gorard, "Algorithmic Causal Sets and the Wolfram Model" (2011.12174, Nov 2020), claims that causal invariance gives "conformal invariance of the induced causal partial order, in a manner that is provably compatible with the measure-theoretic arguments of Bombelli, Henson and Sorkin".
- Dowker, Henson, Sorkin, "Quantum Gravity Phenomenology, Lorentz Invariance and Discreteness", gr-qc/0311055, Mod. Phys. Lett. A19 (2004) 1829. A "phenomenological model of massive particles propagating in a Minkowski spacetime which arises from an underlying causal set. The particles undergo a Lorentz invariant diffusion in phase space". These are "swerves": a Lorentz-invariant discrete substrate in which **the first law holds only on average**. This is the closest precedent for "earning the first law".
- Dowker, Philpott, Sorkin, "Energy-momentum diffusion from spacetime discreteness", 0810.5591, Phys. Rev. D79 (2009) 124047. Philpott, "Particle simulations in causal set theory", 0911.5595, CQG 27 (2010) 042001.
- Kaloper, Mattingly, "Low energy bounds on Poincaré violation in causal set theory", astro-ph/0607485, Phys. Rev. D74 (2006) 106001. Relic neutrinos "constrain the momentum space diffusion constant by k < 10⁻⁶¹ GeV³". These are experimental bounds on a discrete model's departure from inertia.
- **Fresh:** Albertini, Nasiri, Panella, "Stochastic dark matter: Covariant Brownian motion from Planckian discreteness", 2409.02188, Phys. Rev. D 111 (2025) 023514. Builds on swerves; covariant Brownian motion is unique under minimal coupling; constrained by the matter power spectrum.
- Johnston, "Particle propagators on discrete spacetime", 0806.3083, CQG 25 (2008) 202001. A sum over trajectories in a sprinkled causal set reproduces the retarded Klein–Gordon propagator in 1+1 and 3+1 dimensions. Also Johnston, "Feynman Propagator for a Free Scalar Field on a Causal Set", 0909.0944, PRL 103 (2009) 180401.
- Sorkin, "Does Locality Fail at Intermediate Length-Scales", gr-qc/0703099. Aslanbeigi, Saravani, Sorkin, "Generalized Causal Set d'Alembertians", 1403.1622, JHEP 1406 (2014) 024. Abstract: "generalized d'Alembertian operators … which are manifestly Lorentz-invariant, retarded, and non-local". It also finds "evidence that the original 4D causal set d'Alembertian is unstable, while its 2D counterpart is stable."

## 8. Gorard (2020), "Some Relativistic and Gravitational Properties of the Wolfram Model"

**Citation:** J. Gorard, *Complex Systems* 29(2), 2020, pp. 599–654, https://doi.org/10.25088/ComplexSystems.29.2.599; arXiv 2004.14810.

**Verdict: VERIFIED.** It does derive (a) discrete Lorentz covariance from causal invariance and (b) discrete Einstein equations from causal invariance plus a limiting-dimension assumption. The full assumption set is longer.

From the abstract: "we prove that causal invariance (namely, the requirement that all causal graphs be isomorphic, irrespective of the choice of hypergraph updating order) is equivalent to a discrete version of general covariance, with changes to the updating order corresponding to discrete gauge transformations. This fact then allows one to deduce a discrete analog of Lorentz covariance … subsequently using this fact (along with the assumption that the updating rules preserve the dimensionality of the causal graph in limiting cases) to prove that the most general set of constraints on the discrete spacetime Ricci tensor corresponds to a discrete form of the Einstein field equations."

**Assumptions the derivation uses** (quoted from the body):

1. **Causal invariance**, defined as isomorphism of all causal graphs regardless of updating order.
2. **Global discrete hyperbolicity:** "We proceed on the assumption that our spacetime is globally discretely hyperbolic, in the sense that there exists some universal “time function”".
3. **Flat hypersurfaces for the Lorentz step:** "In the particular case in which the discrete hypersurfaces contained within the foliation are required to be “flat” … those hypersurfaces will thus correspond to inertial reference frames, and the statement of causal invariance reduces to a statement of discrete Lorentz covariance."
4. **Constancy of c put in by definition:** "Since the other fundamental postulate of special relativity - namely constancy of the speed of light - is enforced axiomatically by our definition of the edge lengths in causal graphs, this completes the proof."
5. **Edge scale:** "one must presumably assume that every edge of the causal graph corresponds to a spacetime interval on the order of (at most) the Planck scale". This is a physical-interpretation assumption.
6. **Asymptotic dimensionality preservation** (§3.3 "The Discrete Einstein Field Equations"): "we shall assume one further condition on the hypergraph update rules, beyond mere causal invariance: namely, “asymptotic dimensionality preservation”. Loosely speaking, this means that the dimensionality of the causal graph show[s] converge to some fixed, finite value as the number of updating events grows arbitrarily large."
7. **Weak ergodicity**, in the continuum limit: "(subject to a weak ergodicity assumption on the dynamics of the causal graph)".
8. **Existence of a continuum limit:** "Thus, for any set of hypergraph updating rules that are both causal-invariant and asymptotically dimensionality preserving, then if a continuum limit exists in which the causal graph becomes a Riemannian manifold, that limiting manifold must satisfy the vacuum Einstein field equations".
   - Note: the text says "Riemannian", not Lorentzian.
   - Note: the result is for **vacuum**. Matter enters by identifying T^{μν} with "the number of edges bound within nonplanar tangles … which pass through a given discrete hypersurface".
9. **Hypergraph metric idealizations:** unit edge lengths, and "We also assume that all pairs of hyperedges which are not parallel … can be considered to be orthonormal."

**Points relevant to the essay:**

- The Lorentz-covariance argument is about **re-foliating one fixed causal graph** (the BA→AB grid example, observers at v = 0.05 … 0.95). This is passive invariance, exactly as the essay says.
- "Relativistic mass increase" is likewise treated passively: "elementary particles will appear to be “heavier” … on steeper “slices” in the foliation of the causal graph, without changing the eventual outcomes of the updating events."
- Geodesic motion of test particles is *asserted* for "a persistent nonplanar tangle", not derived from a rule.
- The paper lists open problems including "determining the computability-theoretic and complexity-theoretic properties that distinguish inertial and non-inertial reference frames".
