## The final editorial pass and the check of quotations (24 Nov 2026)

The author finished the read-through on 24 Nov ("It's all good"), having read the additions made from the newly supplied parts of the book only in outline and in Claude's summary. So the passages built on chapters 5 and 15 and section 7.3, and the section "By reasoning alone", remain Claude's wording that the author has not read closely.

**Edits in the pass.** "The earlier essay" is named each time (*Phenomenal Intuition*). The dust man, who was not introduced in this essay, is replaced by a gloss of a lookup table. Two asides ("the essay should say so", "the essay should say how weakly") are cut. A dangling "Once that is granted" is repaired. The two paragraphs on Alter's exact argument moved to the end of the zombie section, so that the dilemma and its answer are together and the section leads into "Possible and conceivable". The opening quotes "a very strong intuition" (0:28) and names experienced Mary, with Alter's credit to Stoljar (0:21; he also calls it a variant of Nida-Rümelin's Marianna, which the essay does not mention). The closing sentence is scoped: "nothing in the world is necessary without a ground".

**Quotations from the earlier essays.** All 34 were checked by script against the published sources and match. One was extended to the end of its sentence ("We agree, and deny that this shows a gap in the world"). "Their material may vary…" is from *Can Two Minds Share One Experience?* and is now attributed to it.

**Chalmers's papers, fetched in full.** Exact: the sentence about Mary and other people (two-dimensional argument, section 11); "If I cannot know that P…", the definition of ideal conceivability, and "in such a roundabout way" (conceivability paper, sections 1 and 6); "Zombies do not share our epistemic situation" (premise 5 in "Phenomenal Concepts and the Explanatory Gap"); the four phrases from Chalmers and Jackson, and their report of Block and Stalnaker. That the intensions of phenomenal terms coincide: "there is a strong case that the primary and secondary intensions of phenomenal terms coincide" (section 3), and he notes the assumption "is not necessary" for his argument. His own definitions of the types speak of an epistemic gap: "According to type-A materialism, there is no epistemic gap between physical and phenomenal truths; or at least, any apparent epistemic gap is easily closed"; "According to type-B materialism, there is an epistemic gap between the physical and phenomenal domains, but there is no ontological gap." The essay now follows that wording. His charge against type B is now quoted and not paraphrased: "the type-B materialist recognizes a principle that has the epistemic status of a fundamental law, but gives it the ontological status of an identity." His word is "epistemically primitive", not "brute".
