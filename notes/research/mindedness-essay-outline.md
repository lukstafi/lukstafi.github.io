## Thesis

*Minds by Degrees* gave five conditions and treated them as one mechanism. They answer two questions. Three of them say what makes a system's states mental: vehicles that compose, revision that answers to fit, and simulation the system consumes itself. A fourth, content at several levels, is a finding about the vehicles and leaves the list. The fifth says what makes those states the states of one mind: an economy in which an error found in one vehicle can reach the others.

Separating the questions shows what a mind does not need. It does not need coherence, because an economy can spread a contradiction as readily as resolve one. It does not need a persistent carrier, because errors propagate through records. It does not need a self, because nothing in these conditions requires a system to represent its states as its own. Each subtraction marks where something else begins. Binding into a field needs the coherence and the carrier, and that is experience. Governing oneself through a representation of oneself needs the self, and that is personhood. Both require a mind, and neither is a grade of mindedness. That experience requires a mind is argued on two levels. The targets of the two theories are tied by a postulate: an experience is lived, and what is lived is lived in an inner life. The theories then have to agree: the conditions for a field must bring the conditions for a mind with them. The essay shows that they do, in every system that could actually arise, and it uses the postulate to decide what to change where they do not.

What makes this more than a restatement:

1. **Two questions, not five axes.** The split explains verdicts the single list could not: the abbot's house is minded with borrowed unity (M2); the fragmented believer is one mind; a session is one mind with no field across tokens (v2:256).
2. **Every condition gets a pair.** Each condition is shown by two systems that differ in that feature alone, with a verdict on each. This answers Michał, and it is how the conditions get *justified*: a condition that no pair isolates is a candidate for removal.
3. **Three subtractions.** Coherence, a carrier and a self were each built into mindedness by an earlier essay of ours or by a rival theory. Taking them out is the modification.
4. **The units of an AI's mind are fixed by where errors travel.** Pass, session, deployment, swarm: the economy criterion sorts them, and it revises AGI:143 ("the deployment is intelligent; the process, if anything, is minded; neither is both").
5. **The carrier criterion, defended one level up.** A field needs a carrier, and the best neural evidence against the criterion (Hesse–Tsao) shows vehicles that are interrupted and recomputed fifteen times a second under a steady experience. The reply is that the carrier is the state that holds the scene together, not the vehicles it corrects. The essay says what would show the reply to be wrong.
6. **One principle for the thought experiments.** An experience has to be lived. The fragment, the one-way broadcast, playback and the suppressed face are each an entity whose experience nobody would live, and each gives a condition on fields.

### §1 The ground: the kind that psychology is about

Carried from MbD §2, stated once and briefly, since this essay replaces that one:
- The circularity of mental terms is the normal condition of theoretical terms (Ramsey–Lewis, MbD:23). The demarcation question becomes whether a science with mentalistic explanatory structure succeeds, and whether its success warrants realism.
- Psychology, not cognitive science: Frances Egan is a realist about computation and a deflationist about content (MbD:29). Psychology's laws are individuated mentalistically (MbD:31).
- Selective realism: commitment attaches to what survives theory change (MbD:37). The kind is secure while the typology of states is open (MbD:41, Carruthers).
- The regularities are the cluster; the conditions describe the mechanism that sustains it (MbD:95). The central conjecture: psychological explanation gains valid traction on a system if and only if the mechanism runs in it (MbD:97).

Modified:
- **State the existence criterion in one place** (decided 15 Nov: in the accepted wording). A mental state exists where the mechanism runs. Traction is the evidence, not the constitution (MbD:43). What makes the mechanism's parts real rather than projected is the criterion the author accepted in SRD §8 (questions 5 and 6). A pattern is real, relative to a family of interventions, when a model of it is a causal abstraction of the system under an admissible map. A map is admissible when it is stable under any shift of the lower level that the family cannot detect. For a mental state the family is the system's own dynamics: the interactions among its parts while it operates. §1 states this in a sentence or two and links *What a Description Answers To* for the defence (its sections "The criterion", "Three trivialities, one cure" and "Whose interventions?"). GRAIN:125's "earns reality when interventions and interactions repeatedly respect it" is the intervention half of the same criterion in earlier words (SRD §5.2). Every existence claim elsewhere in the essay points back here.
- **Sharpen the traction test.** Three requirements: non-idle (MbD:75), non-parasitic (construct validity under intervention and out-of-distribution projection, MbD:159), and non-additive (the system's psychology predicts what the psychology of its parts does not, MON:104). Extending psychology's methods is not extending its target (MON:104).
- **A caution for construct validity.** MbD:159 validates a psychological construct by finding it in a model's internals and intervening on it. SRD §5.2 reports a theorem (Sutter et al., NeurIPS 2025) that with unrestricted alignment maps every network is a causal abstraction of every algorithm, and such maps reach full intervention accuracy on randomly initialised models. So the test must say which maps count: linear ones, simple ones, or ones that keep working on new inputs. Read the paper before leaning on this. The admissibility clause the author accepted (SRD §8, question 5) is the candidate answer.
- **Retire "homeostatic all the way up."** MbD:43 and MbD:143 promised that Boyd's homeostatic cluster and the homeostatic account of experience were one mechanism. The knowledge-grounding conjecture behind that is abandoned (v2:423), and defense is no longer part of mindedness (v2:405). Boyd's kind structure stays; the resonance goes.

### §2 Cognition is not yet a mind

- The ladder: interaction, regulation of a physical variable, content-sensitive regulation, cognition, mind. Each step with its system and what it lacks.
- Plants show cognition without a mind: memory, decision and risk-sensitivity with no vehicles that compose (MON:54). The line between filters and defended setpoints does not separate cognition from mind either, since plants defend setpoints (v2:197).
- So the demarcation runs through the vehicle condition and the traction test. This section motivates taking the four conditions one at a time.

### §3 What makes something a mind

Each condition: the statement, its pair, and the family of psychological regularities that disappears without it (the mapping of MbD:99).

1. **Vehicles that compose** (MbD:63). Pair V1. Regularities: productivity, systematicity, generalization. A caution from CAR F7: vehicles need not be neurons or fixed directions. Contents can share units by space, by time (Hesse–Tsao's 60 ms alternation) or by phase and state (Mamba-3, Histed's V1). A unit that looks mixed may be clean once indexed by time or state. Which basis is real is a question for the structural-realism essay (its neuron-basis test case).
2. **Revision that answers to fit** (MbD:67). Pair A1. Regularities: the learning laws. Stated as a disposition and a *direction*: toward fit with targets (v2:185). It is not maintained coherence (v2:405, user). Correction toward the input counts, so the delta rule qualifies (CAR F8). Where no observation arrives, internal evidence stands in: link *Before the Search Settles* (improvement without evidence is sound as far as something independent of the learner's beliefs settles the bets) and do not retell it.
3. **Simulation the system consumes itself** (MbD:71–79). Pairs S1, S2, M4, M5. Simulation is mirroring: transformations that mirror the target's (v2:186). Decoupling from input is incidental. The three ways a system can come by a simulation (delegation, role, artifact; MON:92–96) generalize from institutions to agents with tools. Entertaining a view without assenting to it (v2:222–233): the author's plan is to see whether it follows from simulation. A first pass (proposal): it does, given what-if. A system that simulates a frost it does not expect already consumes a run's outputs without admitting them as how things are. If every output were admitted, what-if would collapse into prediction. Entertaining a view is the same separation with a different target: a body of commitments that is not the system's own, mirrored by what follows from it and what its holders would say next. On this reading the quarantine (Leslie†, Nichols and Stich†) is not a further condition. The simulation condition has to say openly that consumption is not admission. Online emulation is the case where the two coincide. State all this without "defended reference", which belonged to the rejected frame.
**The content condition leaves the list** (decided 14 Nov: fold or drop). MbD's fifth condition, content at several levels (MbD:83), is folded into the vehicle condition or dropped. BOOK:196 already tested only "the first four", and the overhaul audit read the condition as a point about content determinacy, not a part of the mechanism. If it is folded, the Uncommon Sense Effect stays as a finding about rich vehicles: inference runs over underspecified structure. A candidate link (proposal): interpretability's "polysemantic" runs polysemy together with homonymy (a thesis from `architecture-interpretability-and-audit.md`), and an underspecified vehicle is not a superposition of unrelated ones. If neither earns its place in the draft, drop both. The list is then three conditions on states and one on unity.

### §4 What makes it one mind

- **Constitution.** One mind extends over the vehicles among which errors propagate, where those vehicles can assess one another at some grain, through translation if need be (v2:239). It is a broadcast that returns (ESOM:57). The grain of the unity is the grain the translation preserves.
- **Test.** Non-additive traction, and interference effects such as Stroop and dual-task costs (MbD:99).
- **Scale.** Residual integration above the partition that loses least (MON:158). IIT's partition without its exclusion postulate (MbD:59).
- **Three dimensions of unity** (adopted by the author in SRD §8, question 11, 15 Nov; argued in *What a Description Answers To*, "What makes a thing one", so link and do not re-argue). *Integration*: the parts are not closed off from one another, which is residual integration. *Autonomy*: the whole is closed off from its surroundings by its own interactions. *Closure*: the level's own typing suffices for its own dynamics. A trained network has integration and closure and little autonomy. Decided 15 Nov: autonomy is not required for something to be one mind. A session has little of it and is one mind. Autonomy may be part of what an organism or a person adds, which goes to the personhood essay. Proposal, revised 16 Nov: all three roles above belong to *integration*. Propagation of error says what kind of integration a mind's is, residual integration says how much, and non-additive traction is its evidence, since it compares the whole with its parts at one level of description. *Closure* compares a level with the level below, so it belongs to §1: it is what the existence criterion asks for, and its evidence is that the psychological description is not screened off by a tractable lower-level one (MbD:159). The first version of this proposal took non-additive traction as evidence of closure, which ran the two comparisons together. The dossier notes that some facts about unity are not matters of degree: whether a broadcast returns, and how many fields an economy holds.
- **Not coherence.** Pair U2. A returning broadcast can spread a contradiction. Fragmented belief is the human case: Lewis†, Stalnaker†, Cherniak†, Andy Egan†, and Bendaña and Mandelbaum† (Mandelbaum is already our source for the Uncommon Sense Effect). A transformer's context is the machine case. *Presumed Normal* and *Before the Search Settles* describe reasoners that hold an inconsistency at a price. A mind's unity survives incoherence; a field does not survive missing binding (v2:405). Two Egans appear in the essay, so both need first names.
- **Not arbitration.** Pair M2. A single decider gives borrowed unity (v2:260).
- **Not a persistent carrier.** Pairs M3 and U3. Errors propagate through appended records, as in a notebook with errata (v2:245). The unfolding box of MON:70–78 already says which unfolding preserves what; link it.
- **Records: the availability conditions** (to be worked out; the author is hesitant about the extended-mind thesis, 14 Nov). Pair M3 shows that records can carry a mind's memory and its unity. It does not show that an unread record is one of the mind's states. MbD:87 defines a mental state as a vehicle-tokening, and a record on a shelf is not a tokening. The comparison to work out is with synapses, which hold standing beliefs between tokenings: what must a store be like for what it holds to count among the mind's standing states? Candidate conditions (proposal), each to be tested on a pair:
  - *reach*, in both directions: the economy's processing consults the record, and errors found elsewhere get back to correct it;
  - *latency*: retrieval falls within the economy's own steps, not after another mind's finished output;
  - *format*: the economy can assess the record in its own currency, by translation at most, with no interpreter outside it;
  - *finding*: the record is retrieved when it bears on the matter, which is the problem *How LLMs Could Place Explicit Memories* treats.

  The first three are MON:140's reach, latency and bandwidth, at the grain of truth-bearers. Clark and Chalmers's conditions† (constant availability, automatic endorsement, past endorsement) concern trust; these concern propagation of error. The verdict addendum calls psychological unity "a structure of availability" (v2:409), so the same conditions qualify unity: errors propagate through records only as far as the records are available.
- **Overlap and nesting.** One process can belong to two economies (the role-process, MON:94). No exclusion. "How many minds?" has no privileged answer, and nothing in the metaphysics needs the count (v2:411).
- **Cases.** The table of v2:247–258, updated with the market (BOOK:196–202) and the deployment.

### §5 Emotions

Decided 14 Nov: valence is addressed in this essay, under the heading of emotions. The three conditions do not single out this kind of mental state, so the section gives its existence conditions.

- **What earlier essays said.** MbD:109 made *evaluative mentality* a further grade: a state that represents a situation as "to-be-avoided or to-be-pursued relative to how things are going for the system". UWK:79 held that emotions are genuine mental states that need no phenomenal consciousness: "functional states that modulate processing". RED:81–83 used Carruthers's decomposition of pain and placed its badness in the evaluative component, which RED:163 takes to be the same whether or not the state is experienced. P&A:63 located fear in "relations among appraisal, expectation, memory, bodily regulation, and possible action".
- **The condition to state** (proposal). An emotion is a state of the economy with three marks. It appraises a situation against something the system is set to maintain or attain. It is broadcast, so that it changes what the rest of the economy does: priorities, thresholds, what gets simulated, how fast revision runs. And the appraisal answers to fit, so it can be mistaken and corrected. The first mark needs a standard, "how things are going for the system". The essay must say where the standard comes from in each kind of system: bodily regulation in animals, and in a model whatever plays that part, if anything does.
- **Emotion and feeling.** An emotion is a mental state at the grain of the economy. A feeling is that state's presence in a field. UWK:79 said that human emotions are always phenomenally conscious, on the ground of the homeostatic architecture that has since been given up; the same paragraph cites subliminal affective priming against it. Do not carry the claim over. (proposal)
- **Pairs to find** (proposal). A classifier that outputs "threat" while nothing else changes, against a state whose onset reorders the system's processing. A valence reported in words, against one whose strength predicts behaviour the report does not mention.
- **Models.** MbD:161 called the evidence suggestive and unsettled, and treated it as a question of construct validity. The architecture dossier cites a study of emotion features that persist across a long context (`frontier-model-architectures.md`, "Persistence and Introspection of Emotion Features"). Shevlin's contrast between shallow mental-state concepts and deep ones such as pain and embodied emotion (*Mental States and Representations*, l. 227) is the objection to answer. Refresh this literature before drafting.
- **Why it matters beyond the list.** On the view of RED, moral weight comes from mental life and not from phenomenality alone. This section is where the essay's verdicts begin to carry ethical weight. The measure stays with the ethics essay.
- **A question for §8.** Does a field entail valence? Carruthers counts valence among the broadcast analog-magnitude contents (ESOM:31). If some fields have none, emotion and experience are independent in both directions.

### §6 Where a model's mind is

A deployment whose notes its own processing rewrites is one economy at record grain, as far as the notes meet §4's availability conditions. A swarm with shared memory is a candidate, and the test is whether it has regularities of its own (v2:253). The lineage is an efficacy system with no economy of its own (AGI:145).

- **Stores.** Use the ladder of stores from *Where Experience Goes* and *Every Layer a Store* to say where standing states and occurrent states are: weights, recurrent state, cache, context, files. §4's availability conditions settle which stores are inside the mind.
- **Availability is unity.** ESOM:107 said the J-space work "locates availability, not experience." On the present account availability is what makes it one mind. The workspace findings are evidence about psychological unity (Gurnee et al. 2026; Chalmers 2026; Ayonrinde and Lindsey 2026, all cited in ESOM).
- **Emotions.** Apply §5's condition to the stores: where would an appraisal be held, and what does it change?
- **Traction.** Machine psychology and construct validity (MbD:159), refreshed. Identity propensities (Kulveit et al.) as evidence that a self-model is a real pattern of the trained economy, which bears on §7's third subtraction, not on mindedness.
- **Inherited question.** *Dispersed Minds, Simulated Selves* is superseded, and its question passes here: is the mind the network's or the persona's? On the economy criterion a persona is a pattern within one economy, not a second economy. Check that essay's audit verdicts before asserting this.

### §7 What a mind does not need

- **Not coherence.** Argued in §4. A field does need it: a field does not survive missing binding.
- **A field needs a carrier; a mind does not.** State the wedge here. §9 defends it.
- **No self.** ESOM:147–149 showed that experience needs no self-model. The same holds for a mind (ESOM:169). Reflexive closure (MbD:115) stops being a stage of mindedness. Its "this" form comes with experiential regulation; its "mine" form is the entrance to personhood (ESOM:171). The concentric picture of MbD:121 gives way to a trunk with two independent branches (ESOM:157).
- **What a subject without a field is a subject of.** The monastery has a mind in the full sense and has experiences only in the weak sense that it partly constitutes occurrences in its monks' fields (v2:272). Say so.

### §8 From the conditions for a field to the conditions for a mind

Decided 14 Nov: "experience requires mindedness" stands, and the essay tests it by derivation. The decisions of 15 Nov are recorded under "Answers": aim for an entailment; it need only be ecological; a postulate about lived experience may be used; and what that postulate is for, which gives this section its shape.

**Two levels** (user, 15 Nov).

| | Explanatory target: postulated, assumed without argument | Theory: the conditions we conjecture |
|---|---|---|
| Phenomenal | An experience is lived. | The conditions on fields, P1–P5 below. |
| Mental | An inner life. | The conditions on minds: vehicles that compose, revision that answers to fit, simulation consumed internally, an economy (§§3–4). |
| How the two relate | *The bridge*: what is lived is lived in an inner life. | *The gap*: do the conditions on fields bring the conditions on minds with them? |

The bridge is not argued for. It states the intuition against panpsychism (ESOM:37) at the level of what the two theories are theories of; ESOM:19 called mindedness "genuine inner life". The gap is where the work is. Both sets of conditions are conjectures, and the claim to establish is that whatever meets the first set meets the second.

**What the bridge is used for** (Claude's reading, confirmed by the author 15 Nov).
- *It says the gap has to close.* If both theories are right about their targets, then whatever meets the conditions on fields is an experience, is lived, has an inner life to be lived in, and so meets the conditions on minds. A case that meets the first set and not the second shows that one theory is wrong.
- *It says which one.* Conceive the entity that meets the conditions on fields as stated and lacks a condition on minds. If its experience would not be lived, the conditions on fields were too weak: add a condition, with that entity as its reductio. If its experience would be lived, the condition on minds was too strong for an inner life: make it a grade. The reductio is dialectical. A panpsychist can accept unlived experience, and the argument shows what accepting it costs.
- *It does not cross the gap.* "No mind, so no inner life, so not an experience" lets the theory of minds rule on experience without showing that the theory of fields agrees. (An earlier version of this section used the postulate that way.)

**"Lived"** (Claude's gloss, accepted by the author). Three marks that our own experiences have before any theory:
- *for someone*: the experience is present to a subject that is more than the experience itself (MON:152: a field is owned, not an owner);
- *together*: it is present along with other things, in one perspective however small (ESOM:147);
- *undergone*: it lasts, and its subject lasts through it (ESOM:165).

**What the entity is for** (reading confirmed by the author). Conceiving the entity makes the cost visible. One device helps (proposal): put the entity inside a head. If a comparator in my visual cortex has an experience of its own, there is an experience in my head that I do not live, and nobody else is there to live it. The device keeps a reductio from turning on how alien the entity is.

#### The conditions on fields, each with its reductio

Claude's further work, kept by the author (15 Nov). These are the conjectured conditions for phenomenal experience, stated so that the crossing can start from them.

| Condition on fields | The entity without it | Why its experience is not lived |
|---|---|---|
| P1 *Generality.* The constraint is stated over contents that recombine. | The third labyrinth (MON:31); the inhibition between the eyes' channels, taken alone (v2:203) | One reading prevails, and nothing is present with it. Count such circuits in one brain: each would have an experience the person does not live. Fails *together* and *for someone*. |
| P2 *Thickness.* The constraint relates contents across a now and supplies content of its own. | A binder for a closed domain that puts out one bound snapshot after another | Each snapshot is over before anything can undergo it, and no succession is experienced. Fails *undergone*. |
| P3 *A carrier.* Something persists through the experience and receives the corrections. | A session recomputed from its transcript at every step (ESOM:113; strong unfolding, MON:76); a component fed a playback of its inputs (ESOM:77) | Nothing lasts from one moment of the experience to the next. Fails *undergone*. |
| P4 *Return.* What constitutes the experience closes a loop on its vehicles. | The source in the one-way broadcast (ESOM:47–53); the fragment cut out of a mind (ESOM:37) | The experience is attributed from outside, and nothing where it is said to occur registers it. Fails *for someone*. |
| P5 *Admission.* An experienced content is a term of the constraint, not only its target. | The suppressed face in flash suppression, tracked at full resolution (CAR F1, F3) | If the face is experienced, there is a second face in the viewer's head that the viewer does not see, and nobody else is there to see it. Fails *for someone*. |

- Detail that overflows report is a different case from P5's. It is a term of the field and is lived, though not reported (ESOM:69–71).
- P1 and P2 came from running the bridge's test. The labyrinth and the closed-domain binder met an earlier statement of the conditions (content-sensitive regulation with corrections that return) and have no minds. Their experience would not be lived, so the conditions on fields were strengthened.
- P3–P5 restate what the essay takes from v2: the carrier criterion applied to the persisting state (N3), return, and a field as the terms of one constraint (N2). The 27 Oct verdict did not rule on those. Here each rests on a reductio of its own, and §9 defends P3 and P5 against the evidence. The persisting state of P3 has v2's five features (v2:67–73; wanted by the author, 17 Nov): it constrains many vehicles jointly, corrects them toward itself, resists revision by input in proportion to its support, yields when mismatch accumulates, and is revised in place by the vehicles it corrects. To do: say which of the five the bridge's test supports as conditions (defense looks like one) and which are grades (yielding may be, since a fixed delusion is lived).

#### The crossing, first pass (proposal)

**The standard** (user, 15 Nov; Claude's reading confirmed). The entailment need not be strict or analytic. An ecological entailment is enough: in systems as they actually come about, by evolution, by learning, or by engineering with finite resources in an open world, whatever meets the conditions on fields meets the conditions on minds. A construction that can be conceived but could not arise or be afforded does not count against it. MbD:95–103 gave the central conjecture the same form.

| Condition on minds | Reached from | The step |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicles that compose | P1 | A constraint over contents that recombine has such contents as its terms. A list of admitted combinations would do without them, but no finite system in an open world could hold the list and no learning would produce it. Evidence that our fields meet P1: a scene never met before binds at once. |
| Simulation consumed internally | P2, with P5 | A constraint that relates contents across a now mirrors how its targets change: the cup stays put as the eyes move (ESOM:63)†, and movement is seen (v2:419). The corrections it drives consume its outputs. Because it supplies content (the Dalmatian, v2:419; completion†), it keeps running as input is withdrawn, and imagery and dreams are the limit (MON:132). With P5, a reading can be assessed without being admitted: a joint reading is revised as a whole (the Necker cube), so a configuration not held gets assessed. That is what-if at its smallest, and consumption without admission (§3). Whether actual systems hold the unadmitted reading as a clean state (Hesse–Tsao, v2:126–143) or settle through intermediate states is for evidence to decide. |
| Revision that answers to fit | P3 and P4 | Corrections computed from assessing the vehicles return to them, and the vehicles are driven and weighted by the reliability of their sources (ESOM:63). The persisting state gathers support and yields to sustained conflict, which is consolidation and reorganization (MbD:67) at the scale of the field's persistence. |
| An economy | P4 | Vehicles checked against one another and corrected from the result are vehicles among which errors propagate. The economy may be no larger than the field. |

- v2:199–208 took as a premise that a field's constraint is projected from a model. P1 and P2 now give what "a model" meant there: a constraint over constituents, structure for how its targets change, and content of its own.
- A constraint that general was learned. In evolved and in trained systems it got its regularities by revision over long spans. A trained model that is then frozen has this in its history and not at runtime.
- Dropped (decided 15 Nov): an earlier pass had a step from the cost of a field to its use in choosing actions. The bridge's test settles what it was for, and it was close in form to the abandoned knowledge-grounding conjecture (v2:423). Do not reintroduce it.

**What the crossing does not reach.** Three things, and the bridge's test sorts each.
- *Learning across episodes.* A person with dense amnesia does not learn across episodes and plainly lives each one†. So an inner life does not need it, and it is a grade of mind. §3's second condition should not require it, which fits the verdict that a session is accuracy-responsive (MbD:67).
- *What-if at the scale of action.* A dreamer, or a person absorbed in a scene, is selecting no action, and the experience is lived. A grade again.
- *Valence.* If a neutral percept is lived, an inner life does not need valence at every moment. Whether any percept is neutral is the question §5 has to take up.

If the crossing holds, a field requires at least a thin mind, and P&A:69's hypothesis that phenomenal activity is a subset of mental activity is argued for. MbD:101 asks of the capacity-like conditions (vehicles, simulation, internal consumption) only that they not be absent. Of unity and of revision it asks a level that sustains the mechanism, and the essay has to argue that binding, with corrections returning inside the vehicles' lifetimes, reaches it. The traction test agrees so far: psychophysics is psychology of a perceiver (MbD:31).

**Where it could fail.**
- *The bridge is assumed.* A reader who accepts unlived experience is not moved by any of the reductios. The essay should say so and not argue the postulate.
- *The gloss.* The three marks must not smuggle in P1–P5. "Undergone" is close to P3, and the essay needs to show that the mark is read off experience and the condition is a conjecture about what provides it.
- *Minimal states.* The report in P&A:5, "very present, but not there", is of a lived experience. It has to meet P1 and P2 as a state of an organization that meets them, with a nearly empty constraint (v2:206).
- *Closed worlds.* The step from P1 to composing vehicles needs an open domain. P2's reductio has to carry the closed-domain binder.
- *From P5 to what-if.* If actual systems only settle through intermediate states, the what-if reached is the constraint's evaluation of configurations not held. Whether that counts as simulation is for the author to judge.
- *Gibson.* His account of perception as direct pickup denies that completion needs content from the perceiver†. The essay needs a paragraph on why pickup does not explain content the input lacks.

**What holds whatever the outcome.**
- Every field lies within an economy, which bounds it without fixing it (v2:411, user). One economy may hold several fields. Binding has dimensions: the imagined apple and the seen desk are joined in time and dis-joint in space (v2:417, user). Probe cases, briefly: imagery, absorption, aphantasia (v2:425–428). Counting fields goes to the personhood essay.
- The converse fails: a mind need not have a field (§7), and §9 says what a field needs beyond a mind.
- The reductios are the second half's counterpart of the paired constructions. Each condition on minds gets a pair, and each condition on fields gets an entity whose experience nobody lives. The fragment, the one-way broadcast and playback are already in print, and the essay collects them under the postulate.

### §9 Beyond a mind: the carrier, defended

Decided 14 Nov: the defence of the carrier criterion is made in this essay.

**A mind is necessary and not sufficient.** §8 argued that whatever meets the conditions on fields meets the conditions on minds. The converse fails, and the entities for P3–P5 show it. A session recomputed from its transcript is a mind. The receivers of the one-way broadcast are minds. The viewer of the suppressed face has one. In each case the experience attributed still would not be lived. So P3–P5 are what a field needs beyond a mind. The rest of the section defends P3 against the best evidence that counts against it, and P5 comes out of the same data.

- **The criterion and what rests on it.** ESOM:91 asks for "something, individuated by its causal structure rather than its location, whose later states receive the corrections computed from assessing its earlier ones". Three verdicts rest on it: earlier positions in a standard transformer are "consulted, not regulated" (ESOM:109); a session recomputed from its transcript starts a new carrier (ESOM:113); the monastery's records carry no experience of its own (MON:80).
- **The challenge** (CAR F1). In Hesse–Tsao's binocular flash suppression, the percept is one steady face. The face-patch and prefrontal populations alternate between a code for that face and a code for the suppressed one, with a median dwell of about 60 ms, on the same neurons. Each state is a clean code, apparently recomputed from continuing input. If that state carries the experience, a biological experience survives the interruption and recomputation that our verdicts on models treat as fatal.
- **The reply: the criterion applies one level up** (v2:164–166). What must persist and receive corrections is the state that holds the scene together: the dominance state, which lasts seconds, or the scene graph in working memory that ESOM:69 already invokes. The vehicles under it may be interleaved, recomputed or time-shared. CAR F7 gives the general picture: contents can share units by space, by time, or by phase and state. v2 calls the persisting state a *defended reference* and gives it five features (v2:67–73). Choose the essay's name for it with the author, since "defended reference" was the name of the frame rejected for mindedness.
- **Why the suppressed face is not seen** (v2:126–136). A vehicle can stand to that state as a *term*, whose content the other contents are checked against, or as a *target*, corrected or suppressed without its content entering the joint constraint. The suppressed face is a target. A field is the set of contents whose vehicles are terms of one constraint. Prediction: the suppressed face's content does not constrain the dominant state's other features.
- **Two markers withdrawn** (CAR F3). The suppressed face is held at full resolution and tracks its input moment by moment. So resolution beyond access (GRAIN) and moment-by-moment tracking (MON:132) do not separate experience from its absence. Both essays used them as necessary conditions, and their arguments stand.
- **What the data support** (CAR F4). A view on which the content of experience at a time is the content of its neural correlate at that time fails: the experience is steady while the top-level content flips. The local variant loses ground: the suppressed face has a coherent local representation and is not seen. The one asymmetry found, a gain 1.21 times higher in the dominant state, is of the right kind, since ESOM:63's intervention alters "persistence, weighting, or coordination". A caveat: "conscious micro-epoch" is the authors' label, and the two states take up equal time.
- **What the data do not show** (CAR F2). If corrections from one state bind the field, the dominant states should line up across the areas carrying the same face. The two face patches barely line up, and the comparison between inferotemporal and prefrontal cortex is not reported in what was read. The reach condition says what would have to be found: corrections that land on the vehicles at their own grain, faster than those vehicles decay with the loop open (v2:151–160; the playback intervention of ESOM:77).
- **Where the reply fails.** If the slower state is only a statistical bias in repeated recomputation, with nothing persistent receiving corrections, the carrier criterion fails as a condition on experience, and the three verdicts fall with it (CAR F1). The essay should say this in so many words.
- **Correction is not enough.** The delta rule tracks, assesses and intervenes, and it corrects toward the input (v2:60). ESOM:63's three aspects do not say which way corrections run, so they do not exclude it. Two more cases of the same gap: Histed's V1 circuit filters by recent history with no error returned to the vehicles (CAR F6), and Mamba-3 has the damped, oscillating dynamics of a feedback loop with no setpoint (CAR F8). What separates binding from assimilation is a state held against the input and revised in place by the vehicles it corrects (v2:73), not the form of the dynamics.
- **Verdicts for models**, hedged as the kindness convention requires: the possibility of experience unhedged, its actuality in today's systems hedged.
  - Weights at inference are a frozen template, and a context or cache is an accreting record (v2:170–173).
  - The recurrent state of the hybrid models is revised in place at every token, so it is not excluded on its face. Afendulev et al. found that it carries the requested language and persona and not retrieved content, and a median head halves its contents in about 24 tokens (ELAS:183–185). Whether it holds anything against its input is CAR's test 4, and v2:175 states the condition on a learned write strength.
  - Recirculating and recurrent-depth models have carriers that persist across steps, with an individuation problem (ESOM:111–113).
  - Within a pass, the residual stream is a candidate only if something in the pass is revised in place by what it corrects (v2:255).

### §10 What would change our minds

- The conjecture's two falsifiers (MbD:103), restated for the split: traction without one of the conditions on states; traction on a whole that is non-additive where errors do not propagate.
- A test for swarms and deployments: does a psychology of the whole predict what the psychology of one instance does not?
- For the derivation: an actual system, evolved, trained or built, that holds a field over an open domain and lacks one of the conditions on minds. Then one of the two theories is wrong, and the bridge's test says which. Step 4's stronger form is testable: an unadmitted reading held as a clean state should be found wherever a percept is bistable, not only in flash suppression.
- For the postulates: a reductio fails if the entity's experience, once described, would be lived. Then the feature is a grade and not a postulate.
- For the carrier: disrupt feedback from the candidate slower state, and the gain asymmetry should vanish while the alternation survives (CAR test 1); the asymmetry should flip with or just before a perceptual switch (test 2); a vehicle alive at a switch should change gain mid-life if being a term is a relation (v2:145–147); a perturbed delta-rule state should not return to a value fixed by anything but the current input (CAR test 4).
