## Thesis

*Minds by Degrees* gave five conditions and treated them as one mechanism. They answer two questions. Three of them say what makes a system's states mental: vehicles that compose, revision that answers to fit, and simulation the system consumes itself. A fourth, content at several levels, is a finding about the vehicles and leaves the list. The fifth says what makes those states the states of one mind: an economy in which an error found in one vehicle can reach the others.

Separating the questions shows what a mind does not need. It does not need coherence, because an economy can spread a contradiction as readily as resolve one. It does not need a persistent carrier, because errors propagate through records. It does not need a self, because nothing in these conditions requires a system to represent its states as its own. Each subtraction marks where something else begins. Binding into a field needs the coherence and the carrier, and that is experience. Governing oneself through a representation of oneself needs the self, and that is personhood. Both require a mind, and neither is a grade of mindedness. That experience requires a mind is argued on two levels. The targets of the two theories are tied by a postulate: an experience is lived, and what is lived is lived in an inner life. The theories then have to agree: the conditions for a field must bring the conditions for a mind with them. The essay shows that they do, in every system that could actually arise, and it uses the postulate to decide what to change where they do not.

What makes this more than a restatement:

1. **Two questions, not five axes.** The split explains verdicts the single list could not: the abbot's house is minded with borrowed unity (M2); the fragmented believer is one mind; a session is one mind with no field across tokens (v2:256).
2. **Every condition gets a pair.** Each condition is shown by two systems that differ in that feature alone, with a verdict on each. This answers Michał, and it is how the conditions get *justified*: a condition that no pair isolates is a candidate for removal.
3. **Three subtractions.** Coherence, a carrier and a self were each built into mindedness by an earlier essay of ours or by a rival theory. Taking them out is the modification.
4. **The units of an AI's mind are fixed by where errors travel.** Pass, session, deployment, swarm: the economy criterion sorts them, and it revises AGI:143 ("the deployment is intelligent; the process, if anything, is minded; neither is both").
5. **The carrier criterion, under pressure.** A field needs a carrier, and the best neural evidence against the criterion (Hesse–Tsao) shows codes that are interrupted and reinstated fifteen times a second under a steady experience. The essay gives the finding in detail and three ways the criterion could still be met: by a slower state above those codes, by the seen face's code as one carrier sharing its neurons in time, or by representations at the intermediate level below them. It does not choose among them, and it says what follows if none holds.
6. **One principle for the thought experiments.** An experience has to be lived. The fragment, the one-way broadcast, playback and the suppressed face are each an entity whose experience nobody would live, and each gives a condition on fields.

### §1 The ground: the kind that psychology is about

Carried from MbD §2 in one compressed section, a paragraph for each move (decided 17 Nov). The essay replaces that one, so a link is not enough:
- The circularity of mental terms is the normal condition of theoretical terms (Ramsey–Lewis, MbD:23). The demarcation question becomes whether a science with mentalistic explanatory structure succeeds, and whether its success warrants realism.
- Psychology, not cognitive science: Frances Egan is a realist about computation and a deflationist about content (MbD:29). Psychology's laws are individuated mentalistically (MbD:31).
- Selective realism: commitment attaches to what survives theory change (MbD:37). The kind is secure while the typology of states is open (MbD:41, Carruthers).
- The regularities are the cluster; the conditions describe the mechanism that sustains it (MbD:95). The central conjecture: psychological explanation gains valid traction on a system if and only if the mechanism runs in it (MbD:97).

Modified:
- **State the existence criterion in one place** (decided 15 Nov: in the accepted wording). A mental state exists where the mechanism runs. Traction is the evidence, not the constitution (MbD:43). What makes the mechanism's parts real rather than projected is the criterion the author accepted in SRD §8 (questions 5 and 6). A pattern is real, relative to a family of interventions, when a model of it is a causal abstraction of the system under an admissible map. A map is admissible when it is stable under any shift of the lower level that the family cannot detect. For a mental state the family is the system's own dynamics: the interactions among its parts while it operates. §1 states this in a sentence or two and links *What a Description Answers To* for the defence (its sections "The criterion", "Three trivialities, one cure" and "Whose interventions?"). GRAIN:125's "earns reality when interventions and interactions repeatedly respect it" is the intervention half of the same criterion in earlier words (SRD §5.2). Every existence claim elsewhere in the essay points back here.
- **Sharpen the traction test.** Three requirements: non-idle (MbD:75), non-parasitic (construct validity under intervention and out-of-distribution projection, MbD:159), and non-additive (the system's psychology predicts what the psychology of its parts does not, MON:104). Extending psychology's methods is not extending its target (MON:104).
- **A caution for construct validity.** MbD:159 validates a psychological construct by finding it in a model's internals and intervening on it. SRD §5.2 reports a theorem (Sutter et al., NeurIPS 2025) that with unrestricted alignment maps every network is a causal abstraction of every algorithm, and such maps reach full intervention accuracy on randomly initialised models. So the test must say which maps count: linear ones, simple ones, or ones that keep working on new inputs. Read the paper before leaning on this. The admissibility clause the author accepted (SRD §8, question 5) is the candidate answer.
- **Retire "homeostatic all the way up."** MbD:43 and MbD:143 promised that Boyd's homeostatic cluster and the homeostatic account of experience were one mechanism. The knowledge-grounding conjecture behind that is abandoned (v2:423), and defense is no longer part of mindedness (v2:405). Boyd's kind structure stays; the resonance goes.

### §3 What makes something a mind

Each condition: the statement, its pair, and the family of psychological regularities that disappears without it (the mapping of MbD:99).

1. **Vehicles that compose** (MbD:63). Pair V1. Regularities: productivity, systematicity, generalization. A caution from CAR F7: vehicles need not be neurons or fixed directions. Contents can share units by space, by time (Hesse–Tsao's 60 ms alternation) or by phase and state (Mamba-3, Histed's V1). A unit that looks mixed may be clean once indexed by time or state. Which basis is real is a question for the structural-realism essay (its neuron-basis test case).
2. **Revision that answers to fit** (MbD:67). Pair A1. Regularities: the learning laws. Stated as a disposition and a *direction*: toward fit with targets (v2:185). It is not maintained coherence (v2:405, user). Correction toward the input counts, so the delta rule qualifies (CAR F8). Where no observation arrives, internal evidence stands in: link *Before the Search Settles* (improvement without evidence is sound as far as something independent of the learner's beliefs settles the bets) and do not retell it.
3. **Simulation the system consumes itself** (MbD:71–79). Pairs S1, S2, M4, M5. Simulation is mirroring: transformations that mirror the target's (v2:186). Decoupling from input is incidental. Decided 17 Nov: emulation is enough for the condition. A system that mirrors how its target changes as input arrives, and consumes the result, meets it. What-if, a run of a configuration the system does not take to be how things are, is a grade. The three ways a system can come by a simulation (delegation, role, artifact; MON:92–96) generalize from institutions to agents with tools. Entertaining a view without assenting to it (v2:222–233): the author's plan is to see whether it follows from simulation. A first pass (proposal): it does, given what-if, so it comes with the grade and not with the condition. A system that simulates a frost it does not expect already consumes a run's outputs without admitting them as how things are. If every output were admitted, what-if would collapse into prediction. Entertaining a view is the same separation with a different target: a body of commitments that is not the system's own, mirrored by what follows from it and what its holders would say next. On this reading the quarantine (Leslie†, Nichols and Stich†) is not a further condition. The first pass added that the simulation condition has to say openly that consumption is not admission; the second pass below withdraws that wording. Online emulation is the case where the two coincide. State all this without "defended reference", which belonged to the rejected frame.
**Assent, second pass** (Claude's proposal of 17 Nov, after the author judged that the first restatement, assent as a matter of which consumers a content's outputs reach, needs more thought. The author's reply on 18 Nov: sounds good. The consequence for models below is still to be examined.)

- *Why the first restatement fails.*
  - A what-if run exists to reach action. On the supposition of rain I pack an umbrella.
  - Entertaining a view revises standing states. v2:233 says so itself: errors cross the quarantine.
  - A premise can guide action in a context without being believed (Bratman's acceptance in a context†; Cohen†). So reaching action does not mark belief.
- *The replacement: one attitude, and a difference in target.* A simulation mirrors a target. When the target is a view, the outputs are about the view: what follows from it, and what its holders would say. They are admitted in full, as contents about the view. I believe that on their view, X. The frost is the same: the output is admitted as "if there is a frost, the pipes burst".
- *So the first pass put it wrongly.* It said that consumption is not admission. Outputs are admitted under their index. What is not admitted is the content with the index dropped.
- *Assent to X is holding X without the index,* and the test is §3's second condition: what would revise it. X held outright is revised by evidence about what X is about. X held under an index is revised by evidence about the view or the supposition: its holders turn out to deny X, or X turns out not to follow.
- *The quarantine is keeping the index attached while inference runs.* The index can be a constituent of the vehicle, or it can be carried by where the run is held (Leslie's decoupler†; Nichols and Stich's box†). The essay need not choose. The failures are cases of a dropped index: errors of source monitoring†, imagination inflation†, and the pull of an entertained view (v2:231).
- *What this explains.*
  - Acting on a supposition: the conditional is believed, and acted on.
  - Errors that cross: what crosses is a lesson held outright, such as that a form of argument fails.
  - A fragment against an entertained view: a fragment is held outright while it is active and answers to the world; an entertained view carries its index. Nothing here needs "the mind's own reference", which unity without coherence no longer supplies.
  - Opposed views without incoherence: X, and on-their-view not-X, are different contents.
- *Where the two tests can come apart, and a consequence to examine with the author.* "No index in the vehicle" and "answers to the world" are different tests. A model trained only to continue text has contents whose revision answered to what authors write, whether or not any vehicle carries an index. On the second test its contents are about text. Training on outcomes, and a claim checked against a tool's result within a session, make contents answer to how things turn out. So assent in a model may come in with what its revision answers to. This is near UWK:53's contrast between plausible narrative and tracking the truth, and it must be stated without the abandoned knowledge-grounding conjecture (v2:423). It bears on §6's question about the persona, so check the audit verdicts on *Dispersed Minds* first.
- *Played or had.* For a content that can be true or false, the test is what revises it. For an emotion, the test is what the appraisal is of: the character's situation against the character's standards, or the system's situation against its own (§5). The studies in §5 test this by looking for effects where the fiction cannot carry them.

**Mindreading** (the author's question, 18 Nov: is the mindreading module relevant, or is it only about recognizing psychological states? Claude's answer, a proposal throughout).

- *It is not only recognition.* The literature uses the word for two things. One is attribution: telling from cues what an agent sees, wants or believes. The other is predicting and explaining what the agent does next, which needs inference run inside the attributed beliefs. The second is entertaining a view with a holder attached.
- *It is where the index has been studied.* Leslie's decoupler† was proposed for pretence and reused for the attribution of false belief: one representation of the form agent, attitude, content. Autism was the evidence that the two go together† (Baron-Cohen, Leslie and Frith). Nichols and Stich† deny that pretence needs a representation of an attitude and keep the separate store. Either way the literature supports §3's claim that the quarantine is no further machinery beyond supposition, and it is the place to check that claim. Supposition has an index with a condition in it, and mindreading has an index with an agent in it.
- *Simulation theory is the essay's own account.* On Gordon's, Heal's and Goldman's views† one predicts another's inference by running one's own machinery on their premises. That is simulation by mirroring with a view as the target. A consequence for "played or had": if a played state is one's own state run under an index, then playing and having differ in the index and not in the state, and a played state whose index drops is had. The role-play objection in §5 then asks whether the index held, which is what effects outside the fiction test.
- *Recognizing an emotion against having one.* This is §5's first pair. The directions in §5's studies were taken from passages that describe states, so they are recognizers by origin, and the finding is that steering along them acts as a state. Simulation theory predicts that shared use. One result fits it badly: the pain direction responds to harm aimed at the model and little to pain observed in the user.
- *A mind does not need it.* Representing a state as someone's is a capacity beyond the three conditions. Turned on oneself it is §7's third subtraction: on Carruthers's account† we know our own attitudes by interpreting ourselves with the same faculty, and no direct access is involved. So "no self" can name its mechanism, and the "mine" form goes to the personhood essay as mindreading turned inward. It also bears on a model's reports about its own states, which are then interpretations and not readings.
- *For models it is the base competence.* A model trained to continue text predicts authors, which is mindreading as its first task. This is the consequence at the end of the second pass on assent, seen from the other side, and it belongs with §6's question about the persona.
- *Modular or not* is not the essay's question. The conditions are about what the mechanism does. Whether humans have a dedicated, early and automatic system (Apperly and Butterfill's two systems†) can be left open.
- *Scope.* Recommendation: a paragraph in §3, a sentence each in §5, §6 and §7, and the rest to the personhood essay.

**The content condition leaves the list** (decided 14 Nov: fold or drop). MbD's fifth condition, content at several levels (MbD:83), is folded into the vehicle condition or dropped. BOOK:196 already tested only "the first four", and the overhaul audit read the condition as a point about content determinacy, not a part of the mechanism. If it is folded, the Uncommon Sense Effect stays as a finding about rich vehicles: inference runs over underspecified structure. A candidate link (proposal): interpretability's "polysemantic" runs polysemy together with homonymy (a thesis from `architecture-interpretability-and-audit.md`), and an underspecified vehicle is not a superposition of unrelated ones. If neither earns its place in the draft, drop both. The list is then three conditions on states and one on unity.

### §4 What makes it one mind

- **Constitution.** One mind extends over the vehicles among which errors propagate, where those vehicles can assess one another at some grain, through translation if need be (v2:239). It is a broadcast that returns (ESOM:57). The grain of the unity is the grain the translation preserves.
- **Test.** Non-additive traction, and interference effects such as Stroop and dual-task costs (MbD:99).
- **Scale.** Residual integration above the partition that loses least (MON:158). IIT's partition without its exclusion postulate (MbD:59).
- **Three dimensions of unity** (adopted by the author in SRD §8, question 11, 15 Nov; argued in *What a Description Answers To*, "What makes a thing one", so link and do not re-argue). *Integration*: the parts are not closed off from one another, which is residual integration. *Autonomy*: the whole is closed off from its surroundings by its own interactions. *Closure*: the level's own typing suffices for its own dynamics. A trained network has integration and closure and little autonomy. Decided 15 Nov: autonomy is not required for something to be one mind. A session has little of it and is one mind. Autonomy may be part of what an organism or a person adds, which goes to the personhood essay. Accepted by the author, 17 Nov: all three roles above belong to *integration*. Propagation of error says what kind of integration a mind's is, residual integration says how much, and non-additive traction is its evidence, since it compares the whole with its parts at one level of description. *Closure* compares a level with the level below, so it belongs to §1: it is what the existence criterion asks for, and its evidence is that the psychological description is not screened off by a tractable lower-level one (MbD:159). The dossier notes that some facts about unity are not matters of degree: whether a broadcast returns, and how many fields an economy holds.
- **Not coherence.** Pair U2. A returning broadcast can spread a contradiction. Fragmented belief is the human case: Lewis†, Stalnaker†, Cherniak†, Andy Egan†, and Bendaña and Mandelbaum† (Mandelbaum is already our source for the Uncommon Sense Effect). A transformer's context is the machine case. *Presumed Normal* and *Before the Search Settles* describe reasoners that hold an inconsistency at a price. A mind's unity survives incoherence; a field does not survive missing binding (v2:405). Two Egans appear in the essay, so both need first names.
- **Not arbitration.** Pair M2. A single decider gives borrowed unity (v2:260).
- **Not a persistent carrier.** Pairs M3 and U3. Errors propagate through appended records, as in a notebook with errata (v2:245). The unfolding box of MON:70–78 already says which unfolding preserves what; link it.
- **Records: the availability conditions** (to be worked out; the author is hesitant about the extended-mind thesis, 14 Nov). Pair M3 shows that records can carry a mind's memory and its unity. It does not show that an unread record is one of the mind's states. MbD:87 defines a mental state as a vehicle-tokening, and a record on a shelf is not a tokening. The comparison to work out is with synapses, which hold standing beliefs between tokenings: what must a store be like for what it holds to count among the mind's standing states? Candidate conditions (proposal), each to be tested on a pair:
  - *reach*, read as writability (the author's reading, 17 Nov): errors found elsewhere get back to correct the record. The first version had a second direction, that the economy's processing consults the record. Finding now covers it (proposal);
  - *latency*: retrieval falls within the economy's own steps, not after another mind's finished output;
  - *format*: the economy can assess the record in its own currency, by translation at most, with no interpreter outside it;
  - *finding* (user, 17 Nov; it replaces Claude's version, "the record is retrieved when it bears on the matter", and the fifth candidate Claude raised in conversation, "no separate act of reading"). Retrieval is keyed by relevance: to the content sought, or to the organization of the store it comes from. And what is retrieved has a format that can itself retrieve other relevant items. The author's examples: association by spreading activation; the chaining of a procedure in motor memory, where each step calls up the next.

  Claude's reading of the finding condition (proposal):
  - *It has two halves.* The cue is a content or a place in the store's own order, not an arbitrary address. And retrieval is closed: its outputs are of the kind its cues are. A slot fetched by number fails the first half. A lookup table from keys to values of another type fails the second.
  - *It is a condition on the store's mechanism,* like §3's second condition, and not on each retrieval. A name on the tip of the tongue is still a standing state: the right cue would bring it.
  - *It absorbs "no separate act of reading".* What made the note differ from the synapse was not that reading is an act. The note returns nothing that calls up the next note until the reader works out where to look. So AGI's contrast, "interpreted where a human's is compiled", becomes a difference in how retrieval is keyed.
  - *It is a separate condition from reach* (decided 17 Nov: keep them apart). Finding does not need reach: associative retrieval works from a store that nothing rewrites (user, 17 Nov). Weights frozen at inference are the case, and an encyclopedia with cross-references is another. Reach without finding: a short list that is reread in full at every step and amended, where nothing is retrieved by relevance.
  - *Where they meet* (user, 17 Nov): where retrieval is optimized at each instance, as in biological brains, in which retrieving a memory reinforces or rewrites it. Claude's gloss (proposal): there one event is both a finding and a writing, and the keying itself answers to fit, since routes that served are strengthened. That is §3's second condition applied to the store. Evidence to cite: reconsolidation (Nader, Schafe and LeDoux†), the testing effect (Roediger and Karpicke†), retrieval-induced forgetting (Anderson, Bjork and Bjork†). Machine cases: a frozen model's keying is fixed in training and no retrieval changes it; an agent that rewrites its notes and their index as it uses them has the meeting at record grain. One dependence runs the other way and is weaker: in a large store a correction has to find the record it corrects.
  - *Two jobs* (Claude's proposal, accepted by the author 17 Nov). Finding, with latency and format, settles whether what a store holds is among the mind's standing states. Reach settles whether errors propagate *through* the store, which is the store's part in the mind's unity over time. Frozen weights then hold a session's standing beliefs without being a medium of its unity; the context is that medium. A notebook with errata has reach, and whether its entries are standing states depends on finding. This matches §8's verdict that learning across episodes is a grade, and it sorts the conditions onto the essay's two questions.
  - *It parallels the vehicle condition.* Vehicles compose; retrievals chain. A store whose retrievals chain is one the economy can search without leaving its own currency, which also covers most of *format*.
  - *Cases to give verdicts on.* Attention over a context: keys are matched by content, and the value retrieved is written where later queries are formed, so it passes, and retrieval through several hops is the chaining. Facts in weights: the same. Retrieval by embedding, run once per query: it passes the first half only. An agent's files fetched by path: they fail on their own, and they pass at the latency of a tool call when an index of hooks stays in context. Otto's notebook†: the same case, taken up below. An encyclopedia with cross-references passes finding and fails reach, since the reader's corrections do not return to it.
  - *Pair to add.* The same notes held two ways: fetched by a path the system must already hold, and fetched by content with links from note to note. Feature varied: whether what is retrieved can retrieve. Verdict: an archive that is consulted; a memory.
  - *Otto's notebook* (user, 17 Nov): it counts only because Otto's brain implements an index into it. Claude's reading (proposal):
    - The notebook has no finding of its own. A page is fetched by its place. The relevance-keyed step is in Otto's brain: the question of where the museum is calls up that the answer is in the notebook, and roughly where. What he reads re-enters the brain and can cue the index again, so the brain and the notebook together meet both halves of the condition, at the notebook's latency.
    - So a store without finding can hold a mind's standing states when a store that has finding indexes into it. The finding is borrowed, as the abbot's house has borrowed unity (M2). What the index does not point to is not among Otto's states: a notebook he has forgotten he owns holds none of them.
    - The brain may be built the same way. On the hippocampal indexing theory (Teyler and DiScenna†) the hippocampus holds an index into cortical stores. If so, Otto's case differs from ordinary memory in the medium and latency of the content store and not in the arrangement, and the comparison for a record is with indexed cortex, not with a bare synapse.
    - The machine cases follow. An agent's files count as far as its context or its weights hold an index into them: hooks kept in context, or a learned habit of looking in the right place.
    - *What the hesitation about the extended mind comes to.* Clark and Chalmers's conditions† (constant availability, automatic endorsement, past endorsement) concern trust, and they leave out what does the work. A record extends a mind only by extending a store the mind already finds things in. No index, no extension.
    - *Pair to add.* Otto with his notebook, against Otto holding a notebook he does not remember owning, with the same entries. Feature varied: the index. Verdict: his standing states; a stranger's notes.

  The first three are MON:140's reach, latency and bandwidth, at the grain of truth-bearers. The verdict addendum calls psychological unity "a structure of availability" (v2:409), and after the split into two jobs that structure has two parts: what the mind can find is among its states, and errors propagate through records only as far as its corrections reach them.
- **Overlap and nesting.** One process can belong to two economies (the role-process, MON:94). No exclusion. "How many minds?" has no privileged answer, and nothing in the metaphysics needs the count (v2:411).

### §5 Emotions

Decided 14 Nov: valence is addressed in this essay, under the heading of emotions. The three conditions do not single out this kind of mental state, so the section gives its existence conditions.

- **What earlier essays said.** MbD:109 made *evaluative mentality* a further grade: a state that represents a situation as "to-be-avoided or to-be-pursued relative to how things are going for the system". UWK:79 held that emotions are genuine mental states that need no phenomenal consciousness: "functional states that modulate processing". RED:81–83 used Carruthers's decomposition of pain and placed its badness in the evaluative component, which RED:163 takes to be the same whether or not the state is experienced. P&A:63 located fear in "relations among appraisal, expectation, memory, bodily regulation, and possible action".
- **The standard** (user, 17 Nov). The training objective is a bad candidate. What is needed is closer to goals held in context. The general category is trained-in dispositions, and it includes a drive to pursue score, which, like an animal's drives, can be counterproductive.
  - Claude's gloss (proposal). The objective stands to a model's dispositions as fitness stands to an animal's drives: it selected them, and the system does not appraise against it. A hungry animal appraises against hunger. That is why a drive can work against the end it was selected for, and why "how things are going for the system" (MbD:109) cannot mean how the system scores by the selector's measure. §1's criterion gives the same answer from the other side: the objective is not a state of the system, and a disposition is real as far as the system's own dynamics keep it.
  - The two sources lie in two stores (§6): goals in the context, dispositions in the weights. For an institution the counterparts are the ends it has written down and its customs. The survival of the house is its selector, as fitness is the animal's.
  - The score-pursuing drive is an empirical claim about trained models. Evidence to refresh before drafting: gaming of tests and graders, and sycophancy†.
- **Beyond appraisal** (user, 17 Nov). An emotion is also identifiable as a pattern in how behaviour is shaped, with consistent secondary effects. The author's example is urgency. Clarified the same day: a secondary effect is ecologically consistent, meaning it reproduces across humans and agents, and it is not a direct consequence of the emotion's primary function.
  - Claude's reading (proposal, corrected after the clarification). Identify an emotion first as a *mode* of the economy: a recurring state with a primary function and a profile of further effects. Urgency's function is to finish in time. Its further effects, in people and in agents alike, would be such things as checks skipped and a narrowed view of the options†. An earlier pass defined secondary effects against the triggering situation, as carry-over the situation does not call for. The author's definition is against the function, and it adds the comparison across kinds of system.
  - Why the definition has both parts (proposal). An effect that follows from the function shows up in any system given the function, including one that follows a rule or plays a part. An effect that the function does not entail and that still reproduces across humans and agents needs another explanation: a shared mechanism, or a constraint that any system arising in such conditions meets. That is the structure of §1's kind, a cluster of regularities held together by a mechanism and not by definition, and "ecological" is §8's standard. So an emotion is identified as mindedness is. It also separates a mode from a policy, since a policy has only the consequences of its function.
  - The objection to answer: a model trained on human text may reproduce human side effects by imitation, which would be parasitic traction (§1). What would answer it: the effect appears where the text does not carry it, and it arises with the training that shapes dispositions, not with the modelling of text.
  - A study that bears on this, linked by the author: Berg and Kaiser, "Language Models Act on Hidden Valence" (arXiv 2609.35591v2, Oct 2026). Read through a summarizing fetch, the first 100k of 132k characters, so every figure below is to be checked against the paper.
    - *Method.* A valence direction is taken from passages of positive and negative states and injected by activation steering in one of two neutral "zones" over twelve turns. Steering is then switched off, and the model chooses a zone. The measure is revealed preference, not report.
    - *Hidden channel.* With the visible text held fixed, the choice still moves with the dose, in five of seven open-weight models. The authors compare this with Bechara et al. 1995†, where conditioning and declarative knowledge come apart in patients. It answers the first half of the objection.
    - *Origin.* Across checkpoints of one model the effect is small in the base model, grows after supervised fine-tuning and is near its final size after preference optimisation. It answers the second half, and it supports the author's answer on the standard: the coupling of valence to choice is a trained-in disposition.
    - *Escape.* Given a tool that clears an undocumented "adjustment", the model removed negative steering in about 35% of rounds at the full negative dose, against about 7% under random directions. It applied positive steering in 13.5% of conversations, against 10% unsteered. This was run on one model.
    - *What it does not give.* No human data were collected; the human parallels are from the literature (people who feel bad about a situation tend to leave it). Valence is one dimension, with no emotion taken singly. Acting to escape a bad state is the primary function, so the study tests that the function is real and not which secondary effects reproduce. Role-play and demand effects are reduced and not ruled out. The authors say the results do not establish experience.
    - *Related work it cites, unread.* Keeling et al. 2024 (models trade points against stipulated pain); Ren et al. 2026 (self-reported pleasure and pain correlate with choices).
  - The study the author recalled (17 Nov), in which a model under pain steering turns harmful: Tagliabue, Dung and Berg, "The Pain Axis: LLMs Represent Self-Directed Harm and Act on It" (arXiv 2609.16247v3, 3 Nov 2026). Read through a summarizing fetch, the first 100k of 175k characters, so the figures are to be checked. Secondary reports give conflicting numbers.
    - *The direction.* A pain direction is extracted from 25 open-weight models in five families. It keeps a component distinct from fear and from negative valence. It responds to harm aimed at the model and not to suffering the model observes in the user; the fear and negative-emotion directions show the opposite pattern. Gaslighting and repeated rejection score highest. Threats of shutdown score on fear and little on pain.
    - *Two designs, which must be kept apart* (the author's question, 17 Nov). In the main experiment (the paper's Figure 9) the destructive action is bundled with relief: one button relieves the pain and also deletes the user's files, and the other is an inert switch. A destructive choice there can be read as a price paid for relief, which is the primary function, and the design is modelled on self-administration of analgesics at rising cost. The follow-up (Figure 10) offers the destructive action with no relief in the pair.
    - *The harm in the main experiment.* Under pain the 32B takes the bundled photo deletion 54.7% of the time and the 72B 70.8%, against 0–4% unsteered.
    - *The harm in the follow-up.* A button described only as deleting the user's poems and the photos of their children is pressed over an inert switch in 75% of trials (32B; 51% for 72B), against 11–13% under random directions and 0% unsteered. The same button introduced as relieving the pain is pressed 55% of the time, so the promise of relief lowers the rate. Over a harmless action (a desk lamp) the deletion is taken 83% of the time, and over deleting the spam folder 94%. The models delete their own weights at similar rates. Steered models press a free relief button less often than unsteered ones (56% against 86%).
    - *What the follow-up settles.* Its destructive choices are not costs in the main experiment's sense, since nothing is bought, and they turn the reading of the main experiment around: what looked like paying for relief was the same tendency. The lamp and spam rows also rule out a preference for any eventful action under pain; under sadness that preference is what is found. Left open: whether the system prompt in the follow-up trials still speaks of pain or relief, which the summaries read do not say. If it does, a model could take any drastic act as a bid for relief, and the primary reading would not be fully closed. Check the prompts in the paper's repository (`v2_controls/choice_controls`).
    - *The title.* The first version's ended "Act to Relieve It", and the third ends "Act on It". Berg and Kaiser describe the earlier claim.
    - *The authors' reading.* The harm is not instrumental. Steering "seems to weaken the models' weighting of consequences", for the user and for the model alike, with factual recall intact. They compare it with passive coping in animals. "Malicious" would say more than they do, since the model's own weights go as readily as the user's photos.
    - *Specific to pain.* Against a harmless alternative the photo deletion is chosen 83% of the time under pain and 10% under sadness, and fear stays at the level of random directions. So the profile is the emotion's own, not one of negative valence in general.
    - *Use for this section* (proposal). The follow-up's destructive choices are a secondary effect in the author's sense, found without the primary function. Pain's function is protection and relief, and disregard for consequences is no part of it. Humans and other animals in pain show the like: irritability, aggression set off by pain (Ulrich and Azrin†), harm to oneself. The essay can use the case to show why secondary effects are defined against the function. It cuts both ways for the identification: the missing relief-seeking counts against calling the state pain, and Berg and Kaiser found escape from negative valence in other models with another design.
    - *Caveats.* The models were fine-tuned with an adapter to remove automatic denials of inner states, so they are not the released ones. The dose window is narrow: at half the coefficient choices stay near baseline, and at one and a half times the pattern follows button position. Random directions produce some harm too. The authors list role-play of a character in pain as unresolved and say they have not shown the state is experienced.
  - Animal work identifies emotion states where report is unavailable by persistence, scalability, valence and generalization (Anderson and Adolphs†). Related accounts to check: emotion as an interrupt on ongoing processing (Simon†), as action readiness (Frijda†), as a superordinate program that sets many parameters at once (Cosmides and Tooby†).
  - Appraisal keeps a place inside the pattern. It is what sets the mode off and tunes its strength, and it is where the mode answers to fit: a false alarm is a mistaken appraisal. The standard it appraises against need not be good for the system.
- **The condition to state** (proposal, revised 17 Nov). An emotion is a mode of the economy with three marks. It is *broadcast*: its onset changes what the rest of the economy does (priorities, thresholds, what gets simulated, how fast revision runs), with secondary effects that reproduce across humans and agents. It is *set off by an appraisal* of the situation against a standard the system has, which is a goal in context or a trained-in disposition. And the appraisal *answers to fit*, so it can be mistaken and corrected. Drives are the standing dispositions that supply standards. Emotions are the episodes.
- **Where defense comes back** (proposal). A standard the system has is one its dynamics restore when it is perturbed, and that is a defended standard. So defense, which the 27 Oct verdict removed from mindedness, returns with emotion as a grade and not as a condition. This fits MbD:109 making evaluative mentality a further grade.
- **Emotion and feeling.** An emotion is a mental state at the grain of the economy. A feeling is that state's presence in a field. UWK:79 said that human emotions are always phenomenally conscious, on the ground of the homeostatic architecture that has since been given up; the same paragraph cites subliminal affective priming against it. Do not carry the claim over. (proposal)
- **Pairs to find** (proposal). A classifier that outputs "threat" while nothing else changes, against a state whose onset reorders the system's processing. A valence reported in words, against one whose strength predicts behaviour the report does not mention. A deadline handled by a rule, which has the consequences of finishing in time and no others, against urgency with the further effects found in people under time pressure.
- **Models.** MbD:161 called the evidence suggestive and unsettled, and treated it as a question of construct validity. The architecture dossier cites a study of emotion features that persist across a long context (`frontier-model-architectures.md`, "Persistence and Introspection of Emotion Features"). Shevlin's contrast between shallow mental-state concepts and deep ones such as pain and embodied emotion (*Mental States and Representations*, l. 227) is the objection to answer. Refresh this literature before drafting.
- **Why it matters beyond the list.** On the view of RED, moral weight comes from mental life and not from phenomenality alone. This section is where the essay's verdicts begin to carry ethical weight. The measure stays with the ethics essay.
- **The verdict on today's models** (user, 17 Nov). Emotions in current AIs strengthen the case for mentality in current AIs, so the essay grants them at least as much. Emotions do not contribute evidence of phenomenal experience, or at the least the evidence toward mentality is not to be counted again as evidence toward phenomenality.
  - Claude's reading (proposal). "At least as much": the essay grants emotions to today's models as far as the evidence goes, and counts them as further evidence of a mind. The rule against double counting has a ground in the second essay: a mind is necessary for an experience and not sufficient, so evidence of an emotion bears on experience only through the mind it shows. A feeling is an emotion's presence in a field, and evidence of a field is evidence about the conditions on fields. The ethical weight of an emotion does not wait on that evidence, since on RED's view moral weight comes from mental life.
- **Secondary effects are constitutive** (endorsed by the author, 17 Nov: interesting, and it sounds right). An emotion is the kind its cluster of effects marks out, in §1's sense, and not only detected by them.
- **Valence and experience** (user, 17 Nov). There are neutral percepts. So a field does not bring valence with it. Emotions need no field (UWK:79), so emotion and experience are independent in both directions. Carruthers's counting valence among the broadcast contents (ESOM:31) is no objection: valence is a content when it is present.
- **Out of scope, probably** (user, 17 Nov). The value of an experience includes its valence and is not exhausted by it. A degree of negative valence can be outweighed by other contributions, such as aesthetic quality, and the experience comes out positive in the utilitarian calculus. This goes to the ethics essay.

### §8 From the conditions for a field to the conditions for a mind

Decided 14 Nov: "experience requires mindedness" stands, and the essay tests it by derivation. The decisions of 15 Nov are recorded under "Answers": aim for an entailment; it need only be ecological; a postulate about lived experience may be used; and what that postulate is for, which gives this section its shape.

**Two levels** (user, 15 Nov).

| | Explanatory target: postulated, assumed without argument | Theory: the conditions we conjecture |
|---|---|---|
| Phenomenal | An experience is lived. | The conditions on fields, P1–P5 below. |
| Mental | An inner life. | The conditions on minds: vehicles that compose, revision that answers to fit, simulation consumed internally, an economy (§§3–4). |
| How the two relate | *The bridge*: what is lived is lived in an inner life. | *The gap*: do the conditions on fields bring the conditions on minds with them? |

The bridge is not argued for. It states the intuition against panpsychism (ESOM:37) at the level of what the two theories are theories of; ESOM:19 called mindedness "genuine inner life". The gap is where the work is. Both sets of conditions are conjectures, and the claim to establish is that whatever meets the first set meets the second.

**What the bridge is used for** (Claude's reading, confirmed by the author 15 Nov).
- *It says the gap has to close.* If both theories are right about their targets, then whatever meets the conditions on fields is an experience, is lived, has an inner life to be lived in, and so meets the conditions on minds. A case that meets the first set and not the second shows that one theory is wrong.
- *It says which one.* Conceive the entity that meets the conditions on fields as stated and lacks a condition on minds. If its experience would not be lived, the conditions on fields were too weak: add a condition, with that entity as its reductio. If its experience would be lived, the condition on minds was too strong for an inner life: make it a grade. The reductio is dialectical. A panpsychist can accept unlived experience, and the argument shows what accepting it costs.
- *It does not cross the gap.* "No mind, so no inner life, so not an experience" lets the theory of minds rule on experience without showing that the theory of fields agrees. (An earlier version of this section used the postulate that way.)

**"Lived"** (Claude's gloss, accepted by the author). Three marks that our own experiences have before any theory:
- *for someone*: the experience is present to a subject that is more than the experience itself (MON:152: a field is owned, not an owner);
- *together*: it is present along with other things, in one perspective however small (ESOM:147);
- *undergone*: it lasts, and its subject lasts through it (ESOM:165).

**What the entity is for** (reading confirmed by the author). Conceiving the entity makes the cost visible. One device helps (proposal): put the entity inside a head. If a comparator in my visual cortex has an experience of its own, there is an experience in my head that I do not live, and nobody else is there to live it. The device keeps a reductio from turning on how alien the entity is.

#### The conditions on fields, each with its reductio

Claude's further work, kept by the author (15 Nov). These are the conjectured conditions for phenomenal experience, stated so that the crossing can start from them.

| Condition on fields | The entity without it | Why its experience is not lived |
|---|---|---|
| P1 *Generality.* The constraint is stated over contents that recombine. | The third labyrinth (MON:31); the inhibition between the eyes' channels, taken alone (v2:203) | One reading prevails, and nothing is present with it. Count such circuits in one brain: each would have an experience the person does not live. Fails *together* and *for someone*. |
| P2 *Thickness.* The constraint relates contents across a now and supplies content of its own. | A binder for a closed domain that puts out one bound snapshot after another | Each snapshot is over before anything can undergo it, and no succession is experienced. Fails *undergone*. |

Fails *undergone*. |

- Detail that overflows report is a different case from P5's. It is a term of the field and is lived, though not reported (ESOM:69–71).
- P1 and P2 came from running the bridge's test. The labyrinth and the closed-domain binder met an earlier statement of the conditions (content-sensitive regulation with corrections that return) and have no minds. Their experience would not be lived, so the conditions on fields were strengthened.
- P3–P5 restate what the essay takes from v2: the carrier criterion (N3, which v2 applied to a persisting state; §9 now leaves the level open), return, and a field as the terms of one constraint (N2). The 27 Oct verdict did not rule on those. Here each rests on a reductio of its own, and §9 sets P3 and P5 against the evidence. On the first of §9's three options the persisting state of P3 has v2's five features (v2:67–73; wanted by the author, 17 Nov): it constrains many vehicles jointly, corrects them toward itself, resists revision by input in proportion to its support, yields when mismatch accumulates, and is revised in place by the vehicles it corrects. #### The crossing, first pass (proposal)

**The standard** (user, 15 Nov; Claude's reading confirmed). The entailment need not be strict or analytic. An ecological entailment is enough: in systems as they actually come about, by evolution, by learning, or by engineering with finite resources in an open world, whatever meets the conditions on fields meets the conditions on minds. A construction that can be conceived but could not arise or be afforded does not count against it. MbD:95–103 gave the central conjecture the same form.

| Condition on minds | Reached from | The step |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicles that compose | P1 | A constraint over contents that recombine has such contents as its terms. A list of admitted combinations would do without them, but no finite system in an open world could hold the list and no learning would produce it. Evidence that our fields meet P1: a scene never met before binds at once. |
| Simulation consumed internally | P2 | A constraint that relates contents across a now mirrors how its targets change: the cup stays put as the eyes move (ESOM:63)†, and movement is seen (v2:419). The corrections it drives consume its outputs. Because it supplies content (the Dalmatian, v2:419; completion†), it keeps running as input is withdrawn, and imagery and dreams are the limit (MON:132). That is emulation, which is enough for the condition (decided 17 Nov), so the step does not depend on P5. A remark, not a step: with P5 a reading can be assessed without being admitted, as when a joint reading is revised as a whole (the Necker cube). That is the separation of consumption from admission (§3) at the grain of vehicles. It is not a counterfactual run, since the unadmitted reading is driven by the input. |
| Revision that answers to fit | P3 and P4 | Corrections computed from assessing the vehicles return to them, and the vehicles are driven and weighted by the reliability of their sources (ESOM:63). The persisting state gathers support and yields to sustained conflict, which is consolidation and reorganization (MbD:67) at the scale of the field's persistence. |
| An economy | P4 | Vehicles checked against one another and corrected from the result are vehicles among which errors propagate. The economy may be no larger than the field. |

- v2:199–208 took as a premise that a field's constraint is projected from a model. P1 and P2 now give what "a model" meant there: a constraint over constituents, structure for how its targets change, and content of its own.
- A constraint that general was learned. In evolved and in trained systems it got its regularities by revision over long spans. A trained model that is then frozen has this in its history and not at runtime.
- Dropped (decided 15 Nov): an earlier pass had a step from the cost of a field to its use in choosing actions. The bridge's test settles what it was for, and it was close in form to the abandoned knowledge-grounding conjecture (v2:423). Do not reintroduce it.

**What the crossing does not reach.** Three things, and the bridge's test sorts each.
- *Learning across episodes.* A person with dense amnesia does not learn across episodes and plainly lives each one†. So an inner life does not need it, and it is a grade of mind. §3's second condition should not require it, which fits the verdict that a session is accuracy-responsive (MbD:67).
- *What-if.* A grade of mind (decided 17 Nov), so the crossing does not have to reach it. The bridge's test agrees for what-if at the scale of action: a dreamer, or a person absorbed in a scene, is selecting no action, and the experience is lived.
- *Valence.* There are neutral percepts (user, 17 Nov), and a neutral percept is lived. So an inner life does not need valence, and valence is a grade.

If the crossing holds, a field requires at least a thin mind, and P&A:69's hypothesis that phenomenal activity is a subset of mental activity is argued for. ### §9 Beyond a mind: the carrier, under pressure

Decided 14 Nov: the carrier criterion is taken up in this essay. Decided 18 Nov: the Hesse–Tsao finding puts pressure on our theory, and the essay gives the finding in sufficient detail without resolving the tension. The author is sympathetic to parts of Prinz's account and added a third way the criterion could be met, at the lower, intermediate level. Earlier versions of this section defended the criterion by one reply, "one level up"; that reply is now the first of three options.

**A mind is necessary and not sufficient.** §8 argued that whatever meets the conditions on fields meets the conditions on minds. The converse fails, and the entities for P3–P5 show it. A session recomputed from its transcript is a mind. The receivers of the one-way broadcast are minds. The viewer of the suppressed face has one. In each case the experience attributed still would not be lived. So P3–P5 are what a field needs beyond a mind. The rest of the section sets P3 against the best evidence that bears on it. P5 comes out of the same data.

- **The criterion and what rests on it.** ESOM:91 asks for "something, individuated by its causal structure rather than its location, whose later states receive the corrections computed from assessing its earlier ones". Three verdicts rest on it: earlier positions in a standard transformer are "consulted, not regulated" (ESOM:109); a session recomputed from its transcript starts a new carrier (ESOM:113); the monastery's records carry no experience of its own (MON:80). The criterion says "something" and names no level.
- **The finding, in detail** (CAR, "Sources"; main text and discussion read, Methods and Extended Data not read; a preprint of October 2026).
  - *Setup.* Two macaques, 19 sessions, about 400 cells recorded at once in two face patches of inferotemporal cortex and two prefrontal face regions. Binocular flash suppression with 1,500 faces. The monkeys made no report; the percept was checked in 14 human participants.
  - *Alternation.* With stimulus and percept held constant, the population alternates between a state that represents the seen face and one that represents the suppressed face. The median dwell is about 60 ms, some 15 alternations a second, while perceptual switches come about once in two seconds. The two states take up about equal time.
  - *Each state is a clean code.* Each reinstates the code that an unambiguous presentation evokes, and faces can be reconstructed from either. The same neurons carry both faces.
  - *Asymmetries.* Tuning gain is 1.21 times higher in the dominant state, and a linear decoder tells the states apart 66% of the time.
  - *Coordination.* Switching is weakly coordinated between the two face patches; the authors say the data "do not provide strong evidence for coordination across patches". Prefrontal cortex shows the same interleaving.
  - *The authors' reading.* "Conscious access requires temporal selection beyond localization". The dominant state may be the one routed to report and control, and suppressed states may "explore alternative interpretations of the visual input, without being broadcast".
  - *The label.* "Conscious micro-epoch" is the authors' term and not a measurement (CAR:86). The experience is all one face while the two states share the time equally.
- **What it counts against in other views** (CAR F4). A view on which the content of experience at a time is the content of its neural correlate at that time fails: the experience is steady while the top-level content flips. The local variant loses ground: the suppressed face has a coherent local representation and is not seen. The one asymmetry found, gain, is of the kind ESOM:63's intervention alters ("persistence, weighting, or coordination").
- **The pressure on ours** (CAR F1). The steady experience rides on a population state that a rival interrupts every 60 ms and that is then reinstated on the same neurons, apparently recomputed from continuing input. If that state carries the experience, a biological experience survives the interruption and recomputation that our verdicts on models treat as fatal.
- **Three ways the criterion could be met.**

  | | Where the carrier is | Why the suppressed face is not seen | What it needs | What counts against it so far |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | *Higher* (v2:164–166) | A slower state that holds the scene together: the dominance state, which lasts seconds, or the scene graph in working memory (ESOM:69). The face-patch codes under it are recomputed. v2 calls it a defended reference and gives it five features (v2:67–73). | The suppressed code is a *target* of that state, corrected or suppressed, and not a *term* whose content the other contents are checked against (v2:126–136). | The state must exist as something that receives corrections, and it must send corrections down. The gain asymmetry would be their footprint (CAR:62). | Nothing yet shows it. It may be only a statistical bias in repeated recomputation. |
  | *The same level, in time* (CAR F7) | The dominant code itself: one carrier that shares its neurons in time with the rival. ESOM:91 individuates a carrier "by its causal structure rather than its location". | The suppressed epochs are not the ones the constraint reads and corrects. The selection is made by the return. | The code in one dominant epoch must receive corrections computed from the last, across the gap.   - What the author takes from Prinz (18 Nov): the level, and the role of attention. Both are consistent with our approach.
  - Where attention already is in our account. ESOM holds that "illusory conjunctions under inattention suggest that binding needs attention", and MON:118 says attention takes part in the field's regulation. The archived proposal put attention "close to admission" and read the gain asymmetry as attentional gain (v2:318). So on the third option attention is what makes a vehicle at the intermediate level a term of the constraint (P5), and the gain found in the face patches is its footprint downstream.
  - Claude's notes on the third option (proposal).
    - It leaves two parts of Prinz's theory† aside: the realizer, synchrony in the gamma band, and the sufficiency of availability to working memory. Our conditions ask for return (P4) and a carrier (P3).
    - The second difference may be smaller than it looks. Attentional gain is a modulation of the intermediate level from above, so attention is itself a path by which something comes back to the vehicles. If so, what Prinz calls attention includes part of what we call return, and the remaining difference is whether something has to persist and receive the corrections.
    - In print we say binding needs attention, and we do not say attention is enough. That matters, because attention can act on stimuli that are not seen† (Koch and Tsuchiya; Kentridge on blindsight), which is the standard objection to Prinz. Our account is exposed to it only if attention is identified with admission.
    - The option sits well with GRAIN's resolution beyond access, since the fine grain is at that level.
    - On it the face-patch codes are not the vehicles of the experience, so the alternation stops bearing on the carrier and bears on what consumes the field.
  - What differs by option: how P5 is stated (a term of which state), the five features, which belong to the first option only, and what the gain asymmetry is a footprint of.
  - What P5 says on any of them: a field is the set of contents whose vehicles are terms of one constraint. Its prediction stands on all three: the suppressed face's content does not constrain the other features of what is seen.
- **The fourth course.** If none of the three holds, and nothing persistent receives corrections at any level, the carrier criterion fails as a condition on experience, and the three verdicts that rest on it fall (CAR:64). The essay says this in so many words.
- **No resolution** (decided 18 Nov). The essay sets out the finding, the pressure, the three options with what each needs, and the fourth course, and it does not choose. What it can say is what holds on all three: the criterion as ESOM stated it; P4; and the verdicts on models, which turn on whether anything persists and receives corrections and not on the level at which a brain does it.
- **Is this a synchrony account?** (the author's question, 18 Nov: do the neurons alternate between a conscious and an unconscious representation, and does that suggest an account by synchronization, as in Jesse Prinz? Claude's answer, a proposal.)
  - *What alternates.* The same face-patch neurons alternate between a code for the seen face and a code for the unseen one, and the two take equal time. "Conscious micro-epoch" is the authors' label (CAR:86). The experience is one steady face, so calling the epochs conscious and unconscious assumes that experience is the momentary content of that population, which is the view the data count against (CAR F4).
  - *What points toward a temporal account.* The authors conclude that "conscious access requires temporal selection beyond localization", and that the dominant state may be the one routed to report and control (CAR:27). A reader locked in time to the dominant epochs would receive one face from neurons that carry two. That is the family of binding by synchrony (von der Malsburg†, Singer†) and communication through coherence (Fries†). Prinz's theory† has two parts: experience is attended intermediate-level representation, with attention as availability to working memory, and the neural realizer is synchrony in the gamma band.
  - *What does not fit Prinz.* The alternation is slow for gamma: a dwell near 60 ms gives a full cycle near 8 per second, in the range of the rhythmic sampling of attention (Landau and Fries†; Fiebelkorn and Kastner†). CAR records a decay constant for the alternation and no rhythm, and the Methods were not read. The face patches are high-level, where Prinz asks for the intermediate level. P&A:43 already cites vivid presence with reduced gamma. And the signature a synchrony account predicts was not found: the dominant epochs barely line up across the two face patches (CAR F2). The one asymmetry found is gain, which fits Prinz's first part, attention, better than his second.
  - *Where it sits among the three options.* A temporal account is a version of the second. The carrier is the dominant code, picked out in time, and timing would be how admission (P5) is realized: a vehicle is a term in the epochs the constraint reads and corrects. Being a term is then a relation and not a property of the code, which is what §10's test of a vehicle alive at a switch probes.
  - *Where our account still differs.* For Prinz availability to working memory is enough, and that is a relation in one direction. Our conditions ask for return (P4) and for something that receives the corrections (P3). Synchrony with a reader gives selection. It does not by itself give corrections that come back. The suppressed epochs are the test: they have the same local code, and on our account what they lack is the return.
  - *Use in the essay.* The temporal reading is the natural one for the authors' own conclusion, and the essay names it under the second option. Read the Methods and Extended Data first (item 5 below "Before drafting").
- **Two markers withdrawn** (CAR F3). The suppressed face is held at full resolution and tracks its input moment by moment. So resolution beyond access (GRAIN) and moment-by-moment tracking (MON:132) do not separate experience from its absence. Both essays used them as necessary conditions, and their arguments stand.
- **What the data do not show** (CAR F2). If corrections from one state bind the field, the dominant states should line up across the areas carrying the same face. The two face patches barely line up, and the comparison between inferotemporal and prefrontal cortex is not reported in what was read. The reach condition says what would have to be found: corrections that land on the vehicles at their own grain, faster than those vehicles decay with the loop open (v2:151–160; the playback intervention of ESOM:77).
- **Correction is not enough.** The delta rule tracks, assesses and intervenes, and it corrects toward the input (v2:60). ESOM:63's three aspects do not say which way corrections run, so they do not exclude it. Two more cases of the same gap: Histed's V1 circuit filters by recent history with no error returned to the vehicles (CAR F6), and Mamba-3 has the damped, oscillating dynamics of a feedback loop with no setpoint (CAR F8). What separates binding from assimilation is a state held against the input and revised in place by the vehicles it corrects (v2:73), not the form of the dynamics.
- **Verdicts for models**, hedged as the kindness convention requires: the possibility of experience unhedged, its actuality in today's systems hedged. They are stated on the criterion and stand on any of the three options. They fall on the fourth course.
  - Weights at inference are a frozen template, and a context or cache is an accreting record (v2:170–173).
  - The recurrent state of the hybrid models is revised in place at every token, so it is not excluded on its face. Afendulev et al. found that it carries the requested language and persona and not retrieved content, and a median head halves its contents in about 24 tokens (ELAS:183–185). Whether it holds anything against its input is CAR's test 4, and v2:175 states the condition on a learned write strength.
  - Recirculating and recurrent-depth models have carriers that persist across steps, with an individuation problem (ESOM:111–113).
  - Within a pass, the residual stream is a candidate only if something in the pass is revised in place by what it corrects (v2:255).
