# Gorard on Bombelli–Henson–Sorkin: what the primary source says

31 Oct 2026. Source read in full text: J. Gorard, "Algorithmic Causal Sets and the Wolfram Model", [arXiv:2011.12174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12174) (v1 23 Nov 2020, v2 18 Oct 2021; no journal reference on arXiv). Section 3, "Lorentz Symmetry and Causal Invariance", pp. 14–32 of the PDF.

## What the paper claims

Abstract: causal invariance of the rewriting system "can be used to infer conformal invariance of the induced causal partial order, in a manner that is provably compatible with the measure-theoretic arguments of Bombelli, Henson and Sorkin."

Section 3 does three things.

1. **Restates the BHS proof** for Poisson sprinklings (no measurable equivariant map $D:\Omega\to\mathcal H^{n-1}$, because the boost-invariant pushforward measure on the noncompact hyperboloid would have to be infinite). It contrasts the Euclidean case, where a preferred direction map can exist because $S^1$ is compact. This part is a faithful restatement.

2. **Argues causal invariance gives Lorentz invariance directly.** Different updating orders correspond to different foliations (discrete lapse $\alpha$ and shift $\beta^i$). "Transformations between reference frames in general relativity … are given by diffeomorphism transformations corresponding to arbitrary actions of the conformal group C(1, n) on a Lorentzian manifold, and hence causal invariance corresponds precisely to a statement of conformal invariance of the causal network." Lorentz and Poincaré symmetry "follow immediately from the requirement of causal invariance (since causal invariance guarantees that the ordering of non-spacelike-separated updating events is always preserved under arbitrary changes of foliation)." The toy example is a grid-like 1+1 causal network with a "discrete Lorentz transformation" $t' = \lfloor\cosh\rho\,t - \sinh\rho\,x\cdot u\rfloor$, giving a "tipped" foliation. The conclusion (p. 30): "we can prove that no equivariant measurable map exists that could be used to construct a preferred direction map on the causal network, for such a preferred direction map would imply a preferred foliation of the causal network (and hence a preferred updating order on the hypergraph), which is incompatible with the hypothesis that the causal network is invariant under different choices of foliation."

3. **Addresses infinite valency** (pp. 30–32). It grants that sprinkled causal sets have almost surely infinitely many future links per element, so "it might at first appear that the average out-degree of any given updating event in a causal network would need to be infinite in order to maintain compatibility with Lorentz symmetry". It answers that this "would depend upon the (incorrect) assumption that the observer is able to discriminate between individual updating events". One should instead "construct an effective theory by essentially coarse-graining over the underlying (microscopic) causal network by using the specification of the foliation to define an equivalence relation over the set of updating events". With the lapse $\alpha$ made arbitrarily large, the classes grow, "such that the out-degree of vertices in the effective causal network may be unbounded, even if the out-degree of vertices in the underlying (microscopic) is bounded, as required." It appeals to dimensional analysis (its ref. [13]) for a causal-edge scale "many orders of magnitude smaller than anything that can even in principle be observed".

## Assessment (ours)

- **Move 2 is passive invariance.** It shows that the causal graph does not depend on the slicing used to generate it. BHS ask a different question: whether the graph (or the ensemble of graphs) has structure from which a timelike direction can be read off equivariantly. A graph can be foliation-independent and still pick out a frame. Gorard's own toy, the regular grid, does: boosts are not automorphisms of a grid, and a tipped foliation of it is a different slicing of a graph whose rows still define a rest frame. Bolognesi and Lamb make the general point for deterministic, algorithmically generated causal sets: regular ones "would not pass the key test for Lorentz invariance: the absence of preferred reference frames" ([arXiv:1407.1649](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.1649)).
- **The BHS framework does not apply as stated.** BHS concern a probability measure on sprinklings with a Lorentz action. A causal-invariant rule produces one causal graph, on which the Lorentz group does not act. The "provable" compatibility therefore rests on the identification of foliation changes with Lorentz (indeed conformal) transformations, which is asserted rather than derived.
- **Move 3 concedes the corollary microscopically.** Lorentz invariance is claimed for an effective, coarse-grained network. The coarse-graining is defined by a choice of foliation, so it starts from a frame, and the paper does not show that the effective network is the same whichever foliation is used. Unbounded out-degree in the effective network is also weaker than the BHS structure (links spread along the whole hyperboloid). What the argument supports is approximate Lorentz invariance hidden below observational resolution. That is a testable claim, subject to the time-of-flight and diffusion bounds the essay cites; whether the causal-edge scale lies far enough below the Planck scale to evade them is not established in the paper.

## Use in *The Standard of Motion*
