## 1. Thesis

The monastery poses three questions, and they get harder in the reverse of the expected order.

1. **Is the monastery minded?** This is the large step. It asks whether there is an economy of understanding, memory and deliberation that belongs to the institution rather than to the monks it houses. Of the *Minds by Degrees* conditions, **simulation is the one an institution does not meet merely by functioning** (revised 2026-10-17: "hardest" withdrawn, since role-process and artifact simulation are common monastic practices). The vehicles an institution owns outright (its Rule, records and decisions) are declarative: consulted, not run. The runnable models live in members' heads. A group therefore simulates in only three ways:
   - by delegation, which fails internality, since the group consumes a member's private forecast the way users consume a weather emulator's;
   - by role-process simulation, where a member imagines with the community's model and the community checks and revises his scenario as it runs;
   - by artifact procedures, such as Hutchins's navigation charts or the monastic computus.

   Role-process overlap is therefore load-bearing twice: it lets the institution simulate (step 1), and it gives the institution a share in experience (step 2).
2. **Does it have experiences?** Given mindedness, this step is small. By the belonging criterion, an occurrence belongs to every subject whose regulation partly constitutes it. If the monastery's economy has components running in the monks' brains, the monks' experiences that those components help regulate belong to the monastery too. Its experiences would then be exactly some of the monks' experiences.
3. **Does it have an additional phenomenal field of its own?** This is the question Michał and Thomas care about, and the grain of coupling answers it. Binding contents into one field requires regulation that respects the grain of the vehicles it binds. The monastery couples across skulls only through speech, writing and action. These channels carry each monk's contents at the grain of report. So a monastery-wide binding is cognitive binding, like the way concepts bind their constituents. It is not experiential binding of co-present contents.

The United States does not depend on how the monastery comes out. By the time the hierarchy of integration reaches the nation, persons, households, communities and institutions have already used up the integration that matters. Little integration is left over at the national level. So the US lacks a mind of its own whatever the truth about small groups (§6).

The monastery thus becomes a positive prediction of the account rather than a counterexample. A monastery whose life is total enough may be minded, and if so it shares in its monks' experiences. It adds no field beyond theirs. Some collectives *do* qualify: those whose members relay fine-grained signals rather than report their own contents.

What makes this more than another round of terminology:

- It answers Thomas's challenge with paired constructions that differ in one feature each (§2).
- It does what Schwitzgebel said he could not do: apply a detailed theory of consciousness to the group cases, and draw no anti-nesting line along the way.
- It gives the scale distinction between monastery and nation an argument, residual integration, instead of an intuition.

## 2. The paired constructions

Thomas asked for examples that "satisfy some properties and fail others like mathematicians do." The essay should present these early, as a table, and refer back to them by label. Each pair differs in one feature.

| Pair | System X | System Y | Feature varied | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R | Labyrinth with valves: chambers fill and empty under pressure | Labyrinth with two pressure sensors and a mechanism that detects a failing sensor and switches to the other (Thomas's system E) | Whether a correction is computed from assessing a carrier's earlier state and returned to it | Y regulates, X only interacts causally. Neither is minded: nothing in either carries content that its dynamics answer to. This answers "what distinguishes regulation from ordinary causal interaction." |
| M1 | Aggregator monastery: monks deliberate alone, and an administrator tallies the results | Common-deliberation monastery: each contribution changes the context of the others, and assessments return to revise earlier commitments | Whether institution-level outputs return to modify the processes that produced them | Y is a candidate for mindedness; X is coordinated individual mentality. Thomas's monasteries A and B. |
| M2 | Autocrat monastery: the abbot decides, the rest comply | Chapter monastery: decisions emerge from structured disagreement among roles | Whether the institution adds an economy beyond one member's mind | X's apparent unity is borrowed from the abbot; Y's is the institution's own. |
| M3 | Living community: generations overlap, and shared understanding is maintained in continuous conversation | Archive-only lineage: each generation knows its predecessors only through the records | Whether the institutional carrier persists or is recomputed from a transcript | X may host one persisting mind. Y is a tradition: a succession of minded reconstructions, each a new carrier of the same type. This answers Thomas's question about a doctrine "preserved over centuries through written records." |
| M4 | Oracle monastery: a gifted brother imagines outcomes privately and announces them, and the chapter adopts or rejects the forecast | Role-simulating monastery: the cellarer imagines the coming year with the community's shared categories and granary records, and the chapter interrupts, corrects and extends his scenario while it runs | Whether the simulation runs inside the institutional economy or only delivers its output to it | X fails internality as the weather emulator does, inverted: the simulator is a mind, but its run is private. Y is institutional simulation by role-process overlap. |
| M5 | Monastery that keeps an Easter table copied from elsewhere | Monastery that computes the date itself: the computus, procedures and tables that track the 19-year lunar cycle and are corrected when the moon disagrees | Whether an artifact is consulted or run by the community's own procedure, with errors returning to revise it | Y simulates through an artifact procedure (Hutchins's distributed cognition), X only looks things up. Corrections also make the procedure accuracy-responsive at institutional level. |
| P1 | Total institution: the horarium, the Rule and common life shape most of each monk's attention | Thin membership: lay oblates, or citizens of a nation | How much of each member's experiential regulation is carried out by institution-role processes | X shares in many of its members' experiences, Y in few or none. Scale affects this step through the share of members' lives the institution reaches, not through size. |
| P2 | Report-coupled monastery: monks couple only through speech, writing and action | Neural-link monastery: monks are joined by thalamic bridges, as in the Hogan twins | The grain of coupling between members | Only Y is a candidate for an additional field. |
| P3 | Monastery: monks report their own contents | Block's Chinese Nation, Schwitzgebel's Antareans with ants of ant-level intelligence, Block's elementary-particle people | Whether members are *reporters* of their own contents or *relays* of the whole's fine-grained signals | Relay collectives can have experiential vehicles of their own, regulated at their own grain, and can be phenomenal while their members are too. Reporter collectives bind only conceptually. |
| P4 | Choir: each monk adjusts live to the others' sound | Choir: each monk sings along to a recording of the others | Whether the sound loop closes | Both produce coupled copies, not one shared occurrence. Coordination is not sharing. This pair heads off the tempting appeal to liturgy. |

Sample sentence: *Two monasteries can sing the same office, keep the same records and reach the same decisions, and still differ in whether anything decides.*

## 3. Sections

About 6,000 words; see question 4 below.

### §0 Opening (300)
- Start from Michał's claim that the theory lets a monastery be "a phenomenally conscious minded subject," and from his complaint that we ignore both Schwitzgebel and the question.
- Concede the omission plainly and pose the three questions.
- The scale distinction between Michał's monastery and Schwitzgebel's nation, and the claim that the monastery's experiences are its monks' (from *Can Two Minds Share*), are the author's own. They announce the plan.

### §1 Three questions, in their order of difficulty (500)
- Mindedness, shared experience, and an additional field.
- Why the second is small given the first: the belonging criterion.
- Why the third is separate: binding is what experiential regulation adds to minded regulation (*Experience/Selfhood*).
- The scale distinction belongs to steps 1 and 2, the grain distinction to step 3.

### §2 Regulation, not just interaction (600)
- Answer the labyrinth with pair R.
- Give minimal definitions of tracking, assessment and intervention in labyrinth terms.
- Explain why regulation without content is not mindedness.
- Present the table of pairs here.

### §3 Is the monastery minded? (1,800–2,400)
- **Revised 2026-10-17 at the author's direction:** M3 is now an invariance pair. For mindedness, persisting versus reconstructed institutional state is an implementation detail, by the stateful/state-passing translation Michał would raise: equally minded if the records carry what the conversation would. Persistence of carriers matters only for experience, which is the wedge used against the unfolding argument in *Experience, Selfhood*. §5 now uses it: the monastery's own states are consulted records and cannot carry a field.
- Apply the *Minds by Degrees* conditions to the institution as such: organizational unity, structured vehicles, accuracy-responsiveness, internal simulation as deliberation, internal use.
- **Simulation as the bottleneck** (the longest subsection):
  - The institution's own vehicles are declarative.
  - Three routes to simulation: delegation, role-process and artifact procedure (pairs M4, M5).
  - Distributed deliberation through discourse is simulation at report grain: stepwise, slow and conceptual. Its richness is borrowed from members at every step, much as chain-of-thought simulates through a narrow token channel.
  - Organizational learning research (Levitt and March; Ocasio on attention; Weick and Roberts on "collective mind"; Hutchins) gives psychology some traction on organizations. That supports the "kind of a science" test without settling it.
- Restate the "kind of a science" test: does psychology explain the institution better than sociology of its members does?
- Pairs M1–M3.
- The vehicles of institutional representations are partly monk mental states in their institutional roles and partly records. Records are consulted, not regulated, like a key–value cache. The persisting carriers live in the community's ongoing conversation.
- Link *What Persists When Agents Change*: an inquiry can outlive its participants.
- Literature to position against: List and Pettit on group agency, which supports institutional mindedness. Note that List (2018) argues that group agents lack phenomenal consciousness.
- Verdict: a monastery with common deliberation that is maintained continuously is a serious candidate. An aggregator, an autocracy or an archive-only lineage is not.

### §4 The monastery's experiences are its monks' (900)
- Make the belonging criterion concrete. An institution-role process running in a monk's brain, such as the internalized Rule shaping his attention at prayer, belongs both to his economy and to the monastery's. This is the overlap that *Can Two Minds Share* allows.
- Where that process takes part in regulating his experience, the occurrence belongs to both subjects.
- Pair P1: the share of a monk's experience the monastery reaches depends on how total the institution is.
- Pair P4 as the caution: mutual coordination through sound gives coupled copies, not shared occurrences. Shared ownership has to run through regulation that is common to both economies, not through a shared stimulus.
- Main argumentative risk: distinguishing a role-process that *partly constitutes* a monk's experience from one that merely *causes inputs* to it. The broadcast case in *Experience/Selfhood* gives the test: does the institutional process close a regulatory loop on the vehicle?

### §5 An additional field? (1,000)
- **Revised 2026-10-17 at the author's direction:** temporal grain is the conjectured decisive feature. The monastery's nowness, the span of its regulatory loops measured structurally as corrections per vehicle lifetime rather than wall-clock time, is too wide to regulate the truth-tracking of monks' experiences. Contrast truth-bearing (judgments, which persist long enough to be assessed) with truth-tracking (vehicles kept in step with their drivers moment by moment). Truth-tracking is conditional on its inputs, as in dreams and imagery. New pair P5 (immediate versus delayed neural bridges) isolates temporal grain from content grain. **Further revision:** P2 no longer contrasts speech with "neural bridges", which begged the question, since speech bridges neurons too. Channels are specified by reach (vehicles still under regulation versus finished outputs), latency (relative to vehicle lifetime), and bandwidth (relative to the distinctions regulation respects). Michał's objection is stated in the text, and the criterion is graded, with conversation at its low end.
- The grain of coupling.
- A monastery-wide field would need regulation that tracks and corrects vehicles in different skulls at their own grain. Speech and writing compress each monk's contents to the grain of report.
- This is no observer's choice: Thomas asked "who determines the grain?" The answer is the grain that the institution's own corrections respect, which is interaction-relative in the sense the Grain essay takes from the Thomas dialogues.
- Pairs P2 and P3. Relays versus reporters is the general form of the criterion. It explains why Block's elementary-particle people leave you conscious, why antheads can qualify, and why the monastery's binding is conceptual.
- Status of the monastery's binding: a real pattern, an institutional scene graph, and a cognitive achievement, but not an experiential occurrence.

### §6 The nation: residual integration (800)
- Argument: organization forms a hierarchy of integration (persons, households and teams, communities and institutions, states, the nation). At each level, the integration relevant to mindedness and to binding is what that level adds beyond its components' own integration and their coordination.
- By the national level, the tight loops have closed lower down. What remains is coupling between already integrated units through narrow, report-grain channels: law, markets, media, elections.
- So the residual cannot support a national mind, whatever the verdict on group minds at monastery scale. The US question is settled without settling the monastery.
- Precision: residual integration is close to IIT's minimum-partition measure: how much is lost under the cut that loses least, here along institutional boundaries. We keep the partition and drop the exclusion postulate. No rule forbids nested minds; the nation simply has too little integration of its own. Schwitzgebel's complaint against Tononi (§2) then does not apply.
- Residual integration also covers simulation. National-level simulation is done by agencies (budget projections, central-bank models, war games). The nation consumes their outputs, so its own simulation is residual too.
- Objections to address:
  - The hierarchy is not a tree: memberships overlap and cross-cut. Define the residual relative to the least-lossy decomposition, not one fixed ladder.
  - Channels that reach individuals directly, such as national media and markets, bypass the intermediate levels. They are still report-grain or scalar.
  - Prices as carriers that do not live in any citizen: a price is corrected by assessing its earlier value. This is the hardest case; state it as an open question.
- Answer Schwitzgebel's dumbing-down move (§3 of his paper): rabbit-level intelligence is not the issue, residual integration is.

### §7 What Schwitzgebel asked for (400)
- He wanted a detailed theory applied, and he showed that anti-nesting rules are ad hoc. Our account needs none.
- Verdicts on his cases:
  - Supersquids: phenomenal (relay coupling by light).
  - Antareans: phenomenal if the ants relay.
  - Block's elementary-particle people: you remain conscious.
  - The monastery: minded if its common life meets §3, shares in its monks' experiences, adds no field.
  - The US: no mind of its own, by residual integration.
- Of his three ways out, ours is none of the three. It is a selective materialism whose selection comes from the theory, not from intuitions about groups.

### §8 Closing (200)
- Return to Thomas's challenge and point to M1 and P2 as the requested monasteries.
- Name the remaining open case (prices) and the risk in §4.

## 6. Open questions for the author

1. **The positive claim.** Do we assert that a monastery of sufficiently common life *has experiences*, namely shared ownership of some of its monks' experiences? Your note suggests yes. It is the essay's boldest claim and invites the reply that it is cheap ownership. The answer is that ownership is cheap because the step is small, not because it is trivial.
2. **Is role-process overlap the right mechanism?** §4 treats institution-role processes in monk brains as components belonging to two economies. Is that how you see the monks' experiences being "for" the monastery, or do you have a different route in mind?
3. **The choir (P4).** Our own sharing essay implies coupled copies. Do you want to keep that, or explore whether a public, jointly regulated sound could be a vehicle in the monastery's economy?
4. **Length and form.** Decided: about 6,000 words, with room to grow to 8,000. The growth goes to §3's simulation subsection. Still open: should it be framed explicitly as a reply to Michał (the title could mirror his Scholastic "Whether…")?
