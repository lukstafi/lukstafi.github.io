**Yes—there have been substantial developments, but not a single successor doctrine that has displaced the 2007 picture.** My assessment is that the strongest progress has been in making OSR more specific: what deserves realist commitment in an effective theory; how structural identity can be formulated mathematically; and what distinguishes an objectively real pattern from a convenient interpretation of data. There has also been productive criticism of claims that physics establishes the *priority* of structure, rather than merely its indispensability. [University of Bristol](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/effective-ontic-structural-realism/)

For your interests, I would particularly prioritize **effective ontic structural realism**, the recent **real-patterns literature**, and the convergence around **homotopy type theory**. These provide more interesting connections to Wolfram Physics than the general observation that both reject an ontology of independently individuated little objects.

## 1. Effective OSR: probably the clearest direct continuation

The most immediately relevant paper is **James Ladyman and Lorenzo Lorenzetti, “Effective Ontic Structural Realism”**, available since 2023. It explicitly unifies three developments that had proceeded somewhat independently: realism about the common content of competing quantum interpretations; realism about effective quantum field theories; and realism about entities or structures retained from superseded theories. Its proposed unification combines modal structural realism with ETMG’s scale-relative ontology. [University of Bristol](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/effective-ontic-structural-realism/)

A significant precursor is **Porter Williams, “Scientific Realism Made Effective” (2019)**. Williams argues that taking effective quantum field theory seriously changes the realism debate: a theory need not describe the ultimate constitution of reality to describe real features of the world. Philosophical interpretation should engage with the effective theories physicists actually use, rather than postpone realism until an exact, final theory becomes available. [RCNi Company Limited](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1093/bjps/axx043)

Ladyman and Lorenzetti’s distinctive contribution is to treat these apparently different situations as variations on one problem. In their presentation, atoms, quantum fields, and Newtonian gravitational forces need not compete for membership in a single, exclusive inventory of ultimate constituents. A theory can correctly describe certain features within a restricted domain even when its unrestricted interpretation is false. This is not a license to rehabilitate every abandoned posit; the realist commitment concerns what the successful effective description actually captures. [BSPS](https://www.thebsps.org/short-reads/standard-model-ladyman-lorenzetti/)

**What is new relative to ETMG?** Not the idea of scale relativity itself. The advance is a more systematic account of how it handles several concrete problems together:

The historical question—“What survived theory change?”—and the contemporary question—“What is real in a theory known to be effective?”—are brought into the same framework. The quantum-interpretation problem adds another axis: some warranted commitments might survive changes of interpretation as well as changes of scale or successor theory.

My assessment is that this is a stronger development than another generic argument against intrinsic natures. It makes structural realism answerable to particular relationships between theories. It also fits especially well with an interest in **laws shared across classes of microscopic dynamics**, rather than realism about one preferred microscopic implementation.

## 2. Laws, symmetries, and the increasingly demanding meaning of “structure is prior”

### French’s systematic elaboration

**Steven French’s *The Structure of the World: Metaphysics and Representation* (2014)** is the obvious major post-ETMG monograph. Its central positive move is to articulate structure through **laws and symmetries understood modally**, rather than leave “structure” as a generic relational placeholder. It is also more willing than ETMG’s polemical opening might suggest to use resources from contemporary metaphysics in articulating the resulting position. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/6035)

The substantive issue is not whether physics uses mathematical structures—it plainly does—but how those structures distinguish possibilities, constrain instantiations, and support explanations. For a structural realist, a symmetry group or a law is not merely a compact description of the actual arrangement of things.

ETMG already demanded modality. French’s contribution is an extended attempt to specify what such a metaphysics looks like, including its representational commitments.

### McKenzie’s criticism is an important form of progress

I would pair French with **Kerry McKenzie**, particularly **“Structuralism in the Idiom of Determination” (2020; first published online in 2017)**.

McKenzie revisits her earlier attempt to understand structural priority through ontological dependence. Her conclusion is that dependence alone does not establish priority. Recasting the proposal in terms of *determination* exposes a further problem: the symmetry structures of physics do not determine the world’s inventory of fundamental kinds. Classifying or constraining possible particle kinds is not the same as establishing which kinds exist. Interestingly, she does not simply conclude that structuralism fails; she argues that the difficulty can reopen prospects for a different, more naturalistically motivated eliminativism. [RCNi Company Limited](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1093/bjps/axx061)

This sharpens an inference that can otherwise pass too easily:

> Objects cannot be characterized independently of structure; therefore structure completely determines the objects and is ontologically prior to them.

The first claim does not automatically establish the second. That is a real advance in diagnosing what an OSR argument must demonstrate.

McKenzie’s **“Structuralism as a Stance” (2024)** takes a further step. She argues that OSR is better understood as a methodological orientation than as a determinate thesis about the fundamental inventory of the world. One motivation is that structuralists tolerate disagreement over whether quantum fields should themselves count as objects or structures. Her proposed orientation foregrounds the mathematical character of physical representation. [Philosophy of Physics](https://philosophyofphysics.lse.ac.uk/articles/10.31389/pop.77)

This is **not** the discovery that philosophy can involve stances: ETMG already extensively develops that idea. The interesting difference is whether *OSR itself* should be understood primarily as a stance, rather than as a substantive metaphysical commitment adopted within a broader naturalistic stance. Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc… Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc…

**My assessment:** the later literature makes OSR less easy to express as one sweeping thesis, but more intellectually discriminating. “Everything is structural” now needs to be unpacked into claims about identity, individuation, dependence, determination, representation, and modality—which need not stand or fall together.

## 3. Formal foundations: from “we need a better framework” to actual alternatives

This is one area where there is unmistakable progress beyond ETMG’s explicit admission that conventional logic and set theory make its position difficult to articulate. Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc…

### Category theory: progress, but not an automatic ontological victory

**Jonathan Bain’s 2013 work on category-theoretic structure** prompted a substantial debate about whether category theory supports radical OSR. **Vincent Lam and Christian Wüthrich** argued against that inference; **Benjamin Eva’s “Category theory and physical structuralism” (2016)** defended important parts of Bain’s proposal. The disagreement concerns both the formal resources and their interpretation, not simply whether categories contain something called “objects.” [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2726)

The useful lesson is that categorical reformulation can expose what a theory preserves under transformation, while **the choice of mathematical presentation does not by itself settle the ontology**. In particular, a category’s objects may be whole models or structured spaces, not the physical individuals whose fundamentality is disputed. Conversely, mathematical arrows do not automatically become fundamental physical relations.

That debate improved the precision of OSR, but I would not present category theory as having proved that relations can exist without any ontologically significant relata.

### Homotopy type theory is the more distinctive development

Two especially relevant works are **James Ladyman and Stuart Presnell, “The Hole Argument in Homotopy Type Theory” (2020)**, and **Lu Chen, “Univalence and Ontic Structuralism” (2024)**. They connect structuralist questions about identity to a foundational framework in which identifications themselves have mathematical structure. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-019-00293-9)

Ladyman and Presnell work through an actual philosophical-physical problem: how to understand diffeomorphically related spacetime models. A particularly valuable feature is their caution about what does the work. Univalence alone does not magically settle the hole argument; the dependent representation of the manifold and its metric matters. Their analysis distinguishes ingredients that slogan-level appeals to “isomorphism as identity” can obscure. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-019-00293-9)

Chen develops univalent foundations as an implementation of ontic structuralism, including its treatment of identity and the objection that structuralism collapses physical structures into mathematical ones. The latter problem is not simply declared solved: the paper discusses strategies for representing physical distinctions within the framework and acknowledges that a fully developed account of physical modelling is needed. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-024-00768-4)

**What is genuinely new?** A structuralist can now point to worked formal treatments rather than merely ask readers to regard ordinary variables as ontologically innocent placeholders. But the achievement is chiefly one of **articulation and conceptual control**, not empirical confirmation that the world has one particular structural ontology.

This is also the most concrete formal connection to the Wolfram literature; I return to it below.

## 4. Spacetime emergence and dualities: more demanding tests of structuralism

ETMG already anticipated that spacetime might emerge from non-spatiotemporal structure. The subsequent **spacetime-functionalism** literature asks more precisely what would count as successfully recovering it.

The key entry is **Vincent Lam and Christian Wüthrich, “Spacetime is as spacetime does” (2018)**. Their proposal is that a quantum-gravity theory must recover the features of spacetime functionally relevant to empirical evidence, and explain how its underlying structures realize those roles. They discuss this in causal set theory and loop quantum gravity. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04374)

The improvement over “spacetime emerges” is a clearer explanatory obligation. Producing a structure with an appealing geometric interpretation is not enough; the account must explain why the structures support the relevant empirical organization. Conversely, it need not recover an additional, supposedly intrinsic “spacetime substance.”

**Dualities** have generated a parallel refinement. They make it tempting to identify reality with whatever is common to different formulations. But the philosophical literature increasingly insists on distinguishing a mathematical correspondence from an adequately interpreted physical equivalence. **Sebastian De Haro and Enrico Cinti’s *Dualities in Physics* (2026)** provides a current treatment and explicitly rejects the claim that dualities, by themselves, establish structural realism. A common-core strategy is congenial to structuralism, but additional interpretive and realist commitments are required. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/dualities-in-physics/45667B3C71A2C7A5B347748239243EC7)

For the comparison you have in mind, this is important: **equivalence of descriptions, equivalence of computational power, and equivalence of physical content are different achievements**.

## 5. Real patterns: a substantial continuation, including a new 2026 collection

There is now a particularly direct successor to the rainforest-realism side of ETMG:

**Tyler Millhouse, Stephen Petersen, and Don Ross, editors, *Dennett’s Real Patterns in Science and Nature* (MIT Press, March 2026).** It brings the programme into contact with high-level physical structure, biological classification, welfare economics, causal modelling, and neural networks. It is available open access through the publisher. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262052030/dennetts-real-patterns-in-science-and-nature/)

Ladyman contributes **“Patterns All the Way Up: Prolegomena to a Future Naturalized Metaphysics.”** One useful emphasis is **triangulation**: real scientific entities are tracked through different kinds of measurement and across domains, not just found in one privileged representation. The chapter also emphasizes the modal organization of relationships *between* domains and scales, and rejects the assumption that those relationships form one neatly ordered hierarchy. This extends ETMG’s programme rather than replacing it. [MIT Press Direct](https://direct.mit.edu/books/oa-edited-volume/6112/chapter/6036956/Patterns-All-the-Way-Up-Prolegomena-to-a-Future)

### The hard question has become: how much work is hidden in the interpretation?

Here **Tyler Millhouse’s “Compressibility and the Reality of Patterns” (2021)** and **“Really Real Patterns” (2022; online 2021)** are especially valuable.

The first challenges a straightforward inference from the simplicity of a high-level model to its being a compressed representation of the underlying system. Actual data-scientific practice involves feature construction and interpretation that complicate that inference. The second develops an approach sensitive to how difficult it is to interpret a physical system faithfully as exhibiting the proposed pattern. A simple model reached through an elaborate, tailor-made interpretation is not automatically evidence of a simple structure in the target. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/compressibility-and-the-reality-of-patterns/FA3010C3CAE8E32DCD91620B54DB3FA0)

ETMG already knew that compressibility was not sufficient for real patternhood, so the novelty is not merely adding that caveat. It is the more specific investigation of **where apparent patternhood comes from: the system, the representation, or the mapping between them**. Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc…

That matters particularly when a framework has very broad representational or computational resources. If almost anything can be encoded as almost anything else, showing that a desired pattern is *representable* is cheap. Showing that it is recoverable through an independently justified, non-gerrymandered interpretation is much more informative.

### A fresh application beyond fundamental physics

A concrete 2026 example is **Ladyman and Ryan Nefdt, “Are AI language models scientific models of language?”** They argue that LLMs can be good scientific models of aspects of language by capturing its **modal structure**, even without capturing all its causal structure or mechanisms. The claim is not that matching linguistic behaviour establishes identity with the mechanisms of human language. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13194-026-00770-9)

This illustrates a productive direction for structuralism: giving an account of **what a model can genuinely explain across differences in implementation**, rather than choosing between “the model reproduces the real mechanism” and “the model is merely instrumental.”

## What has not been settled

Some later developments sharpen ETMG’s problems rather than remove them. For example, the post-2007 literature on quantum discernibility develops results beyond the cases in the book, but weak discernibility itself was already explicitly discussed there. It should not be advertised as a new escape from the entire individuality debate. [RePub](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/15208) Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc…

More importantly, **Matthias Egg’s “Dissolving the measurement problem is not an option for the realist” (2019)** directly challenges ETMG’s proposed dissolution of that problem. Rejecting a traditional metaphysics of objects does not automatically remove the need to explain the relationship between quantum description and definite experimental outcomes. This remains a dispute, not a settled consequence of OSR. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1355219818301072)

So I would distinguish **improved structuralist accounts of what we can be realist about** from **a structuralist solution to every foundational problem in the underlying physics**.

# Connections to Wolfram Physics worth pursuing

The following are partly connections already present in publications and partly **my proposed synthesis**. The latter should not be mistaken for an established joint OSR–Wolfram programme.

## A. The strongest affinity is effective universality, not simply relational microstructure

For the version of Wolfram Physics you emphasize, the most interesting realist commitment need not be to one microscopic rule. It could concern the **effective modal organization shared across broad classes of rules, once appropriate observer conditions are specified**.

That is a natural application of effective OSR: the object of realist commitment is not necessarily the microscopic implementation, but the stable organization of possibilities and dependencies that survives relevant changes of implementation. The contemporary effective-realism literature supplies a philosophical framework for taking such organization seriously without demanding that it be the ultimate ontology. [University of Bristol](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/effective-ontic-structural-realism/)

The nontrivial step, however, is not the existence of translations between computationally universal systems. By analogy with the duality literature, the translations must preserve the structure relevant to the physical interpretation. **Simulation-equivalence alone does not establish observer-equivalence or physical equivalence.** My proposed bridge is therefore to ask which effective structures survive appropriately constrained changes of rule, encoding, and observer—not to demand selection of one privileged rule. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15866v2)

### A potentially useful missing intermediary: computational closure

An adjacent paper that deserves attention is **Fernando Rosas and colleagues, “Software in the natural world: A computational approach to hierarchical emergence” (2024 preprint)**.

It develops mathematical distinctions between informational, causal, and computational closure of macroscopic processes, using computational mechanics. One schematic informational-closure condition is

$$
I\!\left(Z_{>t};X_{\leq t}\mid Z_{\leq t}\right)=0,
$$

where \(X\) describes a microscopic process and \(Z\) a coarse-grained process. The condition says that, given the macro-history, additional micro-history contributes no further information about the macro-future. Crucially, predictive closure should not automatically be identified with interventionist causal autonomy. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09090v2)

**My suggested connection:** such tools could help distinguish an observer’s merely discarding distinctions from its identifying an autonomous effective process. They provide something more discriminating than “bounded observers coarse-grain.”

This is not itself an OSR paper, and its hierarchical vocabulary need not be adopted as a universal metaphysics. Nor does closure within one stochastic model establish universality across microscopic rules. But it provides a plausible intermediate mathematical target between microscopic computation and claims about robust observer-level laws.

## B. Real patterns can supply an ontology of observers—not just their observations

ETMG’s relevant notion of objectivity is not independence from every possible perspective. Its proposed real patterns must be projectible by physically possible devices, and their reality can be scale-relative. The important contrast is between a pattern being accessible only under certain physical conditions and its being an artefact of someone’s current descriptive convenience. Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc… Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc…

That suggests a stronger connection to observer theory than “reality depends on the observer”:

**The observer itself should qualify as a real pattern under the same standards applied to the patterns it observes.**

This is my proposed OSR reading. Observer boundaries, memory, persistence, and information-processing capacities should not be granted an unexplained ontological privilege. They must be stable enough under relevant variations to support the explanatory role assigned to them. Wolfram’s observer-theoretic emphasis on computational boundedness and persistence makes precisely those features important to characterize. [writings.stephenwolfram.com](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2023/12/observer-theory/)

Ladyman’s recent emphasis on triangulation suggests a useful further test: does a proposed observer or effective entity persist as a target across independently motivated ways of measuring or reconstructing it? That could help separate robust observer-relative organization from patterns that appear only after a specially chosen decoding. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384627756_Patterns_All_The_Way_Up_Prolegomena_to_a_Future_Naturalised_Metaphysics)

There is now some **explicit contact** in the literature. **Hatem Elshatlawy, Dean Rickles, and Xerxes Arsiwalla’s *Towards a Generalized Theory of Observers***—first posted in 2025 and revised in January 2026—engages structural realism and cites ETMG. I would treat it as exploratory bridge-building, rather than as a completed synthesis or an established solution to observer dependence. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16225)

### Logical depth is an especially relevant detail in ETMG

The book does not discuss informational economy only in terms of short descriptions. It explicitly invokes **Bennett’s logical depth**, involving the computation required to generate a structure from a near-minimal description. This makes its relationship to computational irreducibility more interesting than a simple opposition between “the world is compressible” and “the world is irreducible.” Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc…

A short generative description, expensive reconstruction, and a tractable effective description concern different things. The important question for the synthesis is therefore **which observables admit useful predictive compression**, not whether the full microscopic evolution admits a shortcut.

Millhouse’s criticism then becomes directly relevant: apparent simplicity should not be purchased by hiding the difficult computation inside the observation map or interpreter. That is a promising way to make “a pocket of reducibility accessible to an observer” a more demanding claim than “some decoding produces a simple sequence.” [santafe.edu](https://www.santafe.edu/news-center/news/research-brief-really-real-patterns)

## C. HoTT is a concrete point of convergence—but the physical meaning of identification matters

There is a direct mathematical counterpart to the OSR work discussed above:

**Xerxes Arsiwalla and Jonathan Gorard, “Pregeometric Spaces from Wolfram Model Rewriting Systems as Homotopy Types” (2024; preprint 2021).**

The paper develops connections between multiway rewriting systems, higher categorical structures, homotopy types, and synthetic geometry. It explicitly discusses the role of univalence in identifying states, paths, and multiway-system types. This puts the Wolfram programme and the Ladyman–Presnell/Chen structuralist programme in contact with the same foundational resources. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10773-024-05576-0)

The connection I would emphasize is **not** “HoTT proves OSR, and therefore supports Wolfram Physics.” It is that both programmes must make principled decisions about **which differences in representation correspond to genuine differences**.

A useful comparison would distinguish three operations: removing arbitrary labels; relating alternative histories; and relating different computational rules. These need not have the same physical interpretation. A higher identification appropriate to one purpose cannot simply be assumed appropriate to all the others.

The philosophical contribution of the OSR/HoTT literature is to make the representation of these distinctions explicit. Ladyman and Presnell’s warning that univalence alone does not settle the hole argument is instructive here: the relevant structured types and their interpretation must first be specified. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-019-00293-9)

Thus, the shared formalism is a promising research interface, but the **choice and physical justification of the identifications** is where much of the substantive work remains.

## D. OSR does not by itself license the Ruliad’s full ontological interpretation

Here there is a significant difference that broad statements of affinity can conceal.

ETMG distinguishes **mathematical structures used in representation** from **real patterns represented by them**. It also explicitly restricts its criterion of reality to physically possible perspectives, rather than to whatever is logically or mathematically possible. Consequently, its structural realism is not the thesis that every mathematically describable structure is physically real. Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc… Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc…

Wolfram’s stronger ontological interpretation goes further. In **“What Ultimately Is There? Metaphysics and the Ruliad” (2026)**, he distinguishes the Ruliad’s abstract inevitability from the hypothesis that it describes the infrastructure of our physical universe, and discusses how observer conditions connect that infrastructure to experienced laws. [Wolfram Institute](https://wolframinstitute.org/output/what-ultimately-is-there-metaphysics-and-the-ruliad)

My assessment is that OSR can be used to interpret and evaluate this programme, but **does not independently entail it**. In particular, it does not by itself establish that all computational possibilities belong to physical ontology, or that the observed modal organization follows from the proposed observer conditions.

Again, the relevant demand is **not** “tell us which microscopic rule is ours.” It is: *show what restricted, empirically consequential structure follows across the relevant rule and observer classes*. That is where the universal construction can acquire explanatory specificity.

There is also a smaller but revealing issue about **emes**. Wolfram’s 2026 discussion calls them elementary units of identity, while also presenting decomposition into such units as a convenient way of thinking about the structure. [Wolfram Institute](https://wolframinstitute.org/output/what-ultimately-is-there-metaphysics-and-the-ruliad)

From an ETMG perspective, the key question is whether those identities are **structural positions or representational bookkeeping**, or whether they are primitive individuators on which relations subsequently depend. Merely having distinct nodes is not a problem: ETMG explicitly allows structures with symmetries and does not require every position to possess a unique qualitative description. The issue is the explanatory status assigned to their distinctness. Every_Thing_Must_Go-Ontic_Struc…

## Where I would start reading

For **the most direct update to ETMG**, I would start with **“Effective Ontic Structural Realism”**, then Ladyman’s **“Patterns All the Way Up”** in the **2026 real-patterns collection**. These keep the book’s whole project in view, rather than isolating the objects-versus-relations dispute.

For **the most productive challenge to that project**, I would read **Millhouse’s two papers** alongside **McKenzie’s “Structuralism in the Idiom of Determination” and “Structuralism as a Stance.”** They expose where a structuralist account needs more than successful formal representation.

For **the strongest technical connection to Wolfram Physics**, I would pair **Ladyman–Presnell and Lu Chen** with **Arsiwalla–Gorard**, and add **Rosas et al.** for the separate problem of effective computational autonomy. French’s **2014 monograph** remains the substantial book-length elaboration of OSR; **Ross, Ladyman, and Harold Kincaid’s *Scientific Metaphysics* (2013)** is the broader continuation of ETMG’s naturalization programme, including Ladyman and Ross’s chapter **“The World in the Data.”** [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/5075)

**My overall judgment:** the most promising post-2007 direction is not a still more radical declaration that “only structure exists.” It is an account of **which structures are objectively recoverable, which survive changes of scale and realization, how observers belong to those structures, and what justifies treating their modal organization as real**. That is also where I see the deepest potential connection to Wolfram Physics: not at the level of a shared relational vocabulary, but in a scientifically discriminating theory of **observer-accessible universality**.
