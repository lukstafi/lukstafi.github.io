## Thesis

A credence about a settled mathematical fact is what a reasoner has in the interval before its search settles the fact. Three answers say what makes such a credence right: it follows from a good **prior** over the computation; no **adversary** from a bounded class of tests can exploit it; or it **presumes** independence and normality except where structure has been found. Each fails where another succeeds. Logical induction is the adversarial answer presented as a bookmaker, and the market is its presentation, not its substance. What the answers share bears on self-improving AI: improvement without external evidence is logical learning, and it is sound exactly as far as something independent of the learner's beliefs settles the bets.

What makes this more than a tour:

1. **Three answers, one per earlier essay.** Prior ↔ the ontology essay's code; adversary ↔ the bookmaker; presumption ↔ *Presumed Normal*. The frame organizes the literature and argues that the answers must be combined.
2. **The market is a presentation.** The ingredients that matter are pricing the underived, a fixed point for self-reference (continuity), and aggregating a countable class of tests. Defensive forecasting and multicalibration have them without a market; a truth maintenance system lacks all three.
3. **Explosion becomes a price.** Credences do not explode; incoherence is measured by what a Dutch book or an arbitrageur extracts. *Presumed Normal*'s weighted abnormalities and the bookmaker's arbitrage are two ways of charging for an inconsistency.
4. **Settlement, not externality.** Refines *The Given and the Found*'s givenness: what licenses self-improvement is a check independent of the beliefs being trained, internal or not. AlphaZero (exact rules) → MuZero (learned model) → OPST (agreement only) is the scale.

Prior statements to credit (from the novelty searches): Barak's 2015 post "A different type of pseudo" already calls pseudorandomness a computational analogue of frequentist probability and SoS pseudo-distributions one of Bayesian probability, opening on a digit of π; ARC's 2024 "Towards a Law of Iterated Expectations for Heuristic Estimators" applies multiaccuracy to mathematical estimates and cites logical induction; Kosoy (2017, 2021) names defensive forecasting a precursor of logical induction. Apparently unclaimed: joining outcome indistinguishability to logical induction; the three-way comparison of logical induction, defensive forecasting and multicalibration; an adaptive logic whose abnormalities are dependences; the CLuN/MLN glut bridge; self-distillation framed as logical learning with a settlement criterion.

- "A logically omniscient reasoner has nothing to learn from itself."

### §1 Why Bayes cannot price a theorem
Logical omniscience: a coherent prior gives every theorem probability 1. The repairs, each in a sentence or two:
- Hacking 1967, "Slightly more realistic personal probability": personal possibility. "For me, when is a proposition possible? When I do not know it to be false." Sentences, not propositions, carry personal probability.
- Garber 1983: throw "h ⊢ e" into the language "as atomic sentences, unanalyzed and unanalyzable wholes".
- I. J. Good: evolving probability (1968), dynamic probability (1977, with computer chess), Type II rationality (1962, 1971): expected utility net of the cost of deliberation.
- Gaifman 2004: "The restriction on resources is expressed in the framework by a restriction to some finite set of sentences"; the probability is partial, and the axioms hold within its domain. He does not bound proof length, because bounded implication is not transitive.
- Seidenfeld, Schervish & Kadane 2012 (J. Phil. 109, 516–533): three strategies, Good's Statistician's Stooge, changing closure conditions, and rates of incoherence as a guide. The third is the bridge to §5.
- Weatherson 2003: probability relative to any logic's consequence relation. Used again in §5.
- The game-semantic repair: Hintikka's surface versus depth information (1970) and Rantala's urn models (1975), where the domain can change between the draws of the semantic game, so a model can be inconsistent only beyond the quantifier depth anyone has examined (Hintikka, "Impossible possible worlds vindicated", 1975). Gaifman bounds the set of sentences in view; urn models bound depth. Limit, per Jago 2006: under Rantala's semantics agents still know every propositional tautology, so urn models remove omniscience only for quantificational depth.

What they buy: permission for intermediate credences. What they lack: an account of how credences should move as computation proceeds, and why one in ten.

### §2 A bookmaker over sentences
Logical induction (Garrabrant, Benson-Tilsen, Critch, Soares & Taylor 2016), told in the bookmaker essay's vocabulary. Traders are polynomial-time and continuous in prices ("continuous piecewise-rational functions of the market prices"); a deductive process settles sentences one by one; the criterion (Def. 3.0.1) is that "there is no efficiently computable trader T that exploits P relative to D", where exploiting means plausible net worth bounded below but not above. Construction: an approximate Brouwer fixed point at which the combined trader's demand vanishes; all efficiently computable traders enumerated with budgets and combined with **fixed** weights 2^(−k−b), a trader's influence cut off when its losses exceed its budget. The bookmaker essay's "wealth is a kind of memory" (Hedge over traders) is therefore an analogy here, not the construction; say so.
- The combinatorial wall, one level up: exact coherence over sentences is undecidable, worse than #P. Logical induction sidesteps it as the 2026 venues did: coherent as far as bounded arbitrageurs can tell. Discipline comes from consistency with what has been proved, not only from sentences settling: the paper's exploitation "rewards traders for arbitrage, even if they arbitrage between sentences that never 'pay out'." Polymarket's arbitrage is incoherence priced in dollars, but nearly all of it the simplest kind (exclusive outcomes not summing to one); across logically linked markets it was about $0.1M of $39.6M. That smallness is itself a point: bounded arbitrageurs find little of the structured incoherence.
- Results to show: Limit Coherence, Provability Induction, Learning Pseudorandom Frequencies (the one-in-ten answer; abstract: "≈ 10% probability to 'the nth digit of π is a 7'"), Introspection, Self-Trust.
- Limits: convergence rates are not merely unproved but uncomputable (Prop. 5.5.1); not runnable in practice.
- **Box: why MIRI wanted it.** The agenda (Soares & Fallenstein, released 2014) lists "Highly Reliable Agent Designs: Realistic World-Models, Decision Theory, Logical Uncertainty, Vingean Reflection". Embedded agency (Demski & Garrabrant, sequence 2018, arXiv 2019): an agent's own code and outputs are determined computations it cannot evaluate, and logical induction weakens Dutch books to "bets based on quickly computable parts of the tree". Decision theory needs logical counterfactuals ("not yet well understood", the agenda): counterfactual mugging on "the 10000-th digit of pi" (Nesov 2009), five-and-ten (Fallenstein 2013 technical note). Logical induction did not supply them: Garrabrant 2017, "Two Major Obstacles for Logical Inductor Decision Theory", gives a logical-inductor agent that converges to taking the 5. Vingean reflection and the Löbian obstacle (Yudkowsky & Herreshoff 2013); probabilistic reflection by a Kakutani fixed point (Christiano, Yudkowsky, Herreshoff & Barasz 2013); logical inductors trust their future selves. After: radical probabilism (Demski 2020: "treating logical induction as merely a specific way to flesh out Jeffrey's theory"); MIRI dropped its undisclosed 2017 research directions (Dec 2020), put policy and communications first (Jan 2024), and its agent-foundations team, Garrabrant included, left in June 2024; Christiano's line runs into ARC.

### §3 The unsatisfiable circularity: what is special about markets
The user's question: would any truth maintenance system do? A TMS (Doyle 1979; de Kleer 1986) is bookkeeping over derivations already made: the deductive process's job, and *Presumed Normal*'s conditions are ATMS labels in another dress. It lacks three ingredients:
1. Pricing what is not derived, and learning patterns across sentences. Laskey & Lehner 1989 show ATMS with probabilities on assumptions equivalent to Dempster–Shafer belief; that propagates a prior and does not learn from which sentences turn out provable.
2. A fixed point for self-reference. For χ ↔ P(χ) < 0.5: Doyle's TMS meets an "unsatisfiable circularity" ("the TMS must make F in if and only if it makes F out") and loops (the textbook name "odd loop" is Charniak, Riesbeck & McDermott's, 1980); in ASP `p :- not p.` has no stable model; a logical inductor prices the sequence of such sentences toward 0.5 (Paradox Resistance, Thm 4.11.2). The paper: "We attribute the success of logical inductors in the face of paradox to the continuity conditions." The sharpest small example in the essay.
3. Aggregating every efficient test, via budgets.

The non-market twins: Shafer & Vovk's game-theoretic probability (2001), probability as a price Skeptic cannot exploit; defensive forecasting (Vovk, Takemura & Shafer 2005): "for any continuous strategy for Skeptic there exists a strategy for Forecaster that does not allow Skeptic's capital to grow", by choosing a zero of a continuous function, the one-dimensional form of logical induction's Brouwer step, with the same remark that continuous tests "cannot sharply distinguish between the cases p_n < 0.5 and p_n ≥ 0.5"; multicalibration (Hébert-Johnson, Kim, Reingold & Rothblum 2018), a repair loop that "corrects the candidate values of some subset that violates calibration", the same find-a-violated-constraint move as the bookmaker essay's ellipsoid separation and Frank–Wolfe oracle. Lineage: the logical induction paper cites none of Shafer, Vovk, Foster or Vohra; its named market precedent is Beygelzimer, Langford & Pennock 2012 (Kelly bettors). Kosoy (2017; comment 2021) calls defensive forecasting a precursor. Perdomo & Recht 2025 and Noarov & Roth 2026 connect defensive forecasting to OI and multicalibration without logical induction. The three-way comparison appears to be new.

What a market adds beyond the theorems: one shared state (unity), wealth as memory (as analogy, see §2), the relay. Architecture that falls out: a TMS layer (adaptive logic, *Self* with provenance) for what is derived and on what conditions; a pricing layer for everything else.

### §4 Three answers
**Spine: one researcher's path through two answers.** Demski 2012, the prior answer (random consistent theories; approximable only in the limit, and "intended to show that the distribution is asymptotically computable, not to offer an efficient approximation"; it cannot learn beyond its axioms: "P_L will not learn new axioms of number theory. It is limited to the incompleteness of whatever axioms it is given", which is *The Given and the Found*'s demarcation in 2012 form; check these against the published text). Sawin & Demski 2013 (MIRI workshop July 2013, report Dec 2014): computable approximations that converge on believing true Π1 sentences must disbelieve some true Π2 sentences, which bounds exactly the 2012 hope that ∀x.S[x] rises with its instances. Garrabrant's asymptotic logical uncertainty, "a modification to the Demski prior", then logical induction (2016), the adversary answer, which keeps computable approximability, non-dogmatism and approximate coherence and gives up Gaifman inductivity, the property the 2013 theorem rules out (logical induction paper, §1.1). Demski's "Radical Probabilism" (2020) reads the result as Jeffrey's. The presumption answer (ARC) comes from Christiano, whose 2014 write-up had ranked Demski's prior closest to satisfactory among coherent priors.

- **Prior.** Demski 2012; Gaifman; Hutter, Lloyd, Ng & Uther 2013 (which admits no computable approximation, per Sawin & Demski, as the logical induction paper notes); Solomonoff-style priors over sentences; the ontology essay's two-part code as a prior. **Probabilistic numerics** (Diaconis 1988; O'Hagan 1991; Hennig, Osborne & Girolami 2015; Cockayne, Oates, Sullivan & Girolami 2019) is the version that ships: narrow, calibrated, measurable.
- **Adversary.** Logical induction; pseudorandomness and pseudoentropy (Barak, Shaltiel & Wigderson 2003); multicalibration and outcome indistinguishability (Dwork, Kim, Reingold, Rothblum & Yona 2021), which keep individual "probabilities" in scare quotes and allow that "Perhaps Nature is deterministic, but we do not have enough information or computing resources to carry out the predictions ourselves" (Dawid's "On individual risk" is their reference point); Foster & Vohra's calibration (1997, 1998), linking to the bookmaker's no-regret identity; sum-of-squares pseudo-distributions as the uncertainty of "a computationally bounded observer", with "no computationally easy dutch book strategy" (Barak & Steurer's unfinished notes). Credit Barak's 2015 post for the frequentist/Bayesian pairing.
- **Presumption.** Cramér's random model of the primes (1936); Hardy–Littlewood (1923); ARC, "Formalizing the presumption of independence" (Christiano, Neyman & Xu 2022): "it is not clear how to formalize this kind of defeasible reasoning". Defaults in *Presumed Normal*'s sense: independence until a defeater exposes structure.

**The adversary answer splits along two axes.**
- *Where the adversary sits.* Internal tests (logical induction, multicalibration) are enumerated and run by the believer, which must out-compute each of them. Dwork, Lee, Lin & Tankala 2023 prove "the first relative lower bound on the size of multiaccurate predictors", refuting "'dream' predictors that are more efficient than functions in C" (for predictors that use the tests as black boxes, as all known algorithms do). External, stronger adversaries extend what a bounded believer can justify: IP = PSPACE (Shamir 1992; Lund, Fortnow, Karloff & Nisan 1992); "debate with optimal play can answer any question in PSPACE given polynomial time judges" (Irving, Christiano & Amodei 2018); cross-examination "gives debate a boost from PSPACE to NEXP" (Barnes & Christiano 2020 write-up; proof only in a linked document). A real prediction market is the external case disciplined by payment (elicitation): the bookmaker learns from traders it cannot simulate. Logical induction is that market with the traders taken inside.
- *What disciplines it.* Settlement over time: bets bind as sentences resolve, or (in logical induction) as prices must stay consistent with what has been proved; guarantees are aggregate (calibration, bounded loss), and a logical inductor can be badly wrong on any one sentence. Checks on demand: an interactive proof needs no outcome, the verifier spot-checks a local step now, and soundness is per instance, which the adversary answer cannot give.

| | disciplined by settlement over time | disciplined by checks on demand |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **internal adversary** | logical induction, multicalibration, defensive forecasting | self-play under exact rules (AlphaZero); proof search with a checker |
| **external adversary** | prediction markets, scoring-rule elicitation | interactive proofs, PCPs, debate |

OPST fits no cell: agreement among copies of one model is neither an independent outcome nor a check a liar can fail. A *trusted* smarter oracle is a different case: its answer is testimony, and logical uncertainty becomes empirical uncertainty about a source's reliability (the ontology essay's Prov(T, P)). Debate is therefore not a fourth answer; it is the external, checked corner of the adversary answer.

**The corners compose.** The obfuscated-arguments problem (Barnes 2020, with Christiano): "a dishonest debater can often create arguments that have a fatal error, but where it is very hard to locate the error." Prover–estimator debate (Brown-Cohen, Irving & Piliouras, arXiv 2506.13609; v2, July 2026, adds Chen, Li & Xun and fixes v1's main theorem): "The estimator (Bob) has the sole task of assigning probabilities to the prover's subclaims. The prover uses these probabilities to decide which subclaim to recursively debate further." The estimator's guarantee "is known as outcome indistinguishability". So debate reduces a hard claim to checkable steps, and calibration prices the steps nobody can afford to check: the internal-adversary answer at the leaves, stated by the authors.

ARC places the presumption answer against both others. On logical induction, such systems "would not revise the probability they assign to the twin prime conjecture after noticing the heuristic argument … although they would eventually learn to trust these arguments after observing them producing good predictions in practice"; and "Heuristic estimators can be viewed as a type of interactive proof system with very weak guarantees." Their motive is machine learning ("Avoiding catastrophic failures", "Eliciting latent knowledge"); mechanistic anomaly detection is Hilton's later framing (2024).

**Game-theoretic semantics.** Two traditions share the word "game-theoretic": semantics (truth as a winning strategy for Verifier) and probability (a price no gambler exploits; logical induction belongs here). Where semantics offers something: equilibrium semantics for IF logic (Sevenster & Sandu 2010; Mann, Sandu & Sevenster 2011), where mixed strategies give a sentence a value in [0, 1], from imperfect information rather than bounded computation, on finite models, and Galliani & Mann's lottery semantics (2013) makes those values compositional; Japaridze's computability logic and clarithmetic (I: polynomial time, II: polynomial space), where truth is a bounded algorithmic winning strategy; Giles's game for Łukasiewicz logic (1974; Fermüller & Metcalfe 2009), which joins evaluation games with betting. The open point narrows: a logical inductor prices sentences atomically and learns their relations, coherent only in the limit. Pallavi Sudhir & Tran-Thanh (2024, "Betting on what is neither verifiable nor falsifiable") let polynomial-time agents trade on a Hintikka Verifier–Falsifier game over arithmetic, as an explicit alternative to pricing sentences atomically, and leave a compositional measure for future work. Credences defined compositionally as the value of an evaluation game between complexity-bounded players on infinite structures still look open.

Each answer fails where another succeeds: no general prior is computable; adversarial definitions guarantee without explaining (a logical inductor's price is right without a reason, and its rates are uncomputable); presumptions explain, but no one has a formal account of when they are defeated (ARC says so). Attribute ARC's motive only to what they wrote.

Possibly a short paragraph on language models: their credences on mathematical claims were trained by the log score, the bookmaker's rule; the adversarial answer gives an evaluation (good insofar as no cheap test exploits them). Coda-sized; §6 carries the AI stake.

### §5 What explosion becomes
Paraconsistent logic and logical uncertainty answer different problems (inconsistent premises; consistent premises with uncomputed consequences). Where they meet:
- **Credences do not explode.** 0.6 in p and 0.6 in ¬p makes nothing certain. Exploitability replaces explosion; incoherence is measured by the rate at which an incoherent bookie can be made a sure loser (Schervish, Seidenfeld & Kadane, "Measuring incoherence", Sankhyā 2002; Staffel 2015 compares measures); for a market, arbitrage profit. An adaptive logic localizes a contradiction and withdraws; a market localizes it and charges.
- **The bridge already exists.** *Presumed Normal*'s box: `true(A)`, `neg(A)` plus weights e^{−wk} give P(A) + P(∼A) = 1 + P(both) > 1 for finite w, a probability with gluts; w is the strength of the presumption of consistency, and w → ∞ recovers Minimal Abnormality.
- **Cheap logics against omniscience.** Levesque 1984: explicit belief closed under relevance-logic (tautological) entailment, via Belnap's four-valued semantics; for propositional belief base and query both in CNF, classical implication is co-NP-complete while tautological entailment is O(mn). Patel-Schneider (1986, 1989) used four-valued semantics for tractable description logics, citing Levesque. With Weatherson's recipe: coherence with respect to the four-valued logic as the coherence a bounded agent can afford. The adaptive-logic shape: cheap logic as lower limit, classical coherence as upper limit approached by stages.
- **Gaps and gluts.** Belnap–Dunn probabilities (Dunn 2010: "too little information (ignorance)" versus "too much information (conflicted)"; Klein, Majer & Rafiee Rad 2021: P(φ) and P(¬φ) "need not sum up to 1", gaps and gluts at once, with Bayesian and Jeffrey updating). Distinguishes "not examined" from "a heuristic says yes and a failed proof suggests no"; a logical inductor's single price conflates them. Klein et al. motivate gluts by the NP-hardness of consistency checking, the bounded-agent case. NARS's frequency–confidence pair is a relative. Paraconsistent probability proper (Mares 1997†, text not seen; Bueno-Soler & Carnielli 2016) aims at contradictory evidence, not computation.
- **Presumed independent.** A Markov logic network with maximum-likelihood weights is the maximum-entropy distribution matching its formulas' expected counts (Richardson & Domingos 2006, after Della Pietra et al.); with no formulas the distribution is uniform and the ground atoms independent (follows from the formula). So the box's machinery is a presumption-of-independence engine, each formula a found structure. Klein et al. observe that Dunn's four-valued probability already presumes all events independent. Prior art to cite: Grosof 1986, "Maximization of Conditional Independence" as a default, by circumscription; max-ent as a nonmonotonic logic (Goldszmidt, Morris & Pearl 1993); random worlds with "default assumptions of independence" (Bacchus, Grove, Halpern & Koller). Batens's adaptive logics of inductive generalization presume uniformity, the nearest adaptive analogue. An adaptive logic whose abnormalities are dependences appears unclaimed. Kinship with ARC, not identity (theirs is about explaining computations). Callback: the parlay dealers presume independence; the correlation-aware dealer has defeaters.
- **Doubt about consistency.** A logical inductor prices Con(PA), but if the theory were inconsistent its deductive process would prove everything and prices would explode in the limit. A paraconsistent base keeps the reasoner useful. Rarely matters for mathematics; matters for *Self*.

### §6 Learning without evidence
- A logically omniscient Bayesian cannot learn from itself. Good, Garber, logical induction and radical probabilism all describe belief change by thought; Lombrozo's "Learning by thinking in natural and artificial minds" (TiCS 2024) is the cognitive-science framing. Conversely, gains from self-distillation show the model was not logically omniscient (Lampinen's reversals, link *Who Teaches the Weights?*; Lampinen et al. also find fine-tuning can "even exceed ICL generalization, by spending more train time compute to augment the training dataset using in-context reasoning"). Self-distillation is amortized logical learning: slow derivations in context compiled into the fast prior of the weights (amortization, link *How Does a Policy Improve?* l. 161). Usable information (Xu et al. 2020: "V-information can be created through computation") is the formal version, and §4's adversarial answer in another dress: information relative to a bounded predictive family. Barak & Steurer say the same of SoS: "Despite learning no new information, as we invest more computation time, the algorithm reduces uncertainty in the beliefs".
- **Settlement, not externality.** *The Given and the Found* said grounding sets an update's sign. The refinement: a check grounds an update when it is independent of the beliefs being trained, whether or not it is external. The scale:
  - AlphaZero: the rules are exact; self-play outcomes settle value predictions whatever the network believes. Value of a position is a logical fact, so this is logical learning (link, no retelling).
  - MuZero: the model is learned; search settles against a simulator that may be wrong ("more search within the same erroneous simulator can intensify it"; *The Given and the Found* on MuZero's off-policy decay).
  - OPST: no model, only agreement. In bookmaker terms, a market that never resolves, with traders paid for agreeing. Coherence is necessary, not sufficient: a coherent wrong completion passes every consistency filter, like the ontology essay's coherent myth. Its MMLU gains belong to the middle case (latent knowledge made usable), as the authors say. Huang, Block, Foster et al. (2024) model self-improvement as "sharpening" with the model as its own verifier; nothing found ties self-distillation to logical induction or to a settlement criterion.
  - Read against §4's table: AlphaZero is internal and checked; OPST is internal with no independent check. The ways out are an internal check independent of the beliefs (exact rules, a proof checker) or an external one (a verifier, a debate a liar loses, a trusted source). Interactive proofs show a bounded learner can get an independent check without computing the fact itself.
- **Where logical induction sits in *The Given and the Found*'s demarcation.** The condition (axioms, rules) is given; the regularities of its consequences are found. Logical induction is induction over a given deductive closure: it amortizes search over a given condition by learning patterns, and does not discover the condition. Keeps §7 of that essay intact.
