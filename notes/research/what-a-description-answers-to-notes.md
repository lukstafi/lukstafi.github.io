# Notes carried over from the draft of *What a Description Answers To*

These notes stood at the end of the essay's draft (`notes/what-a-description-answers-to.md`) and were moved here on 23 Nov 2026, when the essay was published. They record what was still to verify, which sentences the author has not confirmed in these words, and what was left out. The outline is `structural-realism-essay-outline.md` and the dossier is `structural-realism-variants-dossier.md`.

Draft of 17 Nov 2026, from the outline. Revised with the author on 22 and 23 Nov 2026.

## To verify

Status as of publication is given in the last section.

1. Life run inside Life: the unit-cell constructions (the OTCA metapixel and its relatives), their size, and whether the emulation is exact. The opening and two later sections rest on it.
2. The total-cholesterol example, usually credited to Spirtes and Scheines (2004).
3. Wolfram's wording, checked against the Ruliad essay on 22 Nov (not yet quoted in the text). Frames: each kind of computational system "can be thought of as defining a different coordinate system for describing the ruliad"; "there are many reference frames in which to experience the ruliad—but it's always the same ruliad underneath". Uniqueness: "while the ruliad is unique, the description of it is not"; "the unique limiting structure formed by following all possible rules in all possible ways". Access: "We never get to see the full ruliad; we just sample tiny parts of it"; "as soon as you try to look at 'what's in the ruliad', you have to parametrize or coordinatize it". Mathematics (section "The View from Mathematics"): "Of course there are many mathematical details missing here"; "in the end the ruliad actually seems to be the ∞-category of ∞-groupoids"; Grothendieck's hypothesis gives "a general claim that the ruliad must have 'inevitable geometry'". ## Claims the author has not confirmed in these words

- That the family of interventions sets the tolerance for approximate abstraction.
- That simplicity of a map is evidence of stability, with stability as the criterion.
- The reply to Franklin-Hall (admissibility and the system's own interactions are testable facts, not naturalness).
- The answer to the first half of Ladyman and Ross's warning about Life, and the concession of the second half.
- Section three's sentence on sets of occurrences once ended "weighted by whatever measure turns out to apply", for the author's "reduces into sets at the base level, or what the reality fluid measure turns out to be concerned with". Revised 23 Nov; see the note on measure below.
- The three-dimensional account of unity is the version accepted on 15 Nov; the examples (network on a processor, nation, heap) are Claude's.
- The reply to van Fraassen, expanded on 22 Nov: that the essay accepts what his second horn demands (something that is not structure) and denies the bearer; that a bearer is "what it is apart from" its structure; "The world's structure, then, is always the structure of generation" (the outline's reading of the author's position, marked "seems" there; scoped to the world's structure on 22 Nov at the author's request, so that it is anti-Platonist and not sceptical about mathematics); "Mathematics studies structures whether or not anything answers to them, and some may be structures that nothing could answer to. To describe a structure is not to show that it can be generated." The author, 22 Nov: mathematics is not to be explained here as construction; the essay should allow conceivable structures, perhaps mathematical ones, that are generatively impossible, which is what the reply to the plenitude of suffering needed. The wording is Claude's; and the contrast between a record given all at once and an occurrence that has to be reached, used as the answer to the mathematical-object horn.
- "It is not the same from one base to another" went through two versions on 22 Nov and is now removed. It rendered the author's "Primitive causation is not invariant under a shift of base". With a base as a role, the claim reduces to the sentence that stays: "What can carry over from one rule to another is a pattern."
- The reply to the stronger frame reading (one Ruliad, one generating, rules as frames): the Ruliad taken whole is a totality given all at once, so it is a structure and on the side of description. The author agreed to the reply on 22 Nov; the wording is Claude's.
- The reply to "why not take everything connected as the one base?" (the author's question, 22 Nov; the argument and wording are Claude's): taken whole it is a totality and a description; taken as generated it needs a rule, and a rule that runs every rule is one base among the others, a pattern in each base that can run it. So mutual emulation has no bottom and singles none out. This is an argument from parity. It does not show that a base exists in itself as well as in the bases that run it; that rests on "all bases are real".
- The "concrete" paragraph, as settled 22 Nov. The author withdrew "not abstract in that it's not epistemically accessible" as false, and asked that postulate be separated from inference: that the whole is not concrete is inferred; that it is not abstract is postulated, as section three's claim made of the whole. Claude's wording throughout, including "To be abstract is to be a structure that a description gives". The paragraph on what the postulate buys is Claude's: an abstract whole would have no line between it and the rest of mathematics (existence by description), brute lowest counterfactuals, and generation as one more relation, so that bases would be frames. The author asked for it ("if it was abstract, that work would be harder") and has not read these three. Section three made the same step as if it were an argument, by refusing "a description with nothing described"; it is now marked there as a postulate ("what descriptions answer to is not one more description"), at the author's request, in Claude's wording. The quotation about coordinatizing is no longer in the text.
- The wider reading of the requirement on physics (22 Nov). The author's idea: *The Standard of Motion* needed unbounded degree at some coarse-graining, which favours the many-rule structure over a single finite rule. Claude's additions, unconfirmed: that applying every rule gives each event unboundedly many causal neighbours (reasoned, not found in Wolfram); that a single rule running the others still lies beneath, so the point favours "a pattern of many rules' applications" and not "one Ruliad instead of bases"; and the restated requirement. Not in the text: if the many-rule structure works as a causal set does, *The Standard of Motion* predicts swerving.
- A base is a role (22 Nov, at the author's request after asking whether the essay individuates bases). Section three now says a base "is picked out only through a level that answers to it". Sentences that counted or compared bases as individuals were removed or restated about rules: "all bases are real", "Production is primitive in each", "Bases are many, then", "keeps the bases many", "Every base is real", "which of the region's bases". Kept as shorthand: "the same pattern over each base in the region". Wording is Claude's. A description-free individuation was tried and dropped: a maximal family of occurrences connected by generation needs starting states with an identity of their own, and gives one family for the all-rules system.
- The link to *An Ontology That Pays for Itself* in "Whose interventions?" (22 Nov, the author's suggestion; wording Claude's): compression is kept as a method and as evidence, and given up as what makes a pattern real. That essay says "Code length here is the criterion for choosing structure", which a reader could take this essay to retract. Michał Wójcik's dialogue about the ontology essay is not answered here; the author is holding replies to his work until the queue is done, and only its last turn has been read (notes/private/MRW-ontology_compression_last_turn.md, the chatbot's summing-up). That turn reaches the same split as this passage: the compression machinery is "more valuable as an instrument for discovering and testing conceptual structures than as a route to one comprehensive representation of reality". Its test, that an abstraction be "useful beyond the corpus that justified its introduction", is weaker than this essay's, since a new corpus is still passively collected.
- "The system's own interactions" (22 Nov, after the author found it vague). Added in "Whose interventions?": a definition of "respects" (the effect depends on the finer state only through the variable) and of "own" (the level the typing fixes is closed in Rosas's sense), with the remark that a system can have several such levels. Still open: which of a system's closed levels matters for a given question, experience above all; the essay answers with "the vehicles' own regulation", and regulation is narrower than interaction. The reply to Franklin-Hall is where this costs most, since several families are available and the instrument only tests a candidate relative to one.
- Closure (22 Nov). The author asked whether closure is up to noise, and then objected that matching the finer detail's predictions is too strict: "reproduction within a functional envelope" is what is wanted. Claude agrees and had imported Rosas's closure because it was precise. It is stricter than the essay's own cases in two ways: it conditions on a part's own finer state, where the essay's idea is what parts respond to in one another; and it asks for matched prediction, where the cases show tolerance over a range. Now: a typing is the system's own when its parts respect it in one another across a range of finer conditions (the logic-gate example is Claude's), and the closure dimension is graded by the width of the range and by how little the finer detail adds, with Rosas's closure as the limit. Also corrected: "A heap has none of the three" was Claude's example and was wrong, since a gas or a heap is closed at the grain of its bulk on either reading. Closure alone does not separate a unity from a heap; integration and autonomy do. Not resolved: which range counts as the system's operating range leans on the notion of its operation, the vagueness noted above.
- Chances at a level (23 Nov). The author meant something looser still than a range of finer conditions: the special sciences use probabilistic models. Checked against Rubenstein et al., Definition 3: an exact transformation equates distributions, the pushforward of the fine interventional distribution with the coarse interventional distribution. So the criterion never asked the coarse model to predict as well as the fine one, and the essay's gloss in section five ("mapping its state") was deterministic where the definition is not. Added: a paragraph in section five, a sentence on statistical respect in "Whose interventions?", and "which may give only chances" in the closure dimension. Claude's, unconfirmed: the population-genetics example, and the last two sentences of the new paragraph, which extend section three's "sets of occurrences, weighted by whatever measure turns out to apply" from a base to every level. Possible literature, from memory and not in the dossier: Strevens on microconstancy (*Bigger than Chaos*), List and Pivato on emergent chance.
- Measure (23 Nov). Left out of scope and declared as a debt in section eleven; section ten points to *Presence and Actuality*, which calls the problem of reality measure "unresolved". The author's view, same day: "generation occurrences don't have measure themselves", and "the most tangible account that we have is the Born rule from Wolfram's quantum mechanics." (The author also said the measure "comes from the uncountability of bases, metaphorically speaking", and withdrew that as misleading: the thought behind it was that a measure might resolve the individuation problems for bases. That thought is not in the text.) This replaces the author's earlier "reduces into sets at the base level, or what the reality fluid measure turns out to be concerned with", so section three no longer says "weighted by whatever measure turns out to apply". Confirmed by the author (23 Nov): chances enter with types, as counterfactuals do; they are two cases of modality, which agrees with the essay's terminology. Claude's, unconfirmed: that a chance is a count of the ways something of a type is generated; a weight carried by an occurrence would be an intrinsic nature, which the essay rules out; and counting needs things counted as the same, which in Wolfram is the observer's merging of states. Caveats: the blog's exposition says path counts are "positive integers" and only "the magnitudes of discrete proto-amplitudes", with the complex structure appearing "only in the continuum limit", so the Born rule there is a programme and the squaring is not derived from counting alone; ratios of infinite counts depend on how limits are taken, which Wolfram concedes of the Ruliad; and counting each occurrence once is itself a postulate. Still silently needing a measure: induction, that is, why our region goes on being lawful.
- McKenzie's worry about unfinished physics (23 Nov). The author does not accept it as stated: "There is no missing special science that we need to secure our account. Yet part of the account is falsifiable if physics develops away from the Wolfram project rather than toward it." The reply is rewritten on those lines; the reason given (a final theory would be one more description and would not say what it answers to) is Claude's wording of section eight's point. Removed: "The claims about bases are the exception. They are the speculative part of the essay, and I have marked them", which conceded the worry and editorialized. Also corrected: the essay had "no naturalistic project can speak about fundamental reality before there is a fundamental theory" as a fourth charge. The dossier records only that she "cites a worry", so the text now quotes it (2024, p. 6; from a research pass, to verify). Claude's qualification, not in the text: the picture of section four is exposed and also unsecured, since it rests on the hostage and the debt; only the core claim waits for nothing.
- First item of "What would change my mind", rewritten 23 Nov after the author found it confusing. The old sentence named friction and swerving without saying what they are or why they bear on the requirement. The link, in Claude's words: if rules in general disturb free motion, inertia holds only over a special class of rules, and "none is singled out" fails in practice. Not in the text: the Wolfram group's hoped-for confirmations (a dimension of space that is not an integer or varies with place and time, a dimension-changing cosmology, local dimension change near compact objects, black holes that are not four-dimensional) are the same kind of effect, the substrate showing through, and would count for the picture if they are the same for every rule of the region. These are known to Claude only from search summaries of Wolfram's one-year update and the project's working notes on predictions; the primary texts were not read. With the wider reading of the requirement (many rules together), *The Standard of Motion*'s taxonomy points to swerving as the case to check.
- Detecting and intervening (22 Nov, the author's observation that the essay says "interventions" and uses something more like detectors). New paragraph in "Whose interventions?": the family registers differences, which gives the typing, and also acts, which is what puts the criterion beyond McAllister. Wording Claude's. The words "detect" in the admissibility clause and in the tolerance sentence are left as they are. Dasgupta on symmetry and detection, listed below as left out, is the nearest literature to the detecting half.
- Merging, mostly settled by Wolfram's own text (checked 22 Nov): "whenever we said that two paths in the ruliad 'merge', that's really just saying that we treat the outcomes as equivalent"; "'Viewed from the outside', one could imagine that absolutely nothing is equivalent. Two hypergraphs produced in two different ways … are 'from the outside' in some sense different"; the conflation "can be thought of as an 'act of the observer'". So merging is an identification made by a description, and it leaves individuation by origin alone. The essay does not yet say this; it answers the entanglement of bases only through emulation.
- That Newman's argument presupposes a collection whose members are what they are apart from the relations, so that the reply to van Fraassen serves for Newman too.

## Left out

From the outline: the Thomist resonance of section three's argument; Dasgupta on symmetry and detection; the withdrawn Lewis-style reading of counterfactuals; the note that causal abstraction is rarely exact in practice (Beckers, Eberhardt and Halpern on approximation).

## Verification at publication (23 Nov 2026)

A research pass checked the outstanding items against the sources. Numbers refer to the list under "To verify".

- 1, Life run inside Life: confirmed on LifeWiki (archived copy). The OTCA metapixel is a 2048 × 2048 unit cell of period 35,328 that can emulate any Life-like rule, Life included. Other unit cells were not checked.
- 2, total cholesterol: Rubenstein et al. give the example and credit it to their reference 18. The essay attributes it to no one.
- 3, Wolfram: checked on 22 Nov; see the item.
- 4, Lewis: "For short: we have an arrangement of qualities. And that is all." is on p. x of the Introduction to *Philosophical Papers*, vol. II. The wording was read from a scan; the punctuation comes from Loewer's quotation. More sentences follow it. The principle of recombination is paraphrased in the essay and was not checked.
- 5, the *Metascience* reply: the dossier's main pass read it; "partially explained by reference to fundamental physics" is quoted there. The essay paraphrases.
- 6, 10, 11 (Esfeld and Sachse, Kim, Chalmers, Godfrey-Smith): still without references in the text.
- 7, a Rosas example: still left out.
- 8, McAllister: the decomposition sentence is on p. 141 and begins "Mathematically, any empirical dataset"; the essay now quotes it from "any". "If any pattern is real, then all patterns must be" is on p. 138. The rearrangement of causal chains is on p. 149; he concludes that economy of description is not a reliable criterion, and the reading that the real pattern is the one that persists is the essay's. His example is a global dataset of sea-surface heights. The tide is the essay's illustration, not his, and the text now says whose the dataset is.
- 9, Tononi et al.: all three quotations confirmed in v5. "Exists as such" and "carried along for the ride" describe the "substrate+" view, which the authors reject; their term is "microphysical substrate". From v4 the paper is titled "Only what exists can cause: An intrinsic powers view of free will" and has a sixth author, Matteo Grasso.
- 12, *Every Thing Must Go* 5.1 to 5.4 and chapter 6: still unread.
- 14, van Fraassen: the three quotations are confirmed (pp. 292–293, 293, 294). The paraphrase "What is true of a mathematical structure would be true if nothing concrete existed" is fair and slightly general: his sentence is about statements that "do not imply the existence of anything but abstract entities", which "would still have been true" had God not created nature.
- McKenzie 2024: the abstract's sentence, "do not determine how many particles there are in nature" (p. 9) and the worry (p. 6) are confirmed. The worry is her own, raised in passing with a reference to her 2020 paper and then set aside.
- Koons: "no two substances overlap" is in "Thermal Substances" (*Synthese* 198, 2021), as one half of what Schaffer called the tiling constraint. It is not in "Staunch vs. Faint-hearted Hylomorphism", which the draft cited. Corrected.
- The DOIs for Hall and for Ladyman and Lorenzetti resolve to the right works.
- The sixteen quotations from earlier essays on this blog match their sources.
