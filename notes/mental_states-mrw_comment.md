Thanks, Michał — this is the right question to press on, and your reading isn't obstructed so much as productively mismatched with a distinction we'd left implicit and have now made explicit.

You're tracking real territory. Your semes — accident-tracking building blocks that select aspects of the world, stabilize them across variation, and make them available for combination — overlap substantially with what our article calls sub-denotational representational structure (drawing on Schwartz, Pion, Quilty-Dunn, Mandelbaum, and Caplan's recent work on underspecified representations, which shows these structures have empirical signatures like the Uncommon Sense Effect). Both of us are identifying cognitive kinds that do structural-inferential work without fixing denotation. The convergent arrival is genuine.

But semes aren't mental states on our account. They're closer to concepts — or, more precisely, to proto-concepts that become concepts once external tokens stabilize them (a progression your own dialogue develops beautifully). The distinction we'd been running together in earlier drafts, and that your comment prompted us to make explicit, is between standing structural types (concepts, proto-concepts, semes) and tokening events (thoughts, perceptions, intentions). On our account, mental states are the latter: particular structured representations active in a unified, accuracy-responsive cognitive economy at a time, with content and causal role. Concepts and semes are the standing structural kinds that figure *in* such tokenings as constituents; they aren't themselves mental episodes. Thoughts are mental states in the ordinary sense; the concepts deployed within a thought aren't.

With that distinction in place, the synthesis runs: semes ≈ sub-denotational structural constituents ≈ (once stabilized by external tokens) concepts, all of which are standing structural kinds that figure in mental states rather than being mental states. We've added a paragraph to the article making this explicit, partly in response to this exchange.

What your semes dialogue adds that ours doesn't is a dimension about *how* these standing structural kinds get formed, stabilized, and reshaped over a cognitive lifetime — via accident-tracking, vector-space composition, external-token regulation, and formal-systems-mediated reorganization. Our article brackets cognitive development and focuses on what it takes to be a mind at a time. Your dialogue is doing work at an adjacent level that ours isn't, and the "external tokens as regulative surface" and "formal systems reshape the internal space" points are genuinely supplementary to our framework rather than restatements of it. That's productive territory I'd be glad to continue thinking about together.
