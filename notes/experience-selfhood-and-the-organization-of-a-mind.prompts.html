Brown and the two metaphysical pressures

I would foreground Richard Brown as the interlocutor/adjudicator. Graziano doesn’t seem to help advance the argument. The tension we face is similar to the one that pushed Carruthers into a first-order theorist. But on this tension, we might be holding more firm than global broadcast theorists. It’s this: we want to deny that a cutout piece of meat or silicon is still an experience of red, for example. This anti-panpsychist intuition motivated my former Platonism. We can turn that question back on global broadcast theorists: what’s the experience exactly? Is it the node plus the whole network?

Nitpick: “So denying that the cutout remains conscious does not yet distinguish us from that account.” No, we’re the same as global broadcast in that regard, I put this confusingly. There are two tensions:

  1. Anti-pansychism
  2. Anti-platonism

The downstream universe

What do you mean, “merely described”? I’ll get back to it, a separate concern: We could say global broadcast either upgrades to regulation or puts the experience in the whole system. If you imagine that the experience broadcasts into a causally downstream universe, which is unobservable from the universe in which the broadcast originates. In which universe does experience exist for the global broadcast theorists?

Let me put it this way: I would want to attack externalist attribution. It makes the amount of experience in a universe unbounded and indeterminate.

To connect it to your Brown question: Is the broadcast organization that constitutes experience, or is experience what is broadcasted?

Yudkowsky and the self

That’s one pillar of the essay, but it needs more. The other pillar would be to involve Eliezer Yudkowsky and ask where the self sits in this.

How would Richard Brown classify Eliezer’s position? A classic HOT?

Do the higher-order theories, as accounted for by Brown, relate an experience or its vehicle to a self, and what kind of self?

It’s fair to say that EY does not need an experience of self, but I think he means an actual self rather than a formal self. Otherwise, he would have no empirical grounds to argue against consciousness in (most) animals.

Personhood and residual selfhood

Our richer metaphysics also avails us of a category that might be what EY is after, rather than his claim that it’s quallia, which is personhood. Originally, we had an aspiring person category, but I think we should drop it and make personhood independent of phenomenal consciousness, both requiring mindedness.

We can also ask: what’s the residual of selfhood when we drop, from personhood, the projection through time and the self-sustaining agency?

Yes. Maybe an angle would be to ask how far our conditions for mindedness go into establishing a self.

It also stress-tests whether our definition of mindedness is a natural category. Does it already include selfhood? Seems no. Is selfhood a further achievement of metaphysical significance? If yes, then the question is: could we discard mindedness and focus on selfhood instead. Another outcome is that selfhood is not a natural category or a significant attainment; rather, it’s a midpoint on the way to personhood.
