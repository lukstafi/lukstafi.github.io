Study notes: What a Description Answers To

Recall questions for What a Description Answers To. Answer each from memory, then open the answer to compare. The link under a question leads to the section it draws on.

Before reading

The essay and these questions assume:

  • The Grain of the Hard Problem, “What a coarse-graining is”: the intervention criterion: a coarse variable earns reality when interventions and interactions respect it.
  • The Standard of Motion, “What is free”: passive and active invariance, and the obstacle to exact Lorentz symmetry on a discrete substrate.

Helpful, not required:

  • Causation, Computation, Indexicality: A Complete Metaphysics, “3. Real Patterns and Constitutive Grounding”: the morphism criterion and the dust man.
  • Quantum Mechanics, Observers, and the Ruliad: An Exposition of the Wolfram Physics Project, “The Observer as the Source of Physical Law”: rules, bounded observers and the Ruliad.
  • Presence and Actuality, “What does Michał mean by actuality?”: Thomas’s three tiers and the borrowed notion of esse.

Questions

The answers state what the essay argues. It is written in the first person by Łukasz Stafiniak, with Claude. It quotes Michał Wójcik and his chatbot interlocutor Thomas Epistemes from earlier essays, and it marks two of its own steps as postulates. The last three questions ask what it leaves unproved or unanswered.

What the toy leaves out

state

Dennett held that the glider in Conway’s Life is a real pattern, and Ladyman and Ross built a metaphysics on that answer. The essay accepts the answer and says it leaves out three things. What are they?

Answer

First, the glider supports counterfactuals only as a glider. “Had the glider started one cell to the left, it would have missed the block” compares two histories, and the comparison needs the type glider to say what is held fixed and what is varied. Second, the glider does no producing. Each cell’s next state comes from its neighbourhood by the rule, and the glider’s next position comes from nothing but that. Third, Life can run Life: a grid of large cells, each built from many small ones, carries out Life’s rule, so whether something is the level that produces or a pattern above it depends on where one stands.

Two criteria, two questions

distinguish Two criteria ↗

Two earlier essays gave different tests for when a pattern is real. State each, say which question each answers, and say how section five joins them.

Answer

The morphism criterion came from Putnam’s problem about computation. A pattern is realised in a system when the realization relation is a structure-preserving map, and the dust man fails because his mapping is “an arbitrary lookup table”. It asks which computations a system realises: a model is given and a map is sought. The intervention criterion is Thomas’s: “A coarse variable earns reality when interventions and interactions repeatedly respect it.” It asks which coarse variables are real: a family of interactions is given, and it sorts the fine states into classes. Section five identifies them with the two maps of a causal abstraction. The morphism is the map on states, and the family of interactions is the domain of the map on interventions.

Five answers

state Which structural realism ↗

The variants of structural realism differ on five questions. Give the essay’s answer to each, and say what it keeps and what it drops from Ladyman and Ross on how scales are related.

Answer

There are objects, and relations individuate them, which is the moderate position. The view applies at every scale. Modality is real at every level of patterns and brute at none. The essay makes no claim about levels below the lowest one we could have access to; what that level answers to is a base, and a base is not a level. On the relation of scales it keeps that patterns at every grain are equally real and do not form a ladder. It drops Ladyman and Ross’s denial that special-science patterns must be composed of, or locally supervene on, physical ones. The essay holds that every real pattern is fixed by any admissible finer description of the same system.

Why the construction cannot end in modal structure

argue Counterfactuals come with descriptions ↗

The stone would have broken the window, and that fact is constructed from the level below. Why can the construction not stop at more modal structure? Which step of the argument is a postulate?

Answer

A counterfactual holds something fixed and varies something else, and both need types. So modal structure is structure under a typing, which is structure as described. If what lies beneath every description were itself modal, it would be a description of something further, or a description with nothing described. The first only moves the question down. The second is ruled out by postulate: what descriptions answer to is not one more description. Making modality primitive puts the description where the thing described should be. Ladyman and Ross refuse to say what makes the structure physical and not mathematical; the essay answers that the lowest description answers to production.

Production against the Humean

distinguish Counterfactuals come with descriptions ↗

The Humean agrees that what the lowest description answers to is not modal. Where does the essay part with the Humean, and why is its claim not a counterfactual in disguise?

Answer

For the Humean there are occurrences in an arrangement, each a distinct existence, and laws are the best summary. The essay adds that the occurrences come from one another. An application of a rule takes these inputs and yields these outputs, and the outputs are individuated as its products. This cannot mean that the product would not have existed without the producer, since that is a counterfactual and belongs to a description. It means that what the occurrence is depends on what it came from. The essay does not assert necessary connections between distinct existences. It denies that occurrences at a base are distinct existences.

What a base lacks

explain Counterfactuals come with descriptions ↗

A base is “the generation that the lowest accessible level of description answers to”. What three things does it lack that every level has, and why does the essay conclude that “base” names a role?

Answer

It has no counterfactuals and no types of pattern, and its occurrences carry no weights of their own. A chance is a count of the ways something of a type comes to be generated, so chances enter with types, as counterfactuals do. Having no types, a base cannot be picked out directly: to tell one base from another is to type them. A base is therefore picked out only through a level that answers to it. None of this makes patterns depend on minds, because a description here is a level fixed by a family of interactions, and a system whose parts respond only to certain differences in one another carries its own typing.

Van Fraassen and Newman

object Counterfactuals come with descriptions ↗

Van Fraassen argues that “if there is no non-structure, there is no structure either”. State his dilemma and the essay’s reply, and say why the same reply serves against Newman.

Answer

The contrast he asks for is between a structure and something’s having it. Either nothing marks the world off from its mathematics, and the world is a mathematical object, or something does, and that something is not structure; the second option, he says, leads back to a bearer that has existence and no other properties. The essay accepts what the second option demands and denies what it is said to cost. What is not structure is production, and production is not a bearer. A bearer is what it is apart from the structure laid over it. An occurrence individuated by what it came from is nothing apart from the generation. So the world’s structure is always the structure of generation, and the world is not the record of it. Newman’s objection needs relations that are arbitrary sets of pairs, laid over a collection whose members are what they are apart from them. At a base the generating relations are not laid over the occurrences.

Frames and the one real base

object Bases, and where we are ↗

Two objections say there is really one thing generating. One reads each rule as a frame on the one Ruliad. The other takes everything connected by emulation as the one real base. Give the reply to each.

Answer

To the first: the one thing the frames are frames on would be the Ruliad taken whole, and taken all at once, as one object, it would be a structure, which section three put on the side of description. That reading has a description beneath the frames and nothing that generates. What generates is each application of a rule. To the second: taken all at once the connected whole is again a description and not a base. Taken as generated it needs a rule, and there is one, since a program can run every program a step at a time. But that program is one rule among the others. The rules it runs are patterns in it, and it is a pattern in whatever rule runs it. Containing the rest singles none out, and none is at the bottom.

Neither concrete nor abstract

distinguish Bases, and where we are ↗

The essay says the whole of what is generated is neither concrete nor abstract, and that one half of this is inferred and the other postulated. Which is which? What would be lost if the whole were abstract?

Answer

To be concrete is to be located in a lower level, and to be abstract is to be a structure that a description gives. That the whole is not concrete is inferred: nothing lies beneath it, since any base one might put there is one of its own and no base is God’s pick. That it is not abstract is postulated, as section three’s postulate made of the whole: it is what descriptions answer to and is not one of them. Descriptions of it exist, such as Wolfram’s definition, and they are not the whole. If the whole were a structure and nothing more, no line would separate it from the rest of mathematics, so that to describe a thing would be to show that it exists. The lowest counterfactuals would be brute modal facts. And generation would be one more relation within the structure, so nothing would produce. The essay says this does not refute an abstract whole. It shows what the postulate buys.

The demand on physics

explain Bases, and where we are ↗

Why must physics be stable under a shift of base, and why is this harder than the fact that rules can emulate one another? What wider reading does the obstacle from The Standard of Motion suggest?

Answer

An observer spans a region and cannot tell which rule underlies it, so physics has to be the same pattern over each base in the region. That rules can emulate one another is passive invariance, the independence of a description from a frame. That physics exists, as the same pattern, over each base is active invariance, the existence of the transformed thing, and Life shows it can fail: the ships nearest a glider in speed are different patterns. The first is known, and the second is a theorem nobody has. The wider reading starts from the obstacle that exact Lorentz symmetry seems to need events with unboundedly many causal neighbours, while a finite rule gives each event finitely many. If so, physics is a pattern in the applications of many rules taken together, and the requirement is that it be the same over every base that runs the rules of the region.

The criterion at work

case The criterion ↗

State the criterion. Then say why temperature passes and total cholesterol fails, and why a real pattern may give only chances.

Answer

A pattern is real, relative to a family of interventions, when a model of it is a causal abstraction of the system under an admissible map. Setting the temperature of a gas has one effect on its pressure, whatever the molecules are doing. Setting total cholesterol has no single effect on heart disease, because the effect depends on how the total divides between two kinds that act in opposite directions. In Rubenstein’s definition the two sides of the diagram are distributions: the mapped outcomes of a fine intervention must be distributed as the coarse model says under the corresponding coarse intervention. So the coarse model has to get the chances right, and it need not predict as well as the fine model would, which is what Rosas’s closure asks. Population genetics leaves out the finer causes of each death, and its chances hold across many ways those causes might be arranged.

Three trivialities, one cure

connect Three trivialities, one cure ↗

Which three literatures found that structure-preserving maps are cheap? State the essay’s admissibility clause, and say why using a modal notion to say which modal structures are real is not a circle.

Answer

Newman found it for structure, Putnam for computation, and Sutter and colleagues for causal abstraction: with unrestricted maps any algorithm is an abstraction of any neural network meeting mild conditions. Each literature restricts which maps count, by independence of parts, physical similarity, simplicity, information, linearity or generalization. The essay proposes one clause these approximate: a map is admissible when it is stable under any shift of the lower level that the family of interventions cannot detect. The dust man’s table is rewritten at every nudge, and a map fitted to one network’s weights says nothing about the next. Simplicity is taken as evidence of stability, and stability as the criterion. The clause is a recursion: the shifts are shifts of the lower level, whose modal facts are constructed from the level below it, and the descent ends in the repetition of a rule’s applications.

Compression, detecting and acting

object Whose interventions? ↗

McAllister argues that “if any pattern is real, then all patterns must be”. What does the essay give up, what does it keep, and why does his argument not reach the criterion? Why would a family that only detects not be enough?

Answer

The essay gives up compression as the criterion and keeps it as a method: a shorter description is evidence that a pattern is real and a way of finding candidates, as in An Ontology That Pays for Itself, and it is not what makes the pattern real. McAllister’s argument concerns passively collected data, and the criterion is stated in terms of interventions. The family does two things. Its members register differences, which gives the typing, since fine states that no member can tell apart fall into one class. They also act. A detector that only waits for data is within McAllister’s reach. An intervention puts the system into fine states that would not have turned up unprompted, and a class has to hold together across all of them. At a base the essay accepts his conclusion: whatever is generated is among what there is, and selectivity enters with a family.

Which direction is the feature

case Whose interventions? ↗

A network’s function fixes its hidden representation only up to an invertible linear map. Name the four things that can pick out a basis, and say what the optimizer’s case shows. Which symmetry is left in every case, and what does it illustrate?

Answer

An elementwise nonlinearity can: the linear maps that commute with a ReLU layer are permutations of the axes with positive rescalings. An energy cost on non-negative activations can. Both belong to the network’s own operation, so the axes they pick out are real for the computation. The optimizer can, since Adam normalizes each coordinate separately, but transformers do not rely on a privileged basis to function, so that basis is real for the training process and idle in the computation. Being selected by a system’s dynamics is not enough: the interventions have to be on the process in question. An analyst’s sparsity penalty counts when the network’s own operation respects the directions it finds. In every case neurons can be swapped together with their weights. So the set of axes is real and no axis has an identity beyond its place among the others, which is the moderate position: relata exist, and relations individuate them.

A system’s own typing

explain Whose interventions? ↗

When does an interaction respect a variable, and when is a typing the system’s own? Why does the essay say this asks less than Rosas’s closure?

Answer

An interaction respects a variable when its effect depends on the finer state only through that variable. Respect can be statistical: the effects may vary with the finer state while their chances depend only on the variable. A typing is the system’s own when its parts respect it in one another across a range of finer conditions. A logic gate treats every voltage above a threshold as one value, and within its operating range the rules of the logic hold whatever the voltages are. Rosas’s closure asks that the finer state add nothing to prediction. Here a part’s own finer state may still predict what that part does next, and outside the range the rules may fail. Rosas’s closure is the limit, where the range is everything.

Two senses of cause

distinguish Causes above the base ↗

Hall separated producing an effect from making a difference to it. Where does the essay place each, and how does that answer Kim’s exclusion argument and the choice Tononi and colleagues offer?

Answer

Production belongs to bases. Difference-making belongs to patterns, and Woodward’s interventionism is its theory. Particle physics describes patterns, so its causes are difference-makers too. Kim’s argument is about production, and patterns do not produce, so they compete neither with one another nor with a base. Among difference-makers there is no competition: the stone’s momentum and the motions of its molecules both make a difference to the window. Tononi and colleagues offer intrinsic causal powers at one privileged grain, or a micro level that “exists as such” with everything higher “carried along for the ride”. The essay takes neither: higher levels are carried along as far as production goes, and so is the micro level. Existence is earned by the criterion of section five, and not by producing something the parts do not.

Three ways to be one

case What makes a thing one ↗

Name the three dimensions on which a whole can be one. Place an organism, a trained network on a processor, a nation and a heap. Where does esse find a place?

Answer

Integration: the parts are not closed off from one another. Autonomy: the whole is closed off from its surroundings by its own activity. Closure: the level’s own typing suffices for its dynamics, in that its rules, which may give only chances, hold across a range of finer detail. An organism is high on all three. A trained network is integrated and closed at the grain of its computation and has little autonomy, since it maintains nothing. A nation has thin integration beyond that of its households and institutions. A heap is closed at the grain of its bulk and has neither of the others. A perfectly closed level is a base when taken in itself, as the large cells of Life running Life are. So a pattern has production of its own in the limit of closure, and by degrees short of it. The essay expects a Thomist to answer that a degree of closure still describes what a thing is, and not that it is.

Realism about modality is not modal realism

distinguish The neighbours ↗

The essay removes Lewis’s principle of free recombination and keeps three things apart about the stone that missed the window. What removes the principle, and what are the three things?

Answer

Recombination needs items that are what they are apart from their relations, so that a duplicate can be lifted out and set down elsewhere. An occurrence individuated by what it comes from cannot be lifted out, and since existence comes by generation, describing a combination does not make one. The three things: what would have happened, which is fixed by the modal structure of stone and glass; what is possible, which holds if a support for the situation could be rebuilt, level by level, down to a base; and what occurs somewhere, since the rebuilt situation may be generated in some region. The counterfactual is true and the situation is not actual, and whether it occurs elsewhere bears on neither. How much it would count for is a question of measure that the essay does not answer.

Thesis or stance

object A thesis, with a hostage ↗

McKenzie concludes that structural realism is a stance and not a thesis, and cites a worry about any project claiming insight into fundamental reality before there is a fundamental theory. What does the essay say its view rules out, and how does it answer the worry?

Answer

It rules out six things: categorical natures beneath structure; modal structure with nothing non-modal for it to answer to, of which existence by description is one form; a singled-out base, including a privileged level of particles; productive power at the level of patterns; exclusion by maximality; and brute necessities. The worry supposes that what is fundamental has to be read off a fundamental theory. The essay’s central claim is about what any description answers to, and a final theory would be one more description, the lowest. It would not say what it answers to, because no description does. So no science is still missing that the account waits for. Part of the account is exposed to physics all the same: if physics develops away from Wolfram’s programme, the picture of section four is refuted, and a view that physics can refute is not a stance.

Hostage and debt

limits A thesis, with a hostage ↗

What does the essay leave unproved, and what does it leave unaccounted for?

Answer

The hostage is the requirement of section four, that physics be the same pattern over each base of a region. It is a theorem nobody has proved. The debt is a measure, needed to say what the lowest chances come to and how much what occurs elsewhere counts for. The essay says only what kind of thing it would be: a count of ways of being generated, and not a weight that occurrences carry. The nearest account is the Born rule in Wolfram’s quantum mechanics, where a state’s weight is the number of histories that lead to it, and the step from those counts to quantum amplitudes is taken only in a limit and under assumptions.

Where the account is weakest

limits What makes a thing one ↗

Where does the essay say its account of unity is weakest, and what does it concede about the admissibility clause in section six?

Answer

It is weakest at closure. No open system is exactly closed, and the author does not know whether the three dimensions reduce to one measure. Of admissibility the essay says the clause could fail in three ways: stability comes in degrees, so admissibility does too; a map could be stable for a reason that has nothing to do with the pattern; and linearity, which interpretability practice relies on, is an empirical bet about networks that does not follow from the clause.

What would change the author’s mind

limits What would change my mind ↗

The essay lists six findings that would count against it. Give the first, which concerns physics, and three of the others.

Answer

The first is a proof that the rules which yield our physics are special. A discrete substrate with a rest frame should slow a free body, and one without should make it swerve. If rules in general do one or the other, at sizes that experiment excludes, then inertia holds only over rules chosen to protect it, and the requirement of section four fails. The others: a variable that interventions respect and no admissible map carries to a model, or the reverse, which would separate the two halves of the criterion; a map stable under every undetectable shift that identifies a bogus pattern; a basis that a system’s own operation respects and that does no work under any intervention on it; a counterfactual that needs no typing; and a system high on integration, autonomy and closure that nobody would call one thing, or one low on a dimension that is plainly a unity.

Terms

Introduced in this essay:

  • morphism criterion and intervention criterion: the blog’s two earlier tests for a real pattern: a structure-preserving map from a model to the system, and a coarse variable that interventions respect.
  • moderate structural realism: there are objects, and relations individuate them.
  • modality constructed from below: the modal facts at each level are fixed by the level below and stated under the upper level’s types, so modality is real at every level and brute at none.
  • production: one occurrence coming from another by an application of a rule; what the lowest description answers to.
  • description (as a level): a level fixed by a family of interactions, whether or not anyone describes it.
  • base: the generation that the lowest accessible level of description answers to; a role, picked out only through a level, with no counterfactuals, types or chances.
  • postulate of the described: what descriptions answer to is not one more description.
  • concrete and abstract: concrete: located in a lower level; abstract: a structure that a description gives; the whole of what is generated is neither.
  • requirement on physics: physics must be the same pattern over each base of the region an observer spans; unproved.
  • causal abstraction: a coarse model related to a fine one by a map on states and a map on interventions that commute.
  • family of interventions: the interactions relative to which a pattern is real; its members register differences and also act.
  • admissible map: a map that is stable under any shift of the lower level that the family of interventions cannot detect.
  • respect: an interaction respects a variable when its effect, or the chances of its effects, depend on the finer state only through that variable.
  • a system’s own typing: a typing that the system’s parts respect in one another across a range of finer conditions.
  • production and difference-making: Hall’s two senses of cause: production belongs to bases, difference-making to patterns.
  • integration, autonomy and closure: three graded ways a whole can be one: its parts are not closed off from one another, its own activity closes it off from its surroundings, and its level’s own typing suffices for its dynamics.
  • hostage and debt: the unproved requirement on physics, and the missing account of measure.
